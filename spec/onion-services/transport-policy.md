# Slot lifetime, abuse bounds, and failures

Limit pending slots per client circuit, service registration, connection, and across the relay. Enforce aggregate request/response bandwidth, setup, CPU, queue, and memory ceilings because new circuits or identities can bypass a per-client limit. The service independently caps decrypt/verify work, candidate selection, outstanding quotes/invoices, nonce/replay records, and queues. Use the smaller of applicable path, relay, and service budgets. A legitimate quote can still fail under overload; these bounds do not promise absence of DoS.

Each slot permits one bounded logical reply, using the fixed envelope/cell counts defined below and a strictly limited number of fragments. Fix bytes, cells, reassembly state, and deadline before implementation. Do not assume a complete quote/invoice fits one cell, enlarge the initial onion payload, allocate from an unchecked length, or permit unbounded fragmentation. Include routing/encryption/signature overhead, padding, and both directions in the free accounting. A large authenticated quote still has to fit the advertised budget.

The slot's expiry is absolute from admission and MUST NOT be reset by retries, fragments, acknowledgments, or client activity. Release it on reply completion, timeout, circuit loss, or service registration replacement. Reject late fragments, wrong-service replies, reused handles, and mappings to recycled circuit IDs. Authenticate/reassemble a complete response before acting on it. Bounds must prevent response flooding or using slots as arbitrary third-party forwarding destinations.

Keep transport-slot state distinct from the service's authenticated quote/attempt ledger. An expired slot or rotated introduction circuit does not by itself refund a payment, invalidate an otherwise accepted quote, or authorize a new circuit. Reopening transport requires a fresh slot and authenticated bounded status/retry; existing quote expiry, single redemption, reservations, and uncertain-payment reconciliation still govern. A retransmitted logical request may obtain the cached reply while the quote remains valid, with fresh admitted routing state, but cannot reset its price/expiry or trigger duplicate paid work. An untrusted relay forwarding acknowledgment never permits payment.

For a retry or status request delivered through a fresh route/slot, preserve logical quote idempotency while using a fresh authenticated transport nonce, reply key, and encryption randomness. Cached quote content/authorization is not permission to replay an identical encrypted request or response across different introduction points. Keep stable quote/request references inside the encrypted payload. The final retry envelope must authenticate the new transport binding under the existing client authorization and service identity, without resetting quote validity, funds, or paid-work accounting. Reusing raw cipher state or an AEAD nonce for different plaintext is forbidden. The exact cryptographic construction and retry/status encoding require review before implementation.

The client may retain the eligible introduction circuit within its existing lifetime/resource budget for follow-up or the later paid introduction, or build a new eligible one when needed. Do not require the paid authorization to depend on a reply slot remaining alive while a human approves the quote or Lightning settles. Wallet work remains asynchronous and bounded, outside synchronous Tor cell processing. Exhausting free capacity cannot automatically purchase more capacity from the service's wallet.

## Fixed-size quote envelopes

For service version 1, define one common padded request-envelope size and one common padded reply-envelope size across services. The two sizes need not equal each other. They are version/profile constants, not operator-selected sizes or buckets chosen from actual quote length. All valid quote requests follow the request profile; an admitted quote response and an authenticated service refusal follow the same reply profile.

Serialize the true content length and content inside the end-to-end encrypted envelope, pad to the profile's fixed plaintext length before encryption, and authenticate the padding/length under the final reviewed construction. Use fresh encryption on retries as required above. Only bounded fixed routing/framing fields may remain outside that envelope. Ordinary relay-cell padding alone is insufficient: the introduction endpoint can read relay command length/framing after terminating its Tor layer, so the forwarded ciphertext length must also be constant.

Define a fixed cell count and fixed per-fragment payload lengths for each envelope profile, including the last fragment. A short quote/refusal must not result in fewer cells or a shorter last fragment. Account for encryption tags, signatures, nonce/key material, routing handles, fragment headers, and normal relay padding. Normal congestion/flow-control/padding behavior still applies; fixed logical message cell counts do not promise an identical total network trace.

Before assigning numeric sizes, inventory every required field's maximum encoded length under the selected canonical encoding, calculate cryptographic and routing overhead, then choose fixed request/reply capacities and fragment counts fitting the supported cell format. Keep SCQs compact and bounded. Arbitrarily long descriptions, extra terms, or full wallet invoices must not silently expand a quote; invoice acquisition remains its separately specified bounded method/exchange. The initial byte/cell values are now derived in the initial wire section; they do not set measured free-capacity or latency parameters.

Check size before sending. If a request cannot fit, fail locally without sending an oversized request. If a service cannot produce a conforming quote, it may send an authenticated fixed-size refusal within its admitted slot/budget, rather than adding fragments or truncating terms. Receivers reject oversized, truncated, extra, or inconsistent framing with bounded allocation. Treat all advertised limits as ceilings; they do not authorize a larger service-specific envelope.

Relay admission failures, malformed traffic, slot exhaustion, circuit loss, and timeouts are separate transport outcomes. They cannot be disguised as authenticated service quotes, and this rule does not require unlimited cover replies or extra free capacity under attack. Where a valid admitted service reply is produced, success/refusal content remains inside the same padded envelope. Unavailable or nonconforming transport fails within existing budgets.

Validate the profile using minimum/maximum legal quotes, unequal relay rates, supported payment methods, authentication material, service refusals, and permitted retries. Compare exposed envelope lengths, fragment lengths/counts, and last-fragment sizes at introduction endpoints; ensure true lengths and stable references remain encrypted. Test oversized inputs and free-budget/reassembly exhaustion. Constant nominal size reduces content-length signals but does not hide message timing, missing replies, protocol activity, or relationships already visible to the introduction relay. Apply the shared response-timing policy below and review its interaction with Tor circuit-padding machines. Track this work in ET-23 and ET-21, with no implementation claimed.

**Privacy/timing assessment:** this change targets observable ciphertext lengths and nominal fragment/cell counts at introduction endpoints and along the path. It preserves independent identifiers, fresh retry encryption, encrypted true lengths, and the existing introduction relay's known circuit relationships. Transport failure, congestion, missing replies, and timing can still differ. Numeric capacities, cryptographic framing, scheduling implementation/measurements, padding-machine interaction, and empirical correlation tests remain deferred; only documentation consistency/link checks have been performed for this change.

## Shared response timing

For valid admitted quote requests, fresh quotes, cached quote results, and authenticated service refusals MUST use the same bounded response-scheduling policy for service version 1. A cache hit must not bypass the scheduler and produce an immediate response merely because the result is already available. The policy is shared by the version/profile, not selected to reveal a service, amount, wallet method, request type, or cache outcome. It does not require waiting for a Lightning payment or exposing wallet RPC latency in the quote reply.

The intended design chooses a randomized target release time within a shared bounded response window, anchored to the locally defined admission of a complete valid request and independent of which result is obtained. Schedule computation, authentication, and fresh encryption within that budget. Simply adding a random delay after computation does not make fresh/cached response-time distributions equal; measured computation, queueing, and transport effects must inform the final window. No numeric window or delay distribution is selected here. Avoid globally synchronized release ticks; the final definition must specify its local start event, randomness, and safe time arithmetic.

The target and all fragments must fit the remaining reply-slot, quote-validity, setup, queue, and free-resource limits, with bounded allowance for transmission. Do not extend slot/quote expiry, reset funding deadlines, create extra paid work, or purchase capacity to accommodate a delay. If a result cannot be produced or sent safely in time, use a conforming authenticated refusal only where the admitted budget permits; otherwise let the bounded transport attempt fail. Do not send a quote already expired at release, weaken authentication, or invent paid authorization to fill a response window.

Timers, delayed replies, encryption work, and outstanding request state count against aggregate memory/CPU/queue/concurrency budgets. Malformed/unadmitted requests, circuit loss, and overload remain the separate bounded transport outcomes described above; no unlimited padding or delayed-response state is required under attack. Preserve the fixed-size quote/refusal envelope and fresh per-delivery encryption. Fragment pacing and integration with existing Tor congestion/padding behavior require separate measurement; one local target release time does not control the entire path's timing.

**Privacy/timing assessment:** the policy targets the extra cache/new/refusal timing signal visible to an introduction relay or observer comparing attempts. The introduction endpoint still knows its own routed request/reply association; network delay, congestion, processing overruns, missing replies, and active relay delaying/dropping can remain observable. Test distributions for fresh/cached/refused requests under load, timer/queue limits, fragment completion before expiry, and interaction with Tor padding and deadline enforcement. Random delay is not proof of indistinguishability or protection against an observer watching both ends. Only documentation checks have run; scheduling implementation, parameter measurements, and traffic-correlation tests remain deferred under ET-23/21. Client retry/failover timing is defined separately below and must remain compatible with this service response rule.

## Bounded client retry and failover timing

Before sending a quote request, set a monotonic local delivery deadline that accommodates the advertised common response window, bounded request/response transmission including all fragments, and a measured/configured allowance for network delay. Keep an overall setup deadline as well. A forwarding acknowledgment is not a quote or evidence the service has finished processing. Receiving partial fragments or an untrusted progress/error message MUST NOT reset these absolute deadlines. If there is insufficient setup/slot budget for a normal complete attempt, stop rather than intentionally timing it out early.

On a retryable timeout or transport failure, retire the current local delivery attempt and wait a bounded randomized backoff before another delivery. Maintain at most one active local delivery attempt per logical quote inquiry; do not deliberately send parallel inquiries to every introduction point. An old packet/service computation may still be in flight after local timeout, so serialization is not a substitute for service-side replay/idempotency checks. Count attempts across all introduction points, replacement circuits, descriptor refreshes, and automatic status retries against the same finite retry/setup budget; changing the route or transport nonce does not restart the counter. Choose numeric timeout, retry-count, and backoff parameters only after measurements.

Select backoff locally under the common profile, within the remaining setup deadline and resource limits. Do not retry immediately at an attacker-chosen time because a relay sent an unauthenticated error, redirect, proposed delay, or premature forwarding failure. Even an authenticated service hint cannot override local retry/deadline/budget policy or prescribe a uniquely identifying retry schedule. A valid terminal quote refusal, unsupported version/method, expired authorization, or budget exhaustion is not permission for an automatic retry loop. Existing authenticated stale-capability refresh remains bounded and consumes the same setup budget.

Retry only through eligible introduction points obtained from the authenticated service descriptor. Preserve normal Tor guard selection and stream/client isolation; a quote timeout or change of introduction point MUST NOT itself trigger guard rotation, a persistent new client identity, or merging isolated activity. Do not silently change service, network, protocol, payment method, proposed rendezvous destination, accepted terms, or spending authorization. A genuine change to quote-bound terms requires the appropriate fresh agreement, without reusing committed funds or evading retry limits.

Keep the same encrypted logical inquiry/quote reference and client authorization binding for idempotency, but give each delivery a fresh transport nonce, reply-encryption key, admitted relay-local handle, and encryption randomness under the authenticated retry envelope above. Never expose a shared logical inquiry ID in routing fields or reuse ciphertext across introduction points. A fresh route must return existing valid quote state or a bounded authenticated result, not reset quote price/expiry, duplicate reservations, create another paid build, or buy a new round.

Accept a response for further action only if its transport binding matches the current active delivery and its authenticated quote/version/client/nonce/terms remain valid. A response from an abandoned or timed-out slot MUST NOT reactivate that attempt, automatically accept/pay a quote, extend the setup deadline, or create competing quote acceptances. A later authenticated status query can recover existing quote/payment state under the same bounded rules. A transport timeout is not proof a Lightning payment failed, a refund, or release of uncertain funds; any existing payment commitments retain their separate durable reconciliation rules.

**Privacy/timing assessment:** sequential attempts, local bounded backoff, fresh encrypted retry envelopes, and unchanged guard/isolation rules reduce additional deterministic failover and ciphertext-matching signals. The introduction relay still sees its own request/response routing, and malicious delaying/dropping, congestion, and an observer comparing both ends can still correlate activity. Test loss and delayed/partial replies, expired slots, late valid quotes after failover, unauthenticated forced-retry/redirect hints, deliberately patterned relay delays, retry-counter bypass, cache idempotency, guard stability, isolation, fixed envelope sizes, and all setup/free/queue/spending budgets. Numeric values, runtime support, and measured privacy effectiveness remain deferred under ET-23/21; documentation checks alone establish none of those properties.

## Privacy, remaining wire work, and validation

Preserve the same per-hop payment fields and padding as other El Tor circuits, including dummy zero-fee fields where applicable. The introduction endpoint necessarily sees its registered service circuit and the client circuit it forwards for; the reply feature does not make those relationships invisible. Keep prices, invoices, client keys, requested rendezvous details, and authorization inside the end-to-end encrypted payload. Do not expose a persistent wallet identity or shared cross-hop payment/renewal token for routing. Fresh reply keys and request nonces prevent adding a reusable client handle; timing/size correlation still needs measurement.

Before implementation, specify descriptor capability/delegation encoding, request/reply encryption and authentication, canonical byte layouts, opcode/direction rules, handle generation/binding, fixed limits, fragmentation/reassembly, timeout/error behavior, replay/idempotency, and bounded follow-up invoice/status messages. This section records the approved transport architecture, not a completed wire protocol or crypto review. Numeric budgets must be measured with real quote/invoice sizes and the actual cell format.

Validate encrypted quote round trips with no new service circuit or wallet spending; wrong-key/version/nonce/capability/RP/quote substitution; unsupported endpoints; duplicate/replayed requests; fabricated/wrong-service return handles; stale/recycled circuits; delayed/lost/oversized/fragmented replies; queue/slot floods; and free-budget exhaustion. Verify human approval and late payment remain independent of slot lifetime, and settlement still precedes paid service construction. Implementation is deferred under ET-13/14 and the dedicated reply-transport task ET-23 in the [El Tor TODO](../../TODO.md).
