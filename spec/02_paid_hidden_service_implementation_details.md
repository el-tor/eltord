## 1. Common representation

Offsets are zero-based, ranges half-open. A numeric field of 1/2/4/8 bytes is an unsigned u8/u16/u32/u64 respectively, multibyte values in network byte order. Identity/key/digest/nonce/reference fields are raw bytes, not integers or hex text. Service identity is the 32-byte v3 onion identity public key. Rendezvous identity is the 32-byte Ed25519 relay identity resolved through verified directory information; the RP's other required link/handshake information is obtained from that verified record, not an arbitrary untrusted address supplied by this request.

`service_version=1`; inner `record_kind=1` is initial quote request and `2` is quote reply. `payment_method=1` is BOLT11_INVOICE and `2` is BOLT12_OFFER. Reply `status=1` is an offered quote and `2` is a service refusal. No other values or implicit defaults are accepted in these initial records. These draft assignments are El Tor-local and are not upstream Tor or Lightning registrations. The [acceptance and round-invoice flow](#quote-acceptance-and-round-specific-invoice-acquisition) is now recorded in spec two. Later acceptance, invoice/status, and continuation messages still need separate schemas, not reinterpretation of these two records.

All costs/caps are integer millisatoshis. Per-round service relay cost and service-side outgoing routing allowance are separate; setup and application charges are initial one-time maxima, not recurring fees. The payer-routing cap bounds the client's fee for each payment to the service. Total and outstanding caps cover this service-side purchase, including its applicable payer fees; the client separately budgets payments to its own relays. Checked arithmetic must prevent overflow or rounding-driven excess. Zero cost is permitted when explicitly quoted, but never turns a refusal into a payable quote. Age/round/spending ceilings must be explicit and finite; zero does not mean unlimited. A quote's costs, ceilings, and features must fit the request and local policy.

Timestamps are unsigned Unix seconds; durations are seconds. Preserve the lifecycle requirements for freshness and bounded clock tolerance; a sender timestamp alone cannot prove timely admission. The absolute inquiry deadline is stable across retries, and local monotonic delivery/slot deadlines remain separate. `capability_digest` references the authenticated canonical capability/terms snapshot selected for this inquiry; its canonical descriptor schema still needs definition. New request-key generations may come from a newer authenticated descriptor without silently replacing the inquiry's original price/capability snapshot. If current authority no longer supports that snapshot, refuse/expire the inquiry rather than reset it or automatically buy again.

| 252 | 32 | `delivery_nonce` |
| 284 | 32 | `reply_encryption_key` |

Bytes `[0:244]` are the stable logical request projection. Preserve them, including client authorization and deadline, on all delivery retries. Bytes `[244:316]` select the authorized request-key generation and fresh delivery binding. Changing a logical field requires a new explicit inquiry, not resetting the retry/spending budget or abandoning uncertain payments. The full body is signed by the `client_auth_key`; the 64-byte Ed25519 signature follows at offset 316, producing a 380-byte signed record. Public client key validity and signature validation do not prove uniqueness, settlement, or entitlement to paid work.

`required_features` bit 0 requires in-place paid circuit renewal; other bits are reserved and must be zero in this draft. A successful quote must include every required feature; it may not silently downgrade renewal to forced disconnection. `max_rounds` and `max_circuit_age_seconds` bound approved continuation across allocations, not a requirement to buy those rounds. Requests outside local finite ceilings fail before payment.

The complete 456-byte core is immutable for a logical quoted result. `quote_id` and `circuit_authorization_id` are independent fresh 32-byte service-generated opaque identifiers, protected inside encryption. Generation identifiers are service-scoped u64 counters authenticated by their descriptor delegations; persistence/recovery must prevent ambiguous reuse. They are not public cross-service tracking tokens.

For `status=1`, `refusal_reason=0`, `first_round=1`, `round_count=10`, and `round_seconds=60`. Ten rounds describe the initial allocation only; clients still buy one minute at a time and can stop. `advance_seconds`, `grace_seconds`, build timeout and maximum age/round ceilings come from understood authenticated policy and must fit client limits, key/quote deadlines, and the timing rules in spec two. Encoding these fields does not establish a measured universal payment lead/grace or override operator policy. Start policy `1` means the initial setup/first round is settled and reserved before paid construction, with a bounded build deadline; the exact start/readiness event exchange must be specified before deployment. Other start policies are rejected.

Reply `features` uses the request bit assignments and must satisfy the required set. `funding_policy_ref`, `payment_issuer_ref`, and `acquisition_ref` are 32-byte authenticated references, never URL redirects or proof of settlement. The funding policy must resolve to a fully understood authenticated record specifying fee treatment, start/deadline rules, failure/retry/refund policy and limits before acceptance; a hash of unknown terms cannot authorize payment. The payment issuer reference binds the intended receiver under the authenticated method policy. Acquisition kind `1` denotes the bounded service-issued BOLT 11 invoice mechanism; kind `2` denotes a BOLT 12 offer selected from authenticated metadata. The acquisition reference identifies that mechanism/offer without embedding the variable invoice or offer. Bind the later invoice, receiver, exact amount and round to this SCQ under the separate acquisition protocol. [Spec two's catalog section](#authenticated-payment-policy-catalog-and-reference-resolution) now defines service/type-scoped content references and bounded authenticated resolution. Full descriptor/catalog/offer/policy canonical field schemas remain required work; unsupported/unresolved references fail without payment. Accepted snapshots are retained rather than silently replaced by a newer descriptor.

For `status=2`, keep version/method/service/capability/inquiry/logical digest/client/RP confirmation fields valid. Zero every subsequent core field from `quote_id` through `acquisition_ref`; set only `refusal_reason` to a recognized nonzero code. Initial codes: `1` unavailable capacity/path; `2` requested budget/terms unsupported; `3` authenticated capability snapshot stale; `4` method unavailable. Unknown codes are terminal non-payable failures; authenticated stale metadata still follows the existing bounded refresh policy. Missing/invalid version negotiation is not an exception permitting a payable refusal. The wrapper and service signature remain valid, and the refusal uses the same reply size/scheduler as success. Zero fields must never be interpreted as a zero-price offer.

## 4. Fresh reply wrapper and signatures

Append the following to the core; offsets are relative to the full reply body:

| Offset | Bytes | Field |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 456 | 1 | `record_kind` |
| 457 | 8 | `signer_generation` |
| 465 | 8 | `request_key_generation` |
| 473 | 32 | `delivery_nonce` |
| 505 | 32 | `reply_encryption_key` |
| 537 | 32 | `delivery_request_digest` |

The signed reply body is 569 bytes. `record_kind=2`; request-key generation, nonce, and reply key echo the active authenticated delivery. `signer_generation` selects a currently authorized quote signer under the independently verified descriptor chain; an in-message generation alone delegates nothing. Append the 64-byte service signature at offset 569 for a 633-byte signed reply record. Cached replies may reuse the exact core but must create and authenticate the new wrapper and fresh HPKE delivery. Normal signer rotation may therefore preserve the core without reissuing purchases or repricing.

Define `D(label) = ASCII(label) || 0x00` (exact ASCII without whitespace, followed by one zero byte), and `L(x)=u32be(length(x)) || x`. Use plain Ed25519 over the complete following bytes, not Ed25519ph or an unsigned reconstruction:

- Client proof: `D("eltor/scq/v1/client-request") || L(request_body)`.
- Service signature: `D("eltor/scq/v1/service-reply") || L(reply_body)`; `status` is inside that signed body, so a refusal cannot be recast as success.
- Logical request digest: `SHA256(D("eltor/scq/v1/logical-request") || L(request_body[0:244]))`.
- Delivery request digest: `SHA256(D("eltor/scq/v1/delivery-request") || L(request_body || client_signature))`.
- Stable core digest when referenced by a future acceptance: `SHA256(D("eltor/scq/v1/quote-core") || L(reply_body[0:456]))`. This does not itself define or authorize that later acceptance protocol.

Validate exact original bytes and lengths; reject alternate serialization, integer overflow, unknown fields and unallocated trailing content. Logical idempotency is scoped by service + client authorization + logical inquiry and checked against the stable digest across key generations. A changed request proof or delivery nonce must not create a new purchase under the same inquiry. Retain durable payment/replay state separately from ciphertext caches and reply slots.

## 5. Protected envelope and fixed capacities

The HPKE plaintext is `u16be(record_length) || signed_record || zero_padding`. The request record length MUST be 380; reply/refusal record length MUST be 633. Padding fills the fixed plaintext capacity and MUST be zero after successful AEAD verification. Padding and true length are inside authenticated encryption; do not parse or allocate from unauthenticated decrypted bytes. Reject inconsistent lengths, extra records, nonzero inner padding and truncated ciphertexts.

Use the fixed suite from spec two. HPKE `info` is exactly `D("eltor/scq/v1/hpke-request")` for requests and `D("eltor/scq/v1/hpke-reply")` for replies. AAD is exactly `D("eltor/scq/v1/envelope") || u8(kind)`, with kind 1/2 matching direction and the inner signed record. AAD carries no identity, key generation, price or logical inquiry; those are bound inside. Each delivery creates a fresh standard single-shot base-mode HPKE context, with no separate invented nonce field. Wire ciphertext is `enc[32] || ciphertext_with_tag`; receiver selection is bounded as specified in spec two.

| Component | Request | Reply or service refusal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Canonical body | 316 | 569 = 456 core + 113 wrapper |
| Ed25519 signature | 64 | 64 |
| Encrypted true-length prefix | 2 | 2 |
| Authenticated zero padding | 4 | 185 |
| Fixed HPKE plaintext | **386** | **820** |
| Encapsulation + AEAD tag | 32 + 16 | 32 + 16 |
| Fixed encrypted envelope | **434** | **868** |
| Fragment data capacity | 434 | 434 |
| Fragments per transport leg | **1** | **2** |
| Per-fragment header | 64 | 64 |
| Relay Length per fragment | **498** | **498** |

The request is the maximum initial field inventory with one signature and no embedded invoice, offer, delegation certificate, service-side relay list, or free-form text. Reply references use the same fixed widths for both methods; additional artifacts need their own bounded authenticated mechanism. Never grow this record or add method-specific fragments. The 386-byte request plaintext is the largest plaintext fitting one 434-byte fragment after HPKE overhead; the 820-byte reply plaintext similarly fits two fragments. These are derived layout proposals, not measured free-bandwidth budgets or latency guarantees. A future schema exceeding them requires explicit profile/version work.

## 6. Fragment/routing header (64 bytes)

Every new quote-transport command uses ordinary RELAY cells, StreamID zero, with the same cell sizing and normal per-hop encryption/padding as the rest of this fork; it must not consume RELAY_EARLY. Relay command opcode allocation and capability negotiation remain open. All four legs (client→intro, intro→service, service→intro, intro→client) use the same 498-byte per-fragment logical Length:

| Offset | Bytes | Field |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 1 | framing version, 1 |
| 1 | 1 | request 1 or reply 2 |
| 2 | 1 | zero-based fragment index |
| 3 | 1 | fragment count, request 1 or reply 2 |
| 4 | 32 | role-specific routing value |
| 36 | 16 | fresh leg-local delivery token |
| 52 | 12 | reserved, zero |
| 64 | 434 | envelope slice (outside the 64-byte header) |

Client→intro routing value is the existing 32-byte Ed25519 introduction authentication public key identifying the registered service (the key used by the relay-side introduction circuit map). Intro→service and service→intro routing value is the relay-allocated 16-byte reply-slot handle followed by 16 zero bytes. Intro→client routing value is all zero. The client generates a fresh unpredictable 16-byte token for its admitted attempt; the intro allocates a separate fresh token for the service-facing leg, rewriting routing/token fields when forwarding. Replies echo only the token for their own leg. Neither token is a payment ID, an end-to-end client identity, or shared across different introduction points. Do not use a raw/recycled circuit number as a token or forwarding destination.

Each relay-owned slot maps the actual circuit instances, service registration, both leg tokens, handle, expected kinds/counts, and admitted byte/deadline budgets. Forward only to the mapped destination and validate service replies against the actual registered service circuit and slot. The headers are hop-local routing metadata, not the end-to-end quote signature or a payment proof; rewriting them is permitted only by the corresponding relay and cannot override the signed delivery binding. Encrypt and authenticate all sensitive content independently; a malicious intro may still delay/drop/replay and knows its own routing association.

Admit provisional reassembly/slot work under strict aggregate caps from the first fragment. The absolute admission expiry is not reset by fragments; an incomplete message cannot refresh it. Require index 0 then index 1 for a reply on each ordered leg; request index is always 0. Reject wrong count/kind/token/route/reserved fields, conflicting duplicates, out-of-order/extra fragments, wrong circuit instances, and late fragments. Accept at most the exact envelope length, verify the complete encrypted record before acting, and release bounded transport state under the existing slot rules. A replay cannot reserve unbounded replacement slots. Retransmission uses the existing new-delivery/backoff policy rather than adding an ACK-driven fragment retry loop. Authenticated refusal still requires both reply fragments; malformed/unadmitted/overload failures are not owed cover traffic.

The 498-byte logical limit is a conservative profile budget, not a claim that this fork sends upstream-sized cells: local `tor/src/core/or/or.h` defines CELL_PAYLOAD_SIZE=2048 and an 11-byte relay header, leaving 2037 bytes of relay data. These messages still use Length=498 and the remaining normal fork padding; this draft does not resize cells or assume older clients interoperate. Normal queue, CPU, free-capacity and padding accounting apply to every forwarded copy. A complete successful request/reply consumes six quote fragments across all four legs (one + one + two + two), or 2988 bytes of logical relay payload; actual link traffic, three-hop forwarding in each direction, maintenance and outer padding add overhead.

## 7. Privacy, validation, and remaining gates

**Privacy/timing assessment:** both methods have identical request/reply envelope sizes and fragment counts, including refusals. True lengths, stable IDs, prices, method, client proof and signing/key-generation references remain encrypted. Outer intro registration and leg-local routing state are visible to the terminating introduction relay, which already knows its circuit association; no new service-role marker is added to guard/middle payment headers. Fixed sizes do not hide the existence of the quote exchange, inter-fragment timing, invoice acquisition, traffic correlation, stale-cache behavior or overload. The shared response scheduler must budget completion of both reply fragments before expiry, with the same rule for fresh/cached/refused results; retry/failover remains bounded and sequential. Separate invoice/offer acquisition can still expose method-related timing/size and wallet counterparty information. Do not claim anonymity or full-method indistinguishability from this layout.

Before implementation: independently check offsets and maximum sizes, RFC primitive vectors and El Tor known-answer vectors; compare C/Rust canonical bytes/signatures/HPKE domains/digests; fuzz parsing, lengths, flags, overflow, nonzero padding, wrong domains/keys, altered terms/proofs and unknown enums. Test refusal-as-free-quote confusion; late/duplicate/reordered fragment/slot handling; malicious routing/header rewriting; replay across key generations, new slots and restart; immutable cached cores with fresh wrappers; current/retiring key limits; no duplicate invoice/payment/paid build. Measure endpoint-visible Length/count/timing across both methods and refusal/cache paths, all four transport legs, aggregate free resources and honest success under load. Only documentation/offset/arithmetic checks accompany this draft; no live payment, packet trace, independent crypto audit or runtime implementation has run for it.

Remaining gates include canonical descriptor/delegation/policy/issuer/offer-reference records and freshness, relay opcode allocation/feature negotiation, invoice/acceptance/status/continuation wire, concrete lifecycle/timing/resource budgets, verified directory identity mapping, secure key storage/replay persistence, strict library verification behavior, independent crypto review, and runtime interoperability/privacy tests. Keep [ET-13/14/18/19/21/23/24/25/26](../TODO.md) deferred. This draft does not start the separate paid-circuit replay-protection implementation.

## Circuit construction inherited from spec one

All circuits in this proposal MUST be built the same way as [spec one](01_paid_circuits.md#shared-circuit-construction-and-bounded-free-allowance): descriptor upload/fetch circuits, both introduction paths, and both rendezvous paths inherit the ordinary El Tor per-hop payment construction. Retain the handshake hash/preimage fields, ten unique round identifiers per hop, per-hop encryption, and equal-size padding. Use fresh dummy handshake values where no fee applies; do not omit fields or introduce shared constants for free circuits.

The small free allowance is part of spec one's shared circuit/resource policy, not an onion-service-only circuit header or a shorter payment payload. No guard/middle payment field needs to label its origin as an onion service, and construction size or payment-field presence MUST NOT vary merely because a circuit is using the free allowance. Normal Tor introduction, directory, and rendezvous commands still operate at their intended endpoints. The client-to-service access authorization in this document is carried separately inside the service-encrypted introduction message; it does not replace or change the shared relay-payment construction.

One-minute paid rounds, ten-round payment allocations, early closure, and idle periods are ordinary El Tor lifecycle behavior. Paid renewal inherits spec one; without compatible renewal, expiration still requires replacement. This section preserves the same construction and resource policy across roles; it is not a claim that every traffic pattern is indistinguishable or that these proposed extensions are implemented.

**Service setup:** Before a client arrives, the service establishes circuits to its introduction points and publishes a descriptor listing them to hidden-service directories. The service keeps introduction circuits available using the bounded free allowance described below. Descriptor upload is a separate operation; publishing a descriptor does not require retaining its upload circuit. Introduction circuits carry introduction messages, while the later rendezvous circuits carry application traffic.

The sequence below uses BOLT 12 as the recommended example. It does not require BOLT 12 or receiver-hiding paths; other mutually supported, verifiable payment methods are permitted under the payment-method policy below.

Below is a step-by-step breakdown of this sequence:

Before accepting a payable SCQ or initiating connection-specific paid work, complete the [El Tor version agreement](#el-tor-service-version-agreement) through the [bounded introduction-point bootstrap](#quote-bootstrap-through-the-introduction-point) below. Local version selection precedes the request; the authenticated SCQ response confirms it. Payment handling is mandatory in El Tor, including zero-fee cases; there is no optional-payment flag or ordinary-Tor compatibility mode in this protocol.

1. **Client Fetches Hidden Service Descriptor:** The client uses the onion address to query the Tor network’s distributed hash table (HS directory) for the hidden service’s descriptor ([Tor Project | How do Onion Services work?](https://community.torproject.org/onion-services/overview/#:~:text=it%2C%20so%20they%20connect%20to,descriptor%20of%20SecureDrop%27s%20Onion%20Service)). The descriptor is signed by the HS and contains a list of its introduction point addresses/keys ([Tor Project | How do Onion Services work?](https://community.torproject.org/onion-services/overview/#:~:text=And%20inside%20the%20descriptor%2C%20there,themselves%20to%20SecureDrop%27s%20Onion%20Service)). The HS descriptor also advertises its accepted payment methods and the information needed to request a Service Circuit Quote (SCQ) and obtain a fresh payment request. In this BOLT 12 example, it includes a Lightning **BOLT 12 offer** that the client can use to request a fresh invoice. The method list and invoice-acquisition mechanism must be authenticated with the service descriptor; exact encoding remains open.

2. **Service Circuit Quote and Invoice Request:** Before funding the service's path, the client selects its proposed rendezvous relay and a fresh client authorization key and requests an SCQ through the bounded introduction-point request/reply mechanism below, opening an eligible free introduction circuit before payment. Selecting the rendezvous relay does not require the service to construct its circuit. The service reads candidate relays' descriptors, selects a compatible private path, and quotes the combined per-minute relay cost plus disclosed setup and routing-fee budgets. The client may reject the quote without authorizing paid work by the service. If it accepts, it obtains an initial-round invoice bound to that SCQ, circuit authorization identifier, and client key. In the BOLT 12 example, the advertised offer is used to request a fresh invoice through Lightning; other methods follow their negotiated invoice-acquisition mechanism. Quote delivery, acceptance, and invoice binding remain wire-protocol work. An invoice request or dummy handshake value alone does not authorize service-side spending.

3. **Client Pays Invoice (Lightning Payment):** The client’s Lightning wallet routes the payment through the Lightning Network to the hidden service’s node. Upon successful payment, the hidden service’s Lightning node receives the funds and, as per the Lightning protocol, releases the secret preimage associated with the invoice’s `payment_hash` ([How to prove that a lightning invoice was paid? | Blink FAQ](https://faq.blink.sv/blink-and-other-wallets/how-to-prove-that-a-lightning-invoice-was-paid#:~:text=,was%20included%20in%20the%20invoice)). The preimage travels back to the client as proof of payment. At this point, the payment is complete – the client knows the payment succeeded (having received the preimage), and the hidden service knows it has been paid. The client retains the `payment_hash` and the preimage returned on settlement; the preimage cannot be derived from the hash. These identify and demonstrate payment, but do not by themselves authenticate the connecting client. Before payment, the service must associate the invoice with a fresh client authorization key and agreed terms, for the intended circuit and initial round, as described in revision 1 below.

4. **Client Establishes a Rendezvous Point:** In parallel with the payment (or afterward), the client prepares for the Tor connection. It uses the **rendezvous point (RP)** selected for the accepted SCQ. The client builds a 3-hop Tor circuit to that relay and sends a `RELAY_COMMAND_ESTABLISH_RENDEZVOUS` message to designate it as the RP ([Tor Project | How do Onion Services work?](https://community.torproject.org/onion-services/overview/#:~:text=Before%20the%20introduction%20takes%20place%2C,part%20of%20the%20rendezvous%20procedure)). This message contains a randomly generated 20-byte **rendezvous cookie** that will serve as a shared secret for linking circuits ([The rendezvous protocol - Tor Specifications](https://spec.torproject.org/rend-spec/rendezvous-protocol.html#:~:text=The%20client%20sends%20the%20rendezvous,byte%20value)). The rendezvous point stores this cookie and replies with a `RENDEZVOUS_ESTABLISHED` acknowledgment (confirming it’s ready) ([torspec/rend-spec-v3.txt at main · torproject/torspec · GitHub](https://github.com/torproject/torspec/blob/main/rend-spec-v3.txt#:~:text=39%20)). At this stage, the RP is “parked” waiting for the hidden service to connect with the same cookie; it does not yet know which HS will connect.

5. **Client Builds Introduction Circuit:** For the funded connection, the client reuses its still-eligible bootstrap introduction circuit or opens a new Tor circuit to one of the hidden service’s **introduction points** listed in the descriptor. This is also a 3-hop anonymized circuit terminating at the chosen intro point. (The HS is already connected to this intro point via its own long-lived circuit.) Now the client has two circuits in place – one to the RP and one to the Intro point – and has paid the required Lightning invoice. The client is ready to introduce itself to the HS.

6. **Client Sends `INTRODUCE1` (with Rendezvous Info & Payment Authorization):** Over the intro circuit, the client sends an **`INTRODUCE1`** cell to the introduction relay, requesting introduction to the HS ([torspec/rend-spec-v3.txt at main · torproject/torspec · GitHub](https://github.com/torproject/torspec/blob/main/rend-spec-v3.txt#:~:text=34%20)). This cell (encrypted to the HS’s service key) contains: the rendezvous cookie generated in step 4, the identity of the chosen rendezvous point, the client’s ephemeral key and other handshake data, **and a compact payment authorization** referencing the invoice and proving possession of its bound client authorization key. The authorization covers this connection attempt, including its rendezvous details, as described in revision 1; a payment hash alone is not an access credential. The introduction point then forwards this to the hidden service over the HS’s intro circuit as an **`INTRODUCE2`** cell ([torspec/rend-spec-v3.txt at main · torproject/torspec · GitHub](https://github.com/torproject/torspec/blob/main/rend-spec-v3.txt#:~:text=35%20)). In essence, the intro point “introduces” the client to the HS by delivering the client’s rendezvous details and payment proof. The hidden service receives the INTRODUCE2 message (containing the rendezvous cookie, RP address and payment authorization) ([Tor Project | How do Onion Services work?](https://community.torproject.org/onion-services/overview/#:~:text=The%20introduction%20point%20passes%20your,whether%20you%27re%20trustworthy%20or%20not)).

7. **Hidden Service Verifies Payment and Reserves Round Funding:** Upon receiving `INTRODUCE2`, the HS verifies the client-bound authorization and checks its own Lightning node's invoice records through a synchronized local ledger. The invoice must have been issued by this service for the accepted SCQ, agreed payment method, offer where applicable, circuit/round amount, and terms, and must be settled. When the authorization supplies a preimage, the service checks that its SHA-256 hash equals the invoice's `payment_hash`; a matching pair without the service's settled invoice record is insufficient. The service then atomically reserves the settled funding assigned to this circuit's initial round, including any agreed setup costs, before constructing a paid circuit. Invalid, expired, unpaid, or replayed authorizations do not trigger paid circuit construction. Wallet synchronization happens asynchronously rather than through a blocking wallet RPC in the Tor handshake. `INTRODUCE_ACK` is sent by the introduction point to report introduction delivery; it is not confirmation that the service accepted payment. Service-level rejection/status signaling still needs an explicitly defined protocol.

8. **Hidden Service Builds Circuit to Rendezvous Point:** The hidden service now initiates the rendezvous from its side. Using the round funding reserved in step 7, the HS selects and pays its own relays and creates a new 3-hop Tor circuit out to the chosen rendezvous point (using the rendezvous address provided by the client in the INTRODUCE2 message). Once this circuit to the RP is established, the hidden service sends a **`RENDEZVOUS1`** cell to the rendezvous point over that circuit ([torspec/rend-spec-v3.txt at main · torproject/torspec · GitHub](https://github.com/torproject/torspec/blob/main/rend-spec-v3.txt#:~:text=36%20)). This message includes the same 20-byte rendezvous cookie (received via the intro point) and the HS’s part of the handshake (encrypted with the client’s public key from the introduction handshake). By sending RENDEZVOUS1, the HS is effectively saying “I am the hidden service that the client is trying to reach, here is the cookie to match and my handshake data.” The rendezvous point, upon seeing the cookie, recognizes it from step 4 and knows this HS corresponds to the waiting client connection.

9. **Exchanging `RENDEZVOUS1/2` and Completing Handshake:** When the HS’s RENDEZVOUS1 cell arrives at the rendezvous point, the RP links together the two circuits that share the same cookie (the client’s circuit from step 4 and the HS’s circuit from step 8). It then forwards the HS’s handshake data to the client by sending a **`RENDEZVOUS2`** cell down the client’s rendezvous circuit ([torspec/rend-spec-v3.txt at main · torproject/torspec · GitHub](https://github.com/torproject/torspec/blob/main/rend-spec-v3.txt#:~:text=37%20)). The RENDEZVOUS2 carries the hidden service’s encrypted handshake information to the client. The client’s Tor instance receives RENDEZVOUS2 and completes the cryptographic handshake (e.g. finishing the ntor key agreement using the HS’s public key in the handshake) ([Tor Project | How do Onion Services work?](https://community.torproject.org/onion-services/overview/#:~:text=The%20Onion%20Service%20connects%20to,been%20relayed%20through%20the%20service)). At this point, the rendezvous is established: the client and HS now have a shared session key, and the two Tor circuits are joined at the RP. (The rendezvous point has verified the cookie from both sides matched, and it will simply pass traffic between the client and HS from now on.)

10. **Rendezvous Circuit Established – Data Flows:** The client and hidden service now have an end-to-end encrypted channel through the rendezvous point. The Tor rendezvous handshake is complete, and a secure Tor circuit spans from the client to the HS (consisting of 3 hops from the client to RP, plus 3 hops from the HS to RP) ([Tor Project | How do Onion Services work?](https://community.torproject.org/onion-services/overview/#:~:text=Act%209%3A%20Where%20the%20Onion,Service%20rendezvous%20with%20the%20client)). The rendezvous point does not learn anything about the data or identities; it only knows the cookie used to connect the circuits. At this stage, the client can begin communicating with the hidden service (for example, sending an HTTP request if the HS is a web server). All data (the “secure data stream”) is tunneled through the rendezvous circuit – encrypted end-to-end between the client and HS and relayed by the RP. The hidden service has successfully received payment prior to communication, and the client and HS now communicate over the paid rendezvous circuit as they normally would over any Tor hidden service connection.

## Quote bootstrap through the introduction point

### Chosen architecture

Use the client's introduction circuit and the service's already established introduction circuit to exchange an end-to-end encrypted quote before the service builds a paid rendezvous circuit. Both paths use spec one's ordinary construction and explicitly admitted bounded free allowance at every hop. No additional service-side circuit is built for an unpaid quote request. This avoids a circular dependency in which the client must buy the connection before learning its price.

This requires a new El Tor request/reply extension. Standard Tor `INTRODUCE_ACK` reports forwarding status, not a service-generated response, and ordinary clients normally close a successful introduction circuit. The proposed bootstrap keeps the client circuit open for the bounded reply and uses separate handlers/messages; a quote request MUST NOT accidentally enter the ordinary introduction handler that builds a rendezvous circuit. The existing introduction acknowledgment is never a substitute for an authenticated quote. See the [Tor introduction protocol](https://spec.torproject.org/rend-spec/introduction-protocol.html).

### Request and reply flow

1. **Discover and select.** The client verifies/decrypts the descriptor, selects El Tor service version 1 and a supported payment method, and chooses an advertised introduction point supporting the quote return extension. It chooses its proposed rendezvous relay without needing to build that circuit yet. Service metadata must authenticate the quote capability, envelope/delegation keys, and supported limits. Unsupported or unadmitted paths fail within bounded retries; no ordinary-Tor or paid-bootstrap fallback is implied.
2. **Send an encrypted request.** Build or use an eligible client-to-introduction circuit. Send a bounded logical `SCQ_REQUEST` carrying the selected version, descriptor capability digest, fresh request nonce, client authorization key, a fresh reply-encryption public key, chosen payment method, proposed rendezvous relay, and requested terms/budget limits. Authenticate the request fields together and prove control of the relevant client authorization key under the final envelope design. A signing key is not automatically an encryption key; their use/derivation needs explicit reviewed specification. Encrypt the sensitive payload to the descriptor-authenticated service key.
3. **Allocate a reply slot and forward.** The introduction relay locates the service's existing introduction circuit using its established introduction registration. If its admission limits permit, it generates a fresh unpredictable, relay-local return handle and records an expiring mapping to both actual circuit instances, that service registration, the request, and a strict reply budget. It forwards the opaque encrypted request plus the routing handle to the service. The client cannot supply an arbitrary destination circuit ID. The relay need not decrypt the request, learn the SCQ price, or receive a wallet identity.
4. **Calculate without paid construction.** The service authenticates the request, checks its version/capability/budget/nonce, and selects a compatible candidate relay path from cached verified descriptors. It calculates the fixed SCQ using those rates and the disclosed fee budgets. This is bounded selection and quoting, not capacity reservation, a paid build, or an outgoing relay payment. Duplicate logical requests reuse the existing quote/result rather than choosing more paths, extending validity, or allocating more spending.
5. **Return the encrypted quote.** The service sends a bounded logical `SCQ_REPLY` over its existing introduction circuit, with the relay-local return handle outside the end-to-end encrypted quote. The relay accepts it only from the exact service circuit/registration associated with that slot and forwards it only to the recorded live client circuit instance. The quote authenticates the service/delegation, version, descriptor capability digest, request nonce/digest, client authorization and reply-key binding, proposed rendezvous relay, quote/authorization IDs, price, fees, expiry, schedule, and remaining SCQ terms. Encrypt it to the request-specific client reply key; routing permission alone is not service authentication.
6. **Verify, then fund.** The client decrypts and verifies the exact request/SCQ bindings and confirms version agreement before quote acceptance or payment. It may reject the quote without causing a paid service build. On acceptance, obtain a fresh correctly bound payment request using the selected method, settle initial-round/setup funding, and reserve it under the existing rules. Only then may the service incur paid relay work and build its rendezvous circuit. The later authenticated paid introduction binds the accepted SCQ/client to the actual rendezvous cookie and handshake as required above.

`SCQ_REQUEST` and `SCQ_REPLY` are proposed logical message names, not assigned relay opcodes or deployed interfaces. Client→intro→service and service→intro→client directions need explicit versioned framing and handler rules. The routing handle is local to this one introduction relay/request; it is not a payment ID, SCQ ID, service identity, or common token distributed to guards/middles/other introduction points. A malicious introduction relay can still drop, delay, or misroute ciphertext; it must not be able to forge a quote the client accepts.

Invoice acquisition may use Lightning-native mechanisms such as a BOLT 12 invoice request. If a supported method needs an additional Tor quote-acceptance/invoice/status exchange, give each logical request its own admitted, expiring reply slot and authenticated SCQ/client binding. Do not turn the slot into an unlimited bidirectional connection. These bounded follow-up message encodings remain wire-design work; obtaining an invoice alone is not funding or permission to spend.

Referenced metadata schemas, independent review and implementation remain deferred.

| Field group | Required information and binding |

The El Tor method model MUST support both **BOLT 11 invoice payments** and **BOLT 12 offer-based invoice acquisition/payments**. BOLT 11 supplies the payable invoice; it is not itself a reusable offer format. For BOLT 11, the service issues a fresh round-bound invoice through the authenticated bounded mechanism. For BOLT 12, an advertised offer can obtain a fresh bound invoice for each new round. Retrying the same unresolved payment must first reconcile its existing invoice/transaction rather than blindly generating and paying another one. An offer, invoice request, or quote is not payment or authorization for additional rounds.

Protocol support for both formats does not require every receiver's wallet to implement both. The receiver advertises its actual methods; the client selects their supported intersection before payment. No silent method change is allowed after quote acceptance. BOLT 12 with receiver-hiding blinded paths remains preferred where supported, while BOLT 11 and other explicitly integrated methods follow the existing optional receiver-privacy policy. Neither the BOLT number nor the existence of an offer proves privacy or recipient settlement.

**Privacy/timing assessment:** all request fields above, including method, logical ID, public keys, destination, and price limits, stay inside the authenticated end-to-end encrypted fixed-size envelope. Intermediate routing uses only its independently bound local handle; a request needs no client IP, persistent wallet identity, software inventory, or shared payment ID across hops. Both method selections use the same quote-envelope and response/retry scheduling rules. Invoice acquisition/settlement can have separate size, latency, and counterparty disclosures and must be assessed in its own bounded exchange, rather than silently expanding the quote or claiming identical Lightning traces. Codec sizes, invoice binding, wallet interoperability, and privacy/timing measurements remain deferred under ET-13/14/18/21/23; only documentation checks are performed for this change.

The version-one cryptographic profile below fixes the signature/encryption suite. The introduction relay's forwarding acknowledgment is not this reply.

| --- | --- |
| Request confirmation | Exact service version, intended service and descriptor/capability snapshot, logical inquiry ID/digest, client authorization binding, proposed rendezvous relay, and selected BOLT 11/BOLT 12 method. | Price and fees | Fixed per-round service-side relay total and disclosed service-side routing-fee allowance, separately itemized initial setup/handshake charges and any application fee, explicit integer units/rounding, and all applicable fee caps. Separate the stable logical quote core from the fresh per-delivery reply envelope. The core binds the stable request fields/authorization, quote/connection identifiers, fixed price and terms, selected method, intended service/destination, and original expiry. Each delivery authenticates the exact core or its digest together with the active request's fresh transport nonce, reply key, and delivery-request digest, then uses fresh reply encryption. The final canonical encoding must distinguish a logical-request digest from a digest of the complete delivery request; neither may be an ambiguous hash of an unspecified field subset.

A retry may return the same still-valid core/result inside a newly authenticated encrypted delivery envelope. It MUST NOT reuse the old delivery ciphertext, change the quote price/expiry, substitute another reply key without client authorization, issue competing quote IDs to create duplicate purchases, or make an abandoned delivery payable again. Fresh transport binding does not create a new circuit authorization or reusable payment balance. Authenticate all quote fields and delivery bindings under the service identity/delegation, not merely the routing handle, an invoice hash, or an unauthenticated outer header.

The client must verify the full service/request/delivery bindings, expiry, status, amount/limits, method, and required features before allowing acceptance or invoice payment. The service must authenticate the resulting acceptance and invoice association under the same agreement. Payment instructions must use the negotiated authenticated bounded mechanism or the selected method's native wallet flow; a relay-supplied redirect cannot substitute a payee or new transport. No private service-side relay identities or origin IPs are included in the reply.

An authenticated service refusal uses the same padded reply profile and shared response scheduler, with its bounded reason inside encryption. It still binds the intended service, logical inquiry, and current delivery. Its status MUST make all unused quote/payment fields non-actionable under a canonical encoding; padding or zero-valued fields cannot be interpreted as a valid zero-fee quote. Refusal is never authority to issue outgoing payments or bypass version/method/budget checks. Relay admission failures and timeouts remain separate unauthenticated transport outcomes.

**Privacy/timing assessment:** quote/connection identifiers, request references, method, pricing, acquisition instructions, and authentication material remain inside fresh end-to-end encrypted fixed-size replies. Public-key authentication must not introduce a persistent client wallet identity or distribute stable references across transport hops. Cached cores retain idempotency while per-delivery authentication/encryption changes observable ciphertext; fresh/cached/refused responses share the established scheduling rule. Receiver-selected payment methods can still reveal information to wallet counterparties, and intro routing, timing, loss, and congestion remain observable. Test wrong-service/key/digest/nonce/RP/method substitution, altered prices/expiry/fee caps, stale/competing quotes, abandoned-slot replay, invalid refusal padding, invoice/payee substitution, and exposed length/timing across methods. Encoding, crypto/delegation review, wallet integration, and runtime measurements remain deferred under ET-13/14/18/21/23; documentation checks alone do not prove secure implementation.

### Quote reply authentication and encryption

Use a **service signature inside an HPKE-encrypted reply**. The service has a separate, short-lived quote-signing key; the client learns its authorized public key through verified, encrypted service-descriptor metadata. The normal descriptor authenticity chain anchors this delegation. Quoting must not require the service's long-term onion-identity private key online, reuse a Lightning wallet key, or invent a new public identity field in the descriptor's outer wrapper.

The descriptor-authenticated delegation must identify the service, key/algorithm, allowed quote/bootstrap signing purpose, protocol version, and bounded validity. Scope keys to one onion service and authorized validity period; do not share one quote-signing identity across unrelated services. Only explicit delegations are trusted. The lifecycle rules below govern expiry, rotation, and known compromise. The signature algorithm is fixed by the version-one profile below. Exact record encoding, numeric lifetimes/overlap, clock tolerance, and authenticated revocation representation remain design work; publishing a key somewhere in an unverified response is not delegation.

For each delivery, authenticate/sign the complete canonical logical quote or refusal and its current delivery binding: service/version/delegation, capability snapshot, quote/connection IDs, logical and delivery request digests, client authorization, delivery nonce/reply key, destination, selected payment method, all prices/fees/limits/deadlines, and status. Keep those semantics unambiguous and domain-separated from other signatures. An unchanged cached quote core may be reused, but the authenticated per-delivery binding and encryption must be fresh. The final construction must specify exactly which bytes are signed and how any signed core is committed into the delivery signature.

After signing, serialize the signature with the message, include the true length inside the protected envelope, pad to the agreed fixed plaintext capacity, and encrypt the whole padded reply to the client's fresh per-delivery public encryption key using [HPKE, RFC 9180](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9180.html). The proposed profile uses HPKE base-mode encryption plus the separate inner service signature; a successful HPKE decrypt alone does not prove the service sent the message. Use a reviewed implementation with fresh encapsulation/randomness per delivery and proper key validation. Do not reuse AEAD nonces, invent a replacement KDF/nonce schedule, or treat a signature key as an encryption key by reinterpreting its bytes.

The client decrypts and authenticates the envelope, parses canonical bounded fields, validates the descriptor/delegation and key validity, verifies the service signature over the complete delivery, and then checks the active request, status, version, nonce/keys, amounts, method, and expiry before acceptance or payment. A malformed or validly encrypted but unsigned/wrong-service/wrong-delivery reply fails. A service refusal follows the same protected format and response scheduler, while remaining non-payable. Enforce crypto/parse/reassembly work limits even for failures.

Use the fixed signature algorithm and HPKE KEM/KDF/AEAD suite in the version-one profile below; do not accept arbitrary peer-selected algorithms or silent fallback. Canonical `info`/associated-data domains and framing are also required. HPKE associated data is authenticated rather than encrypted: it must not expose stable quote IDs, client keys, service identities, prices, or wallet details merely to bind context. Keep sensitive bindings inside the encrypted signed message, with only the necessary fixed transport information outside. Request encryption and service request-key lifecycle follow the separate draft below; the quote-signing key is not a request-decryption key.

Store and retire keys under an explicit lifecycle: validate scope/expiry before using a delegation, bound cached authorization, and erase per-delivery encryption secrets when no longer needed. Deleting a key must not discard durable payment/replay records or reopen an expired quote. Descriptor refresh is not instantaneous revocation. Apply the lifecycle policy below to cached delegations and in-flight accepted quotes without silently moving funds or trusting replacement keys; finalize its encoding, persistence, freshness, and verification behavior before deployment.

**Privacy/timing assessment:** the descriptor-protected signer delegation and inner signature avoid a new exposed wallet/operator identity and prevent the introduction relay from authenticating its own substituted quote as the service. Fresh HPKE deliveries retain fixed encrypted sizes and the common scheduler, including signature/encapsulation/tag overhead in capacity and timing measurements. HPKE does not itself hide length, prevent protocol replay/downgrade, or provide forward secrecy after recipient-private-key compromise; ephemeral receiver keys, erasure, rotation, and the protocol's existing checks require separate review. The intro relay still sees its local routing relationship, and active delays/correlation remain possible. Test invalid/expired/wrong-service delegations, reply-key substitution, altered bindings, cached-core wrapping, malformed crypto inputs, algorithm downgrade, replay, key rotation/compromise, resource bounds, and signature/encryption timing. Only documentation checks are performed here; crypto review, complete encoding, suite interoperability, runtime support, and measurements remain deferred under ET-24, ET-14, ET-21, and ET-23 in the [El Tor TODO](../TODO.md).

### Quote-signing key expiry, rotation, and compromise

These rules govern the delegated service quote signer, separately from onion identity, descriptor-signing, wallet, and request/reply encryption keys. The descriptor-authenticated delegation supplies a bounded validity interval and signing scope. Before accepting a new quote or other fresh authorization, verify the trusted descriptor/delegation chain, service, purpose, version, and current key validity. Quote acceptance must occur before both quote expiry and the signing delegation's expiry, and not before the delegation becomes valid. A claimed signing timestamp does not establish issuance before expiry or compromise; backdating cannot restore authorization. Numeric lifetimes, overlap, freshness/refresh rules, clock tolerance, and exact boundary encoding must be defined and tested before implementation.

**Routine rotation:** publish and validate the replacement delegation before retiring the old signer, with bounded overlap. Each signer remains service-scoped and may issue new authorizations only while its delegation remains valid and unretired under the authenticated policy. Persist accepted quote terms, client/circuit bindings, round counters, payment/reservation state, and the trusted delegation snapshot needed to verify that acceptance. Historical public verification material may be retained; erase unnecessary private keys under an explicit retention policy without deleting accounting or replay state.

Normal signer retirement or expiry does not cancel already accepted terms, reprice a circuit, restart its payment schedule, or interrupt an already funded download. Existing accepted authorization remains subject to its original circuit, quote, round, spending, and lifetime limits. Any new continuation, replacement quote, or status authorization that requires a live signature must use a currently authorized signer and bind the existing client/circuit/quote state. Rotation does not itself authorize a new purchase, a second redemption, a transferable balance, or payments beyond the client's existing approval.

**Known compromise:** once a trusted local decision or validated update through the service's descriptor/delegation trust chain marks a signer compromised, reject new authorizations from it and pause further outgoing payments and paid renewals that depend on it. An untrusted relay error, an instruction signed only by the compromised key, or an arbitrary redirect cannot establish replacement authority. Independently authenticate the replacement through the service trust chain, reconcile accepted terms and settled/in-flight payment records, and obtain any required fresh client approval before resuming. A replacement signature does not automatically validate all claims made by the old key or authorize repricing, duplicate spending, or moving funds to a different recipient.

Already settled payments are not automatically refunded. Already funded circuit service remains governed by its existing round deadline, grace, and resource limits while recovery is pending; compromise handling cannot create indefinite unpaid continuation or silently reset deadlines. If trust cannot be restored within those limits, stop renewing and close under the existing rules. Preserve uncertain-payment reconciliation even when abandoning the circuit.

Cached valid descriptors may continue to authorize a compromised key until the client learns a trusted revocation or the bounded authorization expires. A descriptor update is not instantaneous global revocation. Bounded key validity and authenticated refresh reduce this interval but do not eliminate it, and clients must not treat an unauthenticated network message as proof of compromise. Recovery and refresh stay within the existing setup/retry/resource budgets and Tor guard/isolation policy; do not force immediate cross-path retries or unlimited polling. Exact revocation representation, stale-cache handling, crash recovery, and trust-chain-compromise behavior remain review/deployment gates.

**Privacy/timing assessment:** keep signer/delegation references and recovery bindings inside the existing encrypted fixed-size message profile; do not add shared cross-service or cross-hop identity tokens. Routine rotation preserves accepted state rather than exposing a new payment or rebuilding every circuit. Refresh/recovery timing, descriptor observations, disrupted renewals, and wallet activity can still be correlated; neither overlap nor expiry guarantees anonymity or immediate revocation. Test exact validity boundaries and clock tolerance, overlap and stale descriptors, active downloads across normal rotation, forged/backdated quotes and untrusted rekey claims, compromise during prepaid/in-flight rounds, and recovery without duplicate payments, counter resets, or cross-circuit reuse. Only documentation consistency checks are performed here; key-lifecycle implementation, independent crypto review, and runtime/privacy measurements remain deferred under [ET-24 and related tasks](../TODO.md).

### Request encryption and service decryption-key lifecycle

**Draft architecture:** use HPKE base-mode encryption for each quote request to a distinct, short-lived service request-encryption public key. Publish its authorization in the authenticated encrypted descriptor metadata, binding the intended service, request-encryption purpose, service version, fixed cryptographic profile, key generation, and bounded validity/admission window. Clients verify that delegation through the descriptor trust chain before encryption; an introduction relay or unauthenticated error cannot replace it. Keep this key separate from the quote signer, onion identity, wallet, Tor traffic keys, and each client's reply-encryption key. Keys are service-scoped, not shared across unrelated services. This is a new El Tor bootstrap design, not a change to ordinary Tor introduction-handshake cryptography or an implemented feature.

**Protect and bind the request.** The client proves possession of its fresh circuit-specific authorization key over the canonical request and current delivery binding, including the intended service, request-key generation, selected version/capability snapshot, logical inquiry, delivery nonce, reply key, destination, selected payment method, budget, and bounded request validity. Put that proof and the true content length inside the fixed padded plaintext, then encrypt with fresh HPKE encapsulation/randomness for every delivery. The final encoding must specify exact signed bytes and domain separation. HPKE base-mode encryption alone does not authenticate a client: anyone with the advertised public key can encrypt, and a self-asserted client key is not proof of payment, a unique person, or permission for paid work. Validate the client proof and all request semantics before candidate selection or allocating durable quote state; request receipt never triggers an outgoing payment or paid circuit build.

The service bounds framing/reassembly and decryption work before processing, then decrypts, parses canonical bounded fields, verifies the client proof, and checks the authenticated request-key generation, service/version, descriptor capability snapshot, validity, and delivery bindings. Reject wrong-service/key-generation/context substitution and invalid proofs. Keep sensitive fields, stable inquiry IDs, and key-generation references inside encryption. Do not introduce a public service request-key fingerprint or client identity solely to route/decrypt this message. HPKE `info`/associated data must use the final versioned domains and cannot be assumed confidential; necessary outer routing stays limited to the established introduction registration and fixed transport framing. Suite, signature, framing, and domain encodings still require the independent review gate below.

**Routine rotation and admission.** Publish a replacement request-key delegation before ending the old key's admission window. The client uses a currently authorized key with enough remaining validity for the bounded delivery budget; otherwise it refreshes authenticated metadata within the existing setup/retry limits or fails without payment. On the service, allow at most one current and one retiring request key for bounded overlap. Further rotation must not create an accumulating key archive or silently invalidate an outstanding overlap commitment; retire/drain a slot before another ordinary generation occupies it. Since no new public key selector is added, decryption may try only that bounded eligible key set, with key validation and bounded crypto work. Authenticate the key generation inside the plaintext, and specify unambiguous recipient selection and rejection of ambiguous results during crypto/encoding review. Key-position-dependent processing must be included in timing measurements; the shared response scheduler is not a proof of constant-time decryption.

After a key's admission cutoff, it cannot admit a new complete request merely because the sender claims to have encrypted or sent it earlier. Requests fully validated and admitted before cutoff may finish within their already bounded slot/quote deadlines. Keep an old private key only for the specified bounded draining needs, then erase it, including unnecessary persisted copies under the final retention/crash-recovery policy. Already decrypted admitted work does not require indefinite key retention. Exact validity, overlap, client clock tolerance, and drain durations remain unchosen. Admission cutoff and normal key retirement do not expire an accepted SCQ, reset round counters, reopen a paid circuit, or erase replay/payment records; subsequent status/continuation deliveries use a currently authorized request key while binding the existing agreement.

**Retries and compromise.** Each permitted retry uses a fresh delivery nonce, reply key, client proof, and HPKE ciphertext; preserve the logical inquiry and circuit-specific client authorization across key rotation. The service's idempotency ledger spans request-key generations. A re-encrypted delivery cannot reset quote price/expiry, create a second purchase or invoice charge, or bypass outstanding-payment reconciliation. Replay of a valid ciphertext may still reach the service: require bounded replay handling and the existing reply-slot rules rather than treating HPKE as replay protection. No client-supplied callback address, circuit ID, or decrypted field may turn the reply into arbitrary forwarding; only the admitted relay-owned slot can route the encrypted reply to its recorded circuit.

On trusted detection of request-key compromise, stop admitting that key and authenticate replacement authority independently through the descriptor trust chain. A malicious relay's rekey hint is not trusted revocation and cannot force immediate path changes or unlimited refreshes. Compromise of the request-decryption key can expose recorded requests encrypted to it; rotation and later erasure cannot undo a copy already stolen. It does not by itself supply the client's authorization private key, the service's quote-signing key, or a valid payment. Treat a compromise extending to the host or other keys under the corresponding broader recovery policy. Cached descriptors still allow attempted encryption to a compromised key until refresh/expiry, so immediate revocation or retrospective forward secrecy is not promised. Preserve already accepted terms/accounting and reconcile uncertain funds; do not automatically buy replacement circuits or move recipients during recovery.

**Privacy/timing assessment and review gate:** inner request fields and proofs remain end-to-end encrypted, uniformly padded, and independently re-encrypted across deliveries; ordinary spec-one per-hop payment fields remain unchanged. Introduction relays still know their local registration/routing association and observe timing, length/count conformance, loss, and overload. Rotation overlap, decryption-key selection, stale-cache refresh, and rejected ciphertexts can introduce timing clues; use the existing shared admitted-reply scheduler, bounded retries, guard/isolation rules, and aggregate CPU/state limits. Malformed/unadmitted traffic is not owed unlimited padded responses. Test descriptor/key substitution, invalid or swapped client proofs/reply keys, expired-key arrival and pre-cutoff draining, overlap/resource ceilings, cached descriptors, retries across generations, replay after restart, fixed sizes across methods/key generations, and decryption/reply timing under load. No arbitrary numeric defaults or anonymity claims follow from this draft. The version-one suite is fixed below; complete encodings, suite interoperability, recipient-selection review, vectors, key-storage/erasure and crash policy, implementation, and runtime/privacy measurements remain deferred under [ET-25 and related tasks](../TODO.md).

### Version-one cryptographic profile and binary encoding rules

**Approved specification direction; implementation deferred.** El Tor service version 1 uses one fixed quote-bootstrap profile for both requests and replies. It does not negotiate arbitrary algorithm lists or fall back to a different suite. The profile applies to this bootstrap, not to existing Tor circuit handshakes, relay-payment renewal cryptography, or Lightning signatures.

| Operation | Fixed version-one choice | Size / registered value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HPKE mode | Base mode from [RFC 9180](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9180.html) | `0x00` |
| HPKE KEM | DHKEM(X25519, HKDF-SHA256) | KEM `0x0020`; public key and encapsulated key each 32 bytes |
| HPKE KDF | HKDF-SHA256 | KDF `0x0001` |
| HPKE AEAD | ChaCha20-Poly1305 | AEAD `0x0003`; authentication tag 16 bytes |
| Service signature and client request proof | Pure Ed25519 from [RFC 8032](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8032.html) | Public key 32 bytes; signature 64 bytes |

The HPKE identifiers are existing RFC registrations, not newly assigned El Tor command or payment-method values. Use the standard single-shot HPKE construction with fresh encapsulation for every delivery and a reviewed implementation; do not hand-roll the key schedule or transmit an invented extra AEAD nonce. Pure Ed25519 means neither Ed25519ph nor Ed25519ctx may be silently substituted. Use distinct service-signing, circuit-specific client-signing, service request-decryption, and per-delivery client reply-decryption keys. Never convert/reinterpret Ed25519 key bytes as X25519 keys or reuse a wallet, onion identity, or Tor traffic private key for this profile. Enforce the RFC key/point/signature validity rules and reject malformed inputs through bounded parsing and verification.

**Canonical binary policy:** define each request, reply, delegation, logical quote core, and delivery wrapper with an explicit field-by-field schema and exact order. Fixed integers use declared unsigned widths (`u8`, `u16`, `u32`, or `u64`); multibyte integers use network byte order. Define units and bounds for every amount, duration, timestamp, length, and count, including checked arithmetic and explicit zero semantics. Cryptographic byte strings use raw fixed-size bytes with their declared lengths. Any variable-size field must have one declared length-prefix width, a schema maximum, and one permitted encoding; check its bound before allocation. No native struct layout, optional host endianness, omitted-default encodings, floating-point amounts, duplicate fields, or alternate textual serialization may change the bytes being authenticated. Unknown/unsupported required fields or enum values fail under the version policy. Extensions need an explicitly versioned schema rather than arbitrary trailing bytes.

Sign the exact canonical message bytes under distinct, unambiguous purpose/version domains for client requests and service replies/refusals. Finalize the domain bytes and precisely which record excludes the signature itself before producing vectors; never verify a signature against a parser-normalized alternative representation. Encode logical quote/request digests and full delivery digests from explicitly specified field sets so mutable encryption keys/nonces cannot accidentally alter the stable logical purchase identity. The profile does not silently make an unchanged signed core valid for a new delivery: the fresh delivery wrapper still authenticates the current nonce, reply key, and request digest. Use the existing binding and idempotency rules across retries and key generations.

**Envelope and size accounting:** canonical encode and sign first, place the content length and signature inside the protected message, pad to the common fixed plaintext capacity, then HPKE-encrypt the complete padded plaintext. The ciphertext carries the 32-byte encapsulated key and the AEAD ciphertext including its 16-byte tag. HPKE therefore contributes 48 bytes beyond the padded plaintext, before El Tor routing/fragment framing; a record carrying one Ed25519 signature also consumes 64 bytes inside that plaintext. Delegations, client keys, true-length fields, record framing, optional additional signatures, and transport overhead are additional costs, not included in those two figures. Their complete worst-case inventory is required before selecting fixed capacities and cell counts. Success and authenticated refusal use the same fixed reply capacity and scheduler; both BOLT 11 and BOLT 12 selections use this same profile. Oversize records fail within the existing bounds instead of adding unadvertised fragments or truncating signed terms.

The companion linked below now drafts initial request/reply capacities, field offsets/widths, encrypted length prefix and zero padding, record/method/status values, signature domains, HPKE `info`/AAD bytes, and request/core digest encodings. Relay opcodes, descriptor/delegation/revocation and referenced policy encodings, plus other message types remain explicit work. No interoperable wire implementation is claimed until those are complete and reviewed. Validate the chosen primitives against RFC vectors, then create independent El Tor vectors for exact bytes, signatures, encryption, and rejection cases across implementations.

**Privacy/timing assessment:** one version-wide suite and strict encoding avoid per-wallet/per-service algorithm preferences or alternative serialization becoming new bootstrap signals. Signed identities, client proofs, prices, logical identifiers, and true lengths remain inside the fixed-size encrypted envelopes; no public algorithm inventory or stable key selector is added. Fresh encapsulation, separate keys, existing response/retry scheduling, and shared spec-one payment construction are retained. Crypto/key-selection timing, message fragmentation, traffic correlation, compromised recipient keys, and stale descriptors still require measurement and review. Test wrong suite/mode/signature variant, invalid keys/points/signatures, noncanonical integers and lengths, overflow, truncation/trailing data, changed signed fields/domains, nonce/key reuse, replay, fixed capacities, and resource/timing behavior under load. Only documentation and arithmetic checks are performed here; implementation, independent cryptographic review, interoperability, and runtime/privacy validation remain deferred under [ET-13/14/21/23/24/25](../TODO.md).

### Authenticated payment-policy catalog and reference resolution

**Approved design; implementation deferred.** Publish a small, bounded catalog inside the authenticated encrypted service descriptor. It supplies the records referred to by the initial quote's `funding_policy_ref`, `payment_issuer_ref`, and `acquisition_ref`. Each reference identifies exact canonical content under the intended service and record purpose; a digest alone neither proves authenticity/freshness nor authorizes payment. The client must first verify the descriptor trust chain and the inquiry's capability snapshot, then resolve and understand every referenced record before accepting a quote or permitting payment.

| Catalog entry | Required semantics |
| --- | --- |
| Funding policy | Explicit fee units/rounding and unused-fee treatment; setup versus recurring charges; start/build/round/advance/grace rules; finite spending/outstanding/age/round limits; failure, retry, uncertain-payment and refund rules. An entry may define ranges, but the signed SCQ must give the exact accepted numeric terms within those ranges and the client's caps. |
| Payment issuer | The intended receiving authority and its method-specific authentication/binding rules, scoped to this service. It must be possible to verify an acquired invoice belongs to that authority under the chosen wallet protocol. Do not require a public Lightning node identity or location when the negotiated receiver-hiding method can authenticate the receiver through its supported offer/invoice mechanism. |
| BOLT 11 acquisition | The service-authenticated bounded mechanism for obtaining a fresh invoice, its supported profile, issuer reference and required quote/client/circuit/amount/round association. This is not a reusable BOLT 11 offer or an arbitrary network URL. |
| BOLT 12 acquisition | The exact authenticated offer, issuer reference, supported offer/invoice profile and required quote/client/circuit/amount/round association. Resolve the complete canonical offer bytes through this bounded authenticated metadata, rather than trusting only an offer title, bare hash, or relay-supplied replacement. An offer remains invoice-acquisition information, not payment. |

Use the wire companion's `D` and `L` definitions for a proposed reference construction: `SHA256(D("eltor/scq/v1/catalog-entry") || service_identity[32] || u16be(entry_type) || L(canonical_entry))`. Local draft entry types are `1` funding policy, `2` payment issuer, `3` BOLT 11 acquisition, and `4` BOLT 12 acquisition. Each entry must also bind its schema/profile version and applicable payment method; an acquisition entry explicitly names its issuer reference. Scope/type separation prevents treating identical bytes as another service's policy or a different kind of authority. Exact field-level entry and catalog encodings remain deployment gates under the strict binary profile; never hash native structs, display text, loosely parsed JSON, or an unspecified normalized offer representation. Unsupported schema versions or required semantics fail closed for payment.

Authenticate the catalog and its selection under the descriptor/capability snapshot used for the inquiry. Validate exact content hashes, expected entry types, intended service, method, authority, validity and compatibility with the signed quote. Reject duplicate/conflicting catalog identifiers, mismatched issuer/acquisition associations, and unresolved references. The BOLT 11 acquisition kind can resolve only a BOLT 11 entry, and BOLT 12 only a BOLT 12 entry. Payment-issuer bindings must be verifiable by the actual wallet integration; an encrypted catalog or service signature does not make an unsupported wallet check succeed.

Resolution is bounded local lookup in verified metadata, not arbitrary recursive retrieval. Set finite profile limits on entries, bytes per entry, total catalog bytes, offer bytes, verification work, and reference depth before implementation; catalog work shares the existing descriptor-size, free-bandwidth, cache and CPU budgets. Permitted relationships are quote→funding policy/issuer/acquisition and acquisition→the same issuer; do not follow cyclic or unlimited reference chains. If needed, use only the existing bounded authenticated descriptor-refresh policy and original setup/retry budget. An unknown hash or relay redirect cannot force requests to a supplied host, new guards, unlimited refreshes, descriptor growth, or automatic paid capacity. If the selected snapshot/entries cannot be resolved and understood in time, stop without acceptance or payment.

**Acceptance and updates:** persist the verified canonical entries, original capability snapshot, exact signed quote/core digest, client/circuit bindings and acceptance state with the durable payment ledger. Routine descriptor refresh, catalog replacement, key rotation or entry removal does not rewrite accepted prices, receiver, method, deadlines, fee/refund rules, or balances. New entries apply to future quotes; compatible status/key-authority refresh may refer to the retained accepted snapshot without changing its terms. Before acceptance, expired or invalid metadata requires a bounded fresh agreement or failure, not a silent substitution under the same approval. Known compromise, revocation or loss of independently verifiable authority follows the existing suspension/recovery rules; keeping old terms is not permission to pay a compromised or unverified receiver. Never silently replace a recipient or transfer unsettled funds into a new quote.

**Invoice gate:** the later invoice-acquisition response must authenticate the exact invoice (or its canonical digest), accepted quote/core, service, client authorization, circuit, selected issuer/acquisition references, method, amount, round and validity. The client verifies this association and the wallet's own method-specific invoice/offer authentication before paying, within routing/total/outstanding caps. A BOLT 12 invoice must satisfy the authenticated offer/invoice-request relation; a BOLT 11 invoice must satisfy the service-authenticated acquisition relation and intended receiving authority. Do not assume either standard automatically carries the custom El Tor bindings. Reconcile an existing unresolved invoice/payment before replacing it; the receiver still verifies settlement against its own node and single-redemption ledger. Full invoice-response/acceptance/status wire remains separate design work and cannot silently enlarge the fixed initial quote.

**Privacy/timing assessment:** catalog entries stay in the encrypted descriptor layer and their references inside the existing encrypted fixed-size request/reply design. Service/type-scoped hashes avoid intentional cross-service identifier reuse, and receiver privacy remains optional without requiring a public node identity. Anyone authorized to read a descriptor can still learn the advertised policy/offer, and wallet counterparties may observe their method's data. Catalog size/update cadence, stale-cache refresh and later invoice acquisition can affect observable lengths/timing; preserve Tor descriptor padding and the existing bounded quote response/retry rules, then measure rather than claim indistinguishability. Bound catalog/offer/parser work so new identities or unresolvable hashes cannot create unbounded fetches, state or wallet spend. Test content/type/service/method/issuer substitution, unknown/duplicate/cyclic references, stale/missing entries, unknown required semantics, accepted-term retention through updates, compromised-authority suspension, oversized catalogs/offers, invoice/round substitution and duplicate/uncertain payment handling. Only documentation/link/consistency checks accompany this addition; canonical catalog schemas, runtime support, wallet interoperability, independent review and privacy/resource measurements remain deferred under [ET-27 and related tasks](../TODO.md).

### Quote acceptance and round-specific invoice acquisition

**Approved flow; implementation deferred.** Keep quote receipt, client acceptance, invoice issuance, payment in flight, authoritative settlement, reserved funding, and circuit readiness as separate states. Initial acquisition follows these steps; later rounds reuse the accepted quote without repurchasing it or repeating one-time charges. `SCQ_ACCEPT`, round-invoice request/response and status names here are logical messages, not assigned opcodes or completed wire records. Their own bounded encrypted framing, canonical signatures, sizes and timing rules must be specified before implementation; do not overload the initial request/reply record kinds or enlarge the fixed quote envelope.

1. **Client accepts exact terms.** After validating the quote, signer/delegation, catalog entries and local budget, the client signs an acceptance under the circuit-specific authorization key. Bind the intended service/version, immutable quote-core digest and quote ID, circuit authorization, client key, capability/policy/issuer/acquisition references, selected method, explicit total/outstanding/routing limits, and the requested round (initially 1). A fresh delivery binding protects transport retries; the logical acceptance remains stable. The service verifies that first acceptance is timely under both quote and signer validity, that the client controls the bound key, and that every term matches the exact offered core. Persist acceptance atomically before acknowledging it or issuing an actionable invoice. Acceptance alone buys no ten-round lump, proves no settlement, and does not authorize service-side wallet spending or paid construction.

2. **Acquire one round's payment request.** Authenticate a request for the exact accepted circuit/round and method. For the initial round, compute the invoice amount from that round's relay/routing allowance plus the agreed one-time setup/application charges, within the accepted caps; subsequent invoices exclude those one-time charges. Use checked integer arithmetic, explicit fee treatment and validity/payment windows. For BOLT 11, return the fresh invoice through the bounded service-authenticated mechanism. For BOLT 12, use the accepted offer and method-native invoice-request flow, plus the necessary service-authenticated El Tor association; do not assume the wallet protocol automatically commits to every custom El Tor field. Bind the exact invoice or canonical invoice digest to the accepted core, service, client, circuit, issuer/acquisition references, method, exact amount, round and validity. An invoice-generation callback or introductory relay ACK is not this authentication.

3. **Client verifies, pays, and service reserves.** Before initiating payment, verify the invoice's native authentication and receiver/offer relation through the supported wallet, its signed El Tor association, amount, expiry/round window and all spending/routing/outstanding limits. Persist the outgoing intent and pending commitment before dispatch to the wallet. The service confirms the particular invoice was issued under its accepted ledger mapping and is settled through its own receiving node; verify amount, authoritative timing and any supplied hash/preimage as required by the shared validation rules. Atomically reserve that settled funding for this single circuit/round before paid construction or onward relay payment. No pending, held, client-claimed, or callback-only payment counts as reserved settlement. Mark the authorized paid operation durably before dispatch so concurrent/repeated events cannot start another build or spend the same reservation twice. The first reserved round permits the initial paid build; later reserved rounds fund continuation of the existing circuit.

**Idempotency across failures:** scope logical acceptance by service/client/quote-core/circuit, and invoice/payment attempts by that agreement plus exact round. Keep these keys inside authenticated encryption and independent of hop payment IDs. Duplicate acceptance or acquisition messages recover the same accepted state and existing invoice/payment attempt, without restarting quote/build/round deadlines, changing terms or generating another purchase. Keep one unresolved invoice/payment attempt for each circuit/round; replacement requires authoritative reconciliation of the previous attempt, safe invalidation where supported, and no concurrent claim on its funds. A cancellation, RPC/transport timeout, lost response or restarted app is not proof of payment failure. Never retry a wallet dispatch blindly when the outcome is unknown. Retain enough durable invoice/intent/settlement/reservation/build state for restart recovery, using bounded retention and storage policies that do not erase unresolved financial commitments.

A service may return an authenticated bounded status for the same operation, with fresh delivery encryption and the existing client binding. Acknowledging acceptance or returning an invoice does not mean funded, built, rendezvous-ready or delivered. The normal sequence is offered → accepted → invoice-associated → settled/reserved → build-authorized → ready; pending/unknown/failed/expired states remain explicit, and actual settlement can precede a lost acknowledgment. Transitions must be based on verified durable facts rather than assuming that observed message order is authoritative. Late settlement is reconciled under the original circuit/round policy; it cannot reopen a closed circuit, fund another round, or create cross-circuit credit automatically.

**Subsequent rounds and stopping:** the client requests and verifies the next round's invoice early under the accepted advance-payment schedule, remaining cap and active allocation. It may stop requesting/paying at any round. Do not issue or pay an eleventh-round purchase merely because the original quote was accepted: continuation requires the separate SCQ extension and hop-renewal readiness. Retry does not duplicate setup/application charges, increase the number of prepaid rounds, or renew grace. Stopping cannot undo settled/in-flight payments; preserve the documented unused-current-plus-prepaid-next exposure and reconciliation policy. A successful initial funding operation is not permission for an unfunded automatic rebuild after failure. Explicitly quoted zero-charge operations must follow the mandatory El Tor accounting/authorization rules; do not fabricate a settled Lightning invoice or assume a wallet can process a zero-amount payment.

**Privacy/timing assessment:** acceptance, invoice association and status references remain inside authenticated encrypted, purpose-separated messages, with fresh delivery nonces/keys/ciphertext across retries and no persistent wallet/client identity in relay routing. BOLT 11 and BOLT 12 invoice artifacts may have different sizes and native wallet paths, so the initial quote's equal sizes do not imply equal payment traces. Define a bounded common framing/padding and response-timing policy for service-side control exchanges, while measuring wallet/settlement delay separately; do not force unbounded padding or synchronous wallet RPCs into Tor handlers. Cap outstanding operations, parsing/crypto, invoice creation, queues/status queries and durable state without allowing fresh identities or timeouts to trigger paid work. Test copied/altered acceptances, wrong core/client/receiver/method/amount/round, duplicate concurrent requests/settlement events, one-time fee repetition, out-of-order/lost messages, late settlement, restart after wallet/build dispatch, uncertain outcomes, budget/expiry exhaustion, stop-renewal and no unauthorized next round or rebuild. Only documentation checks accompany this flow; wire/state persistence, wallet integration, independent security/privacy review, and correlated paid runtime measurements remain deferred under [ET-28 and related tasks](../TODO.md).

### Funded build status, client readiness, and setup failure

**Approved behavior; implementation deferred.** Funding, building and end-to-end connectivity are distinct facts. Use these logical milestones and outcomes without treating them as assigned wire enum/opcode values:

| Milestone/outcome | Authority and meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `FUNDED` | The service's own receiving node and durable ledger establish settled funds reserved for the accepted circuit/round. A client payment claim or relay ACK cannot establish it. |
| `BUILDING` | The service durably authorizes and dispatches one bounded paid setup attempt against that reservation. It is not proof that all relays settled payment or the rendezvous succeeded. |
| `JOIN_SENT` | The service reports sending its rendezvous join on the intended circuit. Its authenticated report is not independent proof the client received or completed the rendezvous handshake. |
| Client-connected | The client independently verifies completion of its Tor rendezvous handshake for the intended service and actual circuit/attempt before exposing the connection as ready. This is a client conclusion, not a service status that can override local handshake verification. |
| `FAILED` / `EXPIRED` | A terminal setup outcome for that attempt, under its agreed failure/deadline rules. It stops new automatic paid work while accounting/reconciliation continues; it does not prove every in-flight Lightning operation failed. |

Atomically record build authorization and its dispatch/recovery state before the side effect. Any low-level retry must stay within the same bounded authorized operation and spending limits.

**Authenticated status:** bind every service status to the intended service/version, exact quote/core, client/circuit/attempt, funded round/reservation, status/reason, accounting/deadline snapshot and request/delivery freshness context. Use the valid delegated authority and existing client proof rules; neither a routing handle nor a payment preimage authenticates status. Maintain a durable per-attempt status revision and client high-water record to detect stale/rolled-back snapshots. Fresh queries and their replies use fresh authenticated nonce/reply-key/encryption bindings; a repeated query returns the current known state without resetting attempt identity, spending caps, round/grace clocks or setup expiry. Reconcile source facts even if messages arrive out of order; unknown local observations cannot fabricate terminal failure or successful readiness. Complete canonical status encoding, bounded padded framing, revision/restart behavior and signature domains remain design work, rather than new fields in the fixed initial quote.

**Readiness and deadlines:** the client correlates local handshake completion with the accepted destination/service and active attempt, not merely with receipt of `JOIN_SENT`. Do not display connected because funds settled, a relay delivered an introduction, or the service signed a positive message. Local handshake success establishes that connection, not a throughput/uptime guarantee or independently verified private relay expenditure. Loss of a status reply alone must not rebuild the path, discard a valid established stream, or revive a closed attempt. Apply the setup deadline and triggering conditions agreed in the quote; status delivery, reassembly, polling, retries, cache hits and restart cannot extend them. The first paid-minute start is now specified below: first service-side paid relay-round activation, with setup consuming that minute and independent schedules on both halves. Status/readiness cannot reset this anchor, provide free unbounded service, or reset any relay's actual paid-round deadline.

**Failure after funding:** stop issuing new automatic paid operations when the attempt fails or its setup deadline expires. In-flight payments/controller work may already have side effects; record and reconcile them rather than assuming cancellation undoes settlement. Report authenticated, bounded accounting for received settled funds, confirmed incurred charges, active reservations, unresolved outgoing commitments and any unallocated amount under the original policy. Distinguish overlapping reservation/commitment categories so UI or refund logic does not count the same funds twice. Service reports about private costs remain authenticated claims; the client's wallet records are authoritative for its own outgoing payment/fees, and a status signature does not independently prove honest private-path expenditure.

A failed/expired attempt cannot become ready again because a delayed join/status/payment event appears. Reconcile late facts without reopening it or assigning its funds to another circuit automatically. Refund eligibility and amount follow the accepted funding policy, with separately authenticated destination/fee handling and uncertain operations resolved first. No immediate refund, delivery guarantee or reusable balance is implied. Retain terminal/replay and financial records under bounded retention/recovery policy; deleting a transport slot does not delete them.

**Privacy/timing assessment:** status/attempt/accounting and failure references stay inside independently encrypted authenticated control messages, preserving receiver-choice privacy and the ordinary spec-one hop headers. Do not publish wallet details, private relay identities or raw circuit handles in statuses/logs. Setup milestones, failures, status polling and handshake timing can still reveal progress patterns. Define bounded common padded service-control framing and response scheduling for these messages, including failure states, within original transport/setup deadlines; do not claim the initial quote's fixed sizes already cover them. Cap polls, reply/reassembly/crypto/queue work, event subscriptions, concurrent attempts and ledger growth; unauthenticated relay errors cannot trigger immediate path changes or spending. Validate duplicate/lost/out-of-order status and controller events, wrong quote/client/attempt/round bindings, rollback after restart, forged readiness, failed/late handshake, timeout immediately around settlement/dispatch/join, policy-specific failure accounting, uncertain wallet operations and no automatic rebuild/refund/clock reset. ### Slot lifetime, abuse bounds, and failures

Limit pending slots per client circuit, service registration, connection, and across the relay. Enforce aggregate request/response bandwidth, setup, CPU, queue, and memory ceilings because new circuits or identities can bypass a per-client limit. The service independently caps decrypt/verify work, candidate selection, outstanding quotes/invoices, nonce/replay records, and queues. Use the smaller of applicable path, relay, and service budgets. A legitimate quote can still fail under overload; these bounds do not promise absence of DoS.

Each slot permits one bounded logical reply, using the fixed envelope/cell counts defined below and a strictly limited number of fragments. Fix bytes, cells, reassembly state, and deadline before implementation. Do not assume a complete quote/invoice fits one cell, enlarge the initial onion payload, allocate from an unchecked length, or permit unbounded fragmentation. Include routing/encryption/signature overhead, padding, and both directions in the free accounting. A large authenticated quote still has to fit the advertised budget.

The slot's expiry is absolute from admission and MUST NOT be reset by retries, fragments, acknowledgments, or client activity. Release it on reply completion, timeout, circuit loss, or service registration replacement. Reject late fragments, wrong-service replies, reused handles, and mappings to recycled circuit IDs. Authenticate/reassemble a complete response before acting on it. Bounds must prevent response flooding or using slots as arbitrary third-party forwarding destinations.

Keep transport-slot state distinct from the service's authenticated quote/attempt ledger. An expired slot or rotated introduction circuit does not by itself refund a payment, invalidate an otherwise accepted quote, or authorize a new circuit. Reopening transport requires a fresh slot and authenticated bounded status/retry; existing quote expiry, single redemption, reservations, and uncertain-payment reconciliation still govern. A retransmitted logical request may obtain the cached reply while the quote remains valid, with fresh admitted routing state, but cannot reset its price/expiry or trigger duplicate paid work. An untrusted relay forwarding acknowledgment never permits payment.

For a retry or status request delivered through a fresh route/slot, preserve logical quote idempotency while using a fresh authenticated transport nonce, reply key, and encryption randomness. Cached quote content/authorization is not permission to replay an identical encrypted request or response across different introduction points. Keep stable quote/request references inside the encrypted payload. The final retry envelope must authenticate the new transport binding under the existing client authorization and service identity, without resetting quote validity, funds, or paid-work accounting. Reusing raw cipher state or an AEAD nonce for different plaintext is forbidden. The exact cryptographic construction and retry/status encoding require review before implementation.

The client may retain the eligible introduction circuit within its existing lifetime/resource budget for follow-up or the later paid introduction, or build a new eligible one when needed. Do not require the paid authorization to depend on a reply slot remaining alive while a human approves the quote or Lightning settles. Wallet work remains asynchronous and bounded, outside synchronous Tor cell processing. Exhausting free capacity cannot automatically purchase more capacity from the service's wallet.

### Fixed-size quote envelopes

For service version 1, define one common padded request-envelope size and one common padded reply-envelope size across services. The two sizes need not equal each other. They are version/profile constants, not operator-selected sizes or buckets chosen from actual quote length. All valid quote requests follow the request profile; an admitted quote response and an authenticated service refusal follow the same reply profile.

Serialize the true content length and content inside the end-to-end encrypted envelope, pad to the profile's fixed plaintext length before encryption, and authenticate the padding/length under the final reviewed construction. Use fresh encryption on retries as required above. Only bounded fixed routing/framing fields may remain outside that envelope. Ordinary relay-cell padding alone is insufficient: the introduction endpoint can read relay command length/framing after terminating its Tor layer, so the forwarded ciphertext length must also be constant.

Define a fixed cell count and fixed per-fragment payload lengths for each envelope profile, including the last fragment. A short quote/refusal must not result in fewer cells or a shorter last fragment. Account for encryption tags, signatures, nonce/key material, routing handles, fragment headers, and normal relay padding. Normal congestion/flow-control/padding behavior still applies; fixed logical message cell counts do not promise an identical total network trace.

Before assigning numeric sizes, inventory every required field's maximum encoded length under the selected canonical encoding, calculate cryptographic and routing overhead, then choose fixed request/reply capacities and fragment counts fitting the supported cell format. Keep SCQs compact and bounded. Arbitrarily long descriptions, extra terms, or full wallet invoices must not silently expand a quote; invoice acquisition remains its separately specified bounded method/exchange. The initial byte/cell values are now derived in the initial wire section; they do not set measured free-capacity or latency parameters.

Check size before sending. If a request cannot fit, fail locally without sending an oversized request. If a service cannot produce a conforming quote, it may send an authenticated fixed-size refusal within its admitted slot/budget, rather than adding fragments or truncating terms. Receivers reject oversized, truncated, extra, or inconsistent framing with bounded allocation. Treat all advertised limits as ceilings; they do not authorize a larger service-specific envelope.

Relay admission failures, malformed traffic, slot exhaustion, circuit loss, and timeouts are separate transport outcomes. They cannot be disguised as authenticated service quotes, and this rule does not require unlimited cover replies or extra free capacity under attack. Where a valid admitted service reply is produced, success/refusal content remains inside the same padded envelope. Unavailable or nonconforming transport fails within existing budgets.

Validate the profile using minimum/maximum legal quotes, unequal relay rates, supported payment methods, authentication material, service refusals, and permitted retries. Compare exposed envelope lengths, fragment lengths/counts, and last-fragment sizes at introduction endpoints; ensure true lengths and stable references remain encrypted. Test oversized inputs and free-budget/reassembly exhaustion. Constant nominal size reduces content-length signals but does not hide message timing, missing replies, protocol activity, or relationships already visible to the introduction relay. Apply the shared response-timing policy below and review its interaction with Tor circuit-padding machines. Track this work in ET-23 and ET-21, with no implementation claimed.

**Privacy/timing assessment:** this change targets observable ciphertext lengths and nominal fragment/cell counts at introduction endpoints and along the path. It preserves independent identifiers, fresh retry encryption, encrypted true lengths, and the existing introduction relay's known circuit relationships. Transport failure, congestion, missing replies, and timing can still differ. Numeric capacities, cryptographic framing, scheduling implementation/measurements, padding-machine interaction, and empirical correlation tests remain deferred; only documentation consistency/link checks have been performed for this change.

### Shared response timing

For valid admitted quote requests, fresh quotes, cached quote results, and authenticated service refusals MUST use the same bounded response-scheduling policy for service version 1. A cache hit must not bypass the scheduler and produce an immediate response merely because the result is already available. The policy is shared by the version/profile, not selected to reveal a service, amount, wallet method, request type, or cache outcome. It does not require waiting for a Lightning payment or exposing wallet RPC latency in the quote reply.

The intended design chooses a randomized target release time within a shared bounded response window, anchored to the locally defined admission of a complete valid request and independent of which result is obtained. Schedule computation, authentication, and fresh encryption within that budget. Simply adding a random delay after computation does not make fresh/cached response-time distributions equal; measured computation, queueing, and transport effects must inform the final window. No numeric window or delay distribution is selected here. Avoid globally synchronized release ticks; the final definition must specify its local start event, randomness, and safe time arithmetic.

The target and all fragments must fit the remaining reply-slot, quote-validity, setup, queue, and free-resource limits, with bounded allowance for transmission. Do not extend slot/quote expiry, reset funding deadlines, create extra paid work, or purchase capacity to accommodate a delay. If a result cannot be produced or sent safely in time, use a conforming authenticated refusal only where the admitted budget permits; otherwise let the bounded transport attempt fail. Do not send a quote already expired at release, weaken authentication, or invent paid authorization to fill a response window.

Timers, delayed replies, encryption work, and outstanding request state count against aggregate memory/CPU/queue/concurrency budgets. Malformed/unadmitted requests, circuit loss, and overload remain the separate bounded transport outcomes described above; no unlimited padding or delayed-response state is required under attack. Preserve the fixed-size quote/refusal envelope and fresh per-delivery encryption. Fragment pacing and integration with existing Tor congestion/padding behavior require separate measurement; one local target release time does not control the entire path's timing.

**Privacy/timing assessment:** the policy targets the extra cache/new/refusal timing signal visible to an introduction relay or observer comparing attempts. The introduction endpoint still knows its own routed request/reply association; network delay, congestion, processing overruns, missing replies, and active relay delaying/dropping can remain observable. Test distributions for fresh/cached/refused requests under load, timer/queue limits, fragment completion before expiry, and interaction with Tor padding and deadline enforcement. Random delay is not proof of indistinguishability or protection against an observer watching both ends. Only documentation checks have run; scheduling implementation, parameter measurements, and traffic-correlation tests remain deferred under ET-23/21. Client retry/failover timing is defined separately below and must remain compatible with this service response rule.

### Bounded client retry and failover timing

Before sending a quote request, set a monotonic local delivery deadline that accommodates the advertised common response window, bounded request/response transmission including all fragments, and a measured/configured allowance for network delay. Keep an overall setup deadline as well. A forwarding acknowledgment is not a quote or evidence the service has finished processing. Receiving partial fragments or an untrusted progress/error message MUST NOT reset these absolute deadlines. If there is insufficient setup/slot budget for a normal complete attempt, stop rather than intentionally timing it out early.

On a retryable timeout or transport failure, retire the current local delivery attempt and wait a bounded randomized backoff before another delivery. Maintain at most one active local delivery attempt per logical quote inquiry; do not deliberately send parallel inquiries to every introduction point. An old packet/service computation may still be in flight after local timeout, so serialization is not a substitute for service-side replay/idempotency checks. Count attempts across all introduction points, replacement circuits, descriptor refreshes, and automatic status retries against the same finite retry/setup budget; changing the route or transport nonce does not restart the counter. Choose numeric timeout, retry-count, and backoff parameters only after measurements.

Select backoff locally under the common profile, within the remaining setup deadline and resource limits. Do not retry immediately at an attacker-chosen time because a relay sent an unauthenticated error, redirect, proposed delay, or premature forwarding failure. Even an authenticated service hint cannot override local retry/deadline/budget policy or prescribe a uniquely identifying retry schedule. A valid terminal quote refusal, unsupported version/method, expired authorization, or budget exhaustion is not permission for an automatic retry loop. Existing authenticated stale-capability refresh remains bounded and consumes the same setup budget.

Retry only through eligible introduction points obtained from the authenticated service descriptor. Preserve normal Tor guard selection and stream/client isolation; a quote timeout or change of introduction point MUST NOT itself trigger guard rotation, a persistent new client identity, or merging isolated activity. Do not silently change service, network, protocol, payment method, proposed rendezvous destination, accepted terms, or spending authorization. A genuine change to quote-bound terms requires the appropriate fresh agreement, without reusing committed funds or evading retry limits.

Keep the same encrypted logical inquiry/quote reference and client authorization binding for idempotency, but give each delivery a fresh transport nonce, reply-encryption key, admitted relay-local handle, and encryption randomness under the authenticated retry envelope above. Never expose a shared logical inquiry ID in routing fields or reuse ciphertext across introduction points. A fresh route must return existing valid quote state or a bounded authenticated result, not reset quote price/expiry, duplicate reservations, create another paid build, or buy a new round.

Accept a response for further action only if its transport binding matches the current active delivery and its authenticated quote/version/client/nonce/terms remain valid. A response from an abandoned or timed-out slot MUST NOT reactivate that attempt, automatically accept/pay a quote, extend the setup deadline, or create competing quote acceptances. A later authenticated status query can recover existing quote/payment state under the same bounded rules. A transport timeout is not proof a Lightning payment failed, a refund, or release of uncertain funds; any existing payment commitments retain their separate durable reconciliation rules.

**Privacy/timing assessment:** sequential attempts, local bounded backoff, fresh encrypted retry envelopes, and unchanged guard/isolation rules reduce additional deterministic failover and ciphertext-matching signals. The introduction relay still sees its own request/response routing, and malicious delaying/dropping, congestion, and an observer comparing both ends can still correlate activity. Test loss and delayed/partial replies, expired slots, late valid quotes after failover, unauthenticated forced-retry/redirect hints, deliberately patterned relay delays, retry-counter bypass, cache idempotency, guard stability, isolation, fixed envelope sizes, and all setup/free/queue/spending budgets. Numeric values, runtime support, and measured privacy effectiveness remain deferred under ET-23/21; documentation checks alone establish none of those properties.

### Privacy, remaining wire work, and validation

Preserve the same per-hop payment fields and padding as other El Tor circuits, including dummy zero-fee fields where applicable. The introduction endpoint necessarily sees its registered service circuit and the client circuit it forwards for; the reply feature does not make those relationships invisible. Keep prices, invoices, client keys, requested rendezvous details, and authorization inside the end-to-end encrypted payload. Do not expose a persistent wallet identity or shared cross-hop payment/renewal token for routing. Fresh reply keys and request nonces prevent adding a reusable client handle; timing/size correlation still needs measurement.

Before implementation, specify descriptor capability/delegation encoding, request/reply encryption and authentication, canonical byte layouts, opcode/direction rules, handle generation/binding, fixed limits, fragmentation/reassembly, timeout/error behavior, replay/idempotency, and bounded follow-up invoice/status messages. This section records the approved transport architecture, not a completed wire protocol or crypto review. Numeric budgets must be measured with real quote/invoice sizes and the actual cell format.

Validate encrypted quote round trips with no new service circuit or wallet spending; wrong-key/version/nonce/capability/RP/quote substitution; unsupported endpoints; duplicate/replayed requests; fabricated/wrong-service return handles; stale/recycled circuits; delayed/lost/oversized/fragmented replies; queue/slot floods; and free-budget exhaustion. Verify human approval and late payment remain independent of slot lifetime, and settlement still precedes paid service construction. Implementation is deferred under ET-13/14 and the dedicated reply-transport task ET-23 in the [El Tor TODO](../TODO.md).

## El Tor service version agreement

### Required payment protocol and strict version matching

This protocol is for El Tor clients and El Tor onion services. Payment handling is fundamental, including zero-fee handshakes and traffic within the bounded free allowance, which still use the uniform construction in spec one. A zero charge does not disable payment authorization or remove payment fields. There is no `payment_required=false` negotiation and no requirement to support ordinary Tor clients or previous El Tor versions.

Start with one explicitly implemented **El Tor service protocol version, 1**, on both endpoints. This number identifies the client/service agreement, not an application release, Tor handshake type, BOLT number, or relay command opcode. A version denotes a complete, specified set of quote, authorization, funding, round, and continuation semantics; it must not mean a partially implemented assortment of features. It is a proposed version label, not a claim that those encodings or implementations are complete today.

The service advertises the versions it explicitly supports in its authenticated, encrypted service-descriptor metadata, alongside accepted payment methods and the authenticated quote-bootstrap information already required above. Keep this metadata inside the protected service information; do not add a public service-role or wallet marker to the descriptor's outer wrapper or intermediate relays' payment payloads. Exact descriptor extension encoding remains wire-format work. Missing, malformed, or unknown El Tor metadata is an error, not evidence of optional payment or permission to use ordinary Tor.

For the initial release, both endpoints support version 1 or the attempt fails. There is no backward-compatibility obligation. A future endpoint may deliberately implement and advertise more than one complete service version, but nothing promises that it will. Do not use an application build/version string to decide protocol compatibility.

### Agreement before a payable quote

1. **Read and validate the advertisement.** The client verifies the service descriptor and its normal validity/revision rules on the intended El Tor network. Parse bounded, canonical metadata: explicit supported service versions, required features/subprotocol versions, accepted payment methods, and authenticated quote-bootstrap information. Initial deployments advertise only service version 1. Unknown optional fields may be ignored only under a defined encoding rule; an unknown required feature makes the attempt incompatible.
2. **Choose locally.** The client selects the highest service version that it implements, the authenticated service advertises, and local policy permits. Initially that can only be 1. Choose a mutually supported payment method under the receiver-choice policy separately. A BOLT version is not a service-protocol version or a privacy guarantee. If no complete permitted match exists, stop before requesting a payable invoice or starting new connection-specific paid work.
3. **Bind the request.** Through the bounded authenticated bootstrap, send the selected service version, a fresh client nonce, the existing fresh client authorization public key, the chosen payment method, and a digest of the authenticated capability block the client relied on. The digest must cover the canonical versions, required features, methods, and bootstrap delegation/identity, bound to this service and descriptor revision. Do not send the client's complete software/wallet capability list, app build, persistent wallet identity, or a shared transport-hop identifier merely to negotiate.
4. **Confirm in the SCQ.** The service verifies the request against the advertised snapshot it still accepts and returns the selected version, client nonce, capability-block digest, client-key/method binding, and its normal SCQ terms in the authenticated quote. The client verifies an exact match before accepting the quote or paying. This reuses the SCQ response rather than adding a separate mandatory negotiation round trip. A server response selecting another version is not permission to downgrade.
5. **Keep the agreement fixed.** Bind quote acceptance, invoice association, access authorization, status/retry requests, round accounting, and SCQ continuation to the accepted service version and quote. Existing circuit/round and replay rules continue to apply. Version/descriptor changes cannot silently reinterpret an accepted circuit, change its price, or move settled funds into a different protocol. A different service version needs a new explicit agreement and the applicable reconciliation rules.

Version agreement is not quote-price acceptance, relay admission, settlement, or a delivery guarantee. Discovery and negotiation must fit the bounded free bootstrap; an incompatible or unpaid requester cannot cause the service to build paid circuits or spend wallet funds. Locally accepted activity on other circuits is not a new authorization for this attempt.

The final bootstrap and SCQ wire formats must define the canonical capability-block bytes, digest domain, nonce length, authentication/delegation, payload limits, and replay handling. Those encodings remain deferred design work in ET-13/14/19; this section defines their required semantics without inventing an unauthenticated preliminary handshake.

### Required features and hop-level protocols

Service version agreement and per-hop capability/admission checks are separate. The client checks its own relay path; the service checks its private path and commits to compatible construction through its quote/readiness rules. Do not disclose private relay identities or send the end-to-end capability digest, SCQ ID, nonce, or client key to every transport hop as a common renewal token.

A service profile must identify which relay-payment, bounded-free bootstrap, authorization, quote, and continuation subprotocols it requires. A required feature must be supported before paid work relying on it begins. In particular, service version 1 does not magically imply support for the proposed per-hop renewal wire version 1: the requisite relay advertisement and authenticated admission are still needed. If the agreed profile requires renewal, an unsupported hop is a failed path, not permission to silently substitute a forced ten-minute disconnect. Any explicitly allowed finite, non-renewing behavior must be part of the accepted profile/terms, not a downgrade invented after payment. Finalizing the complete version-1 feature profile is an implementation gate.

### Mismatch, stale metadata, and errors

Use clear local outcomes: `ELTOR_VERSION_MISMATCH`, `ELTOR_REQUIRED_FEATURE_UNSUPPORTED`, `ELTOR_PAYMENT_METHOD_UNSUPPORTED`, and `ELTOR_CAPABILITIES_STALE`. These are proposed semantic outcomes, not assigned relay command values or finalized bootstrap error encodings. Explain the mismatch in the app without implying that changing wallet privacy settings or paying anyway will repair an unsupported service version.

An authenticated stale-capability response may cause at most one descriptor refresh and a fresh bounded agreement attempt, within existing setup/rate limits and without a wallet charge. Recompute selection from the verified replacement metadata; never interpret a timeout, missing field, unauthenticated error, or malformed message as an authenticated downgrade request. A peer still incompatible after that attempt fails cleanly. A service may briefly honor a cached advertised snapshot only when its complete semantics, keys, validity, and price commitment remain supported; it must not falsely claim old-version support to accept payment.

There is no automatic fallback to ordinary Tor, an unadvertised older El Tor version, a different payment method, a reusable payment session, or a mode that skips payment handling. The authenticated version advertisement and exact quote binding protect negotiation from simple substitution; they do not prevent a malicious service from lying about what it implements, withholding service, or presenting stale but still valid metadata. Retain the existing payment-exposure and freshness limits.

### Validation and implementation status

Test version-1 matching, no common version, missing/invalid metadata, unknown required features, unsupported wallet methods, manipulated version/digest/nonce/method fields, substituted quotes/invoices, replayed requests, stale descriptors, bounded refresh/retry, and descriptor changes during an accepted circuit. Check that unsupported requests cause no new paid service-side work, no attempted connection-specific payment, no silent fallback, and no cross-circuit funding reuse. Verify per-hop readiness separately and measure negotiation sizes/traffic without exposing a persistent client fingerprint.

Implementation remains deferred. [ET-19 in the El Tor TODO](../TODO.md#compatibility-app-behavior-and-validation) tracks this agreement, with bootstrap/SCQ encoding in ET-13/14, app behavior in ET-20, and version/renewal testing in ET-21. Documentation checks do not demonstrate a deployed compatible client or service.

## Revision requirements

These four requirements retain the descriptor, invoice, introduction, and rendezvous architecture while defining its authorization and economic boundaries. They are proposed requirements; exact encodings and interoperability rules must be specified before implementation.

### 1. Bind payment authorization to the client

The service MUST verify payment against its own Lightning node's records. It must confirm the expected invoice or transaction, service association, offer where applicable, amount, terms, and settled status. If a preimage is presented, its SHA-256 hash MUST match that invoice's payment hash. A pending payment, an invoice issued by another recipient, or a matching hash/preimage pair without a settled local invoice MUST NOT authorize access.

The Lightning node is authoritative for settlement; the service's durable ledger is authoritative for access redemption within the agreed window. An invoice can remain settled after its access allowance has been used, so checking settled status alone does not prevent replay.

A hash identifies an invoice, and a preimage demonstrates payment knowledge; neither identifies the connecting client. Before payment, the service MUST bind the invoice to a fresh client authorization public key through an authenticated invoice/quote exchange. The client proves possession of the corresponding private key when requesting access. This is a separate authorization key unless a reviewed construction explicitly defines safe reuse of a Tor or Lightning key. The protocol must specify how the binding is carried and authenticated; an arbitrary payer note is insufficient.

The signed authorization MUST cover the protocol version/domain, service identity, invoice identifier, opaque circuit authorization identifier, round number, agreed terms, expiry, unique attempt identifier, rendezvous relay and cookie, and client handshake material. This prevents moving an authorization to a different service, circuit, or round. The service MUST reject expired authorizations and atomically prevent duplicate redemption. A pre-build authorization identifier must be bound to the resulting circuit once it is created; its exact encoding remains to be specified. Retry and refund rules below do not grant reusable access across circuits.

Use a fresh authorization identity for each new paid circuit rather than a reusable wallet/node identity. Keep payment authorization inside the service-encrypted introduction payload. The final compact encoding MUST fit Tor's introduction payload constraints; do not insert an entire invoice or an unbounded proof. Advertise support and version requirements so unsupported clients do not pay for an extension they cannot use.

### 2. Define who funds each circuit

Paying the service for access and paying transport relays are distinct charges. The client funds the variable costs of its connection one circuit round at a time. The service maintains its introduction circuits within the bounded free allowance below; exceeding that allowance MUST NOT silently charge the operator. Descriptor publication and retrieval use the proposed bounded free discovery allowance below. Optional paid capacity beyond that baseline requires explicit payer approval and a spending cap.

| Activity | Payer and budget |
| --- | --- |
| Service-to-introduction-point circuits | Free within advertised limits at every hop, including bounded setup and replacement work. |
| Descriptor publication, storage, and client retrieval | Proposed bounded free discovery service, covering path traffic and HSDir resource limits; any optional paid extras require explicit approval and caps. |
| Client introduction circuit | May use the same purpose-independent free allowance where supported and sufficient. |
| Client-to-rendezvous circuit | Client pays its chosen relays in one-minute rounds. |
| Service-to-rendezvous circuit | Service pays its privately selected relays using this client's funding for the corresponding circuit and round. |
| Application access, setup fees, and wallet/routing fees | Explicitly disclosed in the quote, separately from transport allowance. |

The descriptor lists introduction points, not a permanent rendezvous point. The client selects the rendezvous relay for an attempt. The service retains private selection and payment of its own path; the client MUST NOT need a list of service-side relays to fund access. Onion-service traffic has no exit to the public Internet. Relay charging details remain governed by [the paid circuit proposal](01_paid_circuits.md), with endpoint-specific rules still to be defined.

Paid circuits retain spec one's one-minute payment rounds and ten-round allocations, with explicitly bounded timing padding for Lightning routing and settlement. Extending a paid circuit past the initial allocation requires the renewal procedure below. The client pays its own side and funds the service's side one round ahead. Each funding payment MUST be bound to one circuit and round and MUST NOT authorize another circuit or round. Before payment, the authenticated Service Circuit Quote (SCQ) MUST specify the fixed round price, setup or handshake charges, acceptance expiry, padding/deadline rules, fee limits, and failure/refund policy. The service MUST reserve settled round funding before relay payments, including any costs of the agreed padding. A pending or held Lightning payment is not settled funding. This is circuit-specific payment accounting, not a reusable payment session or shared balance across circuits. Without successful renewal, expiration requires a replacement circuit with a new authorization and payment sequence. An admitted continuation stays bound to the existing circuit and does not transfer its funding elsewhere.

### 3. Specify failures, retries, and round accounting

Payment settlement and Tor rendezvous completion are separate events. A successful Lightning payment MUST NOT be represented as a successful connection. Maintain a durable circuit/round ledger recording invoice settlement, bound client key, circuit authorization identifier, round number, terms, expiry, reservations, actual charges, and consumed payment identifiers. Each attempt has a unique identifier, bounded reservation, deadline, and lifecycle: reserved, building, active, then closed or failed.

Concurrent introductions, duplicate wallet notifications, retries, and restarts MUST NOT create duplicate funding, reservations, circuits, or payments. Persist reservations and outgoing-payment identifiers before starting paid work. Duplicate requests must refer to the existing attempt rather than launch another one. After a timeout or restart, reconcile uncertain outgoing payments and circuit state before releasing a reservation or retrying a payment.

If rendezvous fails, record setup or relay costs already incurred under the agreed terms and reconcile the unused part of that attempt's reserved funding. An exact retransmission is idempotent and MUST NOT create another paid circuit or charge. A fresh attempt for an unestablished circuit requires an authenticated retry decision, fresh attempt details, and atomic rebinding of only demonstrably unspent funding; the old authorization must become unusable. Replacement of an established or expired circuit starts a new payment sequence. Do not automatically carry unused rounds to another circuit or grant unlimited free rebuilds. A failed attempt may have consumed real relay fees.

The protocol still needs an authenticated status/retry mechanism, a definition of successful connection establishment, and an explicit unused-funding/refund policy. Refunding unused funding is a separate Lightning operation requiring authenticated destination handling and its own fee policy; it does not establish a reusable payment session. Do not interpret introduction delivery acknowledgment as payment acceptance, completed rendezvous, or delivered application data.

### 4. Cap spending and keep wallet work off the handshake path

An unpaid, malformed, expired, or replayed request MUST NOT cause the service to initiate a paid rendezvous circuit, relay payment, or unlimited automatic top-up. Before any outgoing payment, enforce both the circuit round's remaining reserved budget and the operator's wallet-wide limits, including routing fees, outstanding payment commitments, and concurrent attempts. Renewal or rebuild costs must also fit these limits. Missing round funding or exhausted limits stop further paid work. When a required payment misses its bounded deadline/grace period, close the paid circuit; it MUST NOT silently fall back to the free allowance.

Standing infrastructure needs separate protection: the bounded free introduction allowance below MUST NOT trigger operator wallet spending when it is exhausted. Any separately paid descriptor publication or hosting needs explicit spending caps; unsolicited introductions or descriptor retrievals MUST NOT create unbounded charges. Apply introduction rate limits, bounded invoice issuance and queues, and bounded retention of unsettled requests. Standard Tor introduction DoS controls can help limit forwarding; they do not themselves enforce a financial budget. Hitting a cap may reduce availability but MUST NOT silently increase spending.

A background wallet component synchronizes settled invoices and reconciles outgoing payments into the durable ledger. The Tor handshake reads local authorization state and reserves circuit/round funding without waiting on wallet network RPCs. If settlement is not yet synchronized, defer or reject the attempt without spending; allow a later authenticated retry. If wallet state is stale or unavailable, do not authorize new spending until it can be reconciled. Payment, replay, and reservation state required to prevent overspending must survive process restarts.

These measures bound financial exposure; they do not eliminate CPU, bandwidth, liquidity, or availability attacks. Tor encryption and Lightning blinded paths also do not eliminate payment timing/amount correlation. The final protocol needs a privacy review as well as spending-limit validation.

## Service Circuit Quote (SCQ)

### Definition and calculation

A **Service Circuit Quote (SCQ)** is the onion service's authenticated quote to the client for funding its **service-to-rendezvous circuit**. The client separately selects and pays the relays on its own circuit to the same rendezvous point. An initial SCQ covers one service-side circuit for at most ten one-minute rounds, paid incrementally rather than all ten rounds upfront. Continuing that same circuit requires an authenticated, explicitly authorized SCQ extension under the renewal rules below. It is not a reusable payment session or a balance across circuits.

Relay operators set their own prices in their relay descriptors, as in spec one. The service reads those descriptors, selects a compatible candidate path, and totals its cost. It does not override relay rates or assume they are equal. Payment intervals and capabilities must be compatible with the quoted schedule. Candidate selection and quoting do not authorize paid construction or relay payments.

The fixed per-minute amount includes the combined selected relay rates and an explicitly disclosed service-side Lightning routing-fee budget. One-time relay handshake/setup charges are itemized separately and MUST NOT recur every round. Specify fee limits, integer units such as millisatoshis, any rounding, and how unused reserved fee amounts are treated. A separate application/access charge, if any, must be identified and accepted separately from the transport total rather than hidden in relay costs.

The client can display its own selected-path costs alongside the SCQ to show the complete connection budget. Its own wallet routing fees remain subject to its own fee limits; do not silently count them again as service-side charges. The service need not reveal its relay identities or full path. An SCQ authenticates the service's price commitment, not an independent proof of its private relay costs, path selection, or delivered bandwidth.

### Descriptor advertisement and required terms

The onion-service descriptor advertises accepted payment methods, SCQ capability, and how to obtain an authenticated quote. Generate an SCQ for the intended connection from current candidate relay prices. It need not be uploaded to HSDirs or trigger descriptor republication for every connection or round. Discovery metadata and cached descriptors are not substitutes for a current accepted quote.

The exact wire format remains open. Authenticated terms MUST include:

| Field or term | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Protocol version and unique quote identifier | Identify this agreement and avoid confusing quotes or versions. |
| Version-agreement binding | Confirm the selected El Tor service version, fresh client nonce, authenticated descriptor capability-block digest, and client-key/payment-method selection before quote acceptance or payment. |
| Service identity and client authorization binding | Authenticate the intended service and bind acceptance to a fresh client authorization key without requiring its public wallet identity. |
| Opaque circuit authorization identifier and rendezvous relay | Bind the quote to the intended service-side circuit/destination without disclosing its private path. |
| Fixed per-round amount, unit, and breakdown | State the selected relay-rate total and disclosed service-side routing-fee budget for each 60-second round. |
| One-time charges and wallet fee limits | Itemize setup/handshake costs and bound all authorized extra spending. |
| Authorized period, maximum rounds, total cost, and outstanding commitments | Initially at most ten rounds/ten minutes; any continuation needs an accepted extension. Include setup/padding costs and bound cumulative spending and how much may be reserved, prepaid, or in flight at once. |
| Acceptance expiry and round/build schedule | Distinguish the deadline to accept from the lifetime of the accepted fixed price; specify lead time, grace, build timeout, and start conditions. |
| Payment method and invoice association | Bind each payable invoice/payment identifier to this accepted SCQ, circuit, and specific round. |
| Failure, retry, and unused-funding rules | Disclose permitted incurred costs and reconciliation/refund handling without automatic cross-circuit credit or unlimited rebuilds. |

Authenticate the SCQ as belonging to the onion service through its descriptor-authenticated identity or delegated signing key. The exact signing/delegation scheme and domain-separated serialization remain to be defined. Requests, replies, and acceptance need a bounded authenticated bootstrap exchange available before the paid service-side circuit exists. A quote receipt, a client acceptance, an issued invoice, and settlement are separate states.

### Fixed price and acceptance sequence

Once accepted within its validity window, the per-minute price is fixed for that circuit's agreed lifetime. Paying incrementally does not permit repricing each minute. Changed relay descriptors or service metadata cannot alter an accepted SCQ. The renewal draft preserves the accepted per-minute price for the continuing circuit; changing prices at renewal boundaries remains an open decision. The client may reject the quote, or stop renewing an accepted circuit under the existing performance/payment rules; acceptance does not commit the client to purchasing all ten rounds.

The proposed sequence is: request SCQ, select candidate relays and calculate cost, authenticate and deliver the quote, client accepts or declines, obtain and settle initial funding, then construct/pay the service-side circuit. The client may prepare its own rendezvous circuit independently. The service MUST NOT incur paid work until the agreed initial funding is settled and reserved. Before requesting payment, decline if no workable candidate path fits the quoted price.

Each underlying relay MUST honor the rate accepted when its circuit is established for that circuit's lifetime. This commitment requires relay negotiation and enforcement; publishing a descriptor alone does not reserve a price or capacity. If a cached rate is stale or a relay refuses the terms, use an equivalent path within the accepted budget/terms or follow the disclosed failure/requote policy. Never silently raise an accepted price or spend beyond the circuit's settled funding. A quote is not a guarantee that capacity has been reserved or that a build will succeed.

Record the accepted SCQ in the circuit/round ledger and bind every distinct subsequent invoice/payment to it. Duplicate quote requests or acceptance messages MUST NOT create extra circuits, duplicate reservations, or duplicate charges. Quote changes require fresh identifiers and explicit acceptance; old and replacement authorizations must not claim the same funds concurrently. Circuit replacement starts a new SCQ/payment sequence. Continuing the existing circuit past round ten requires an accepted SCQ extension and relay renewal; neither quote acceptance nor elapsed time automatically buys an eleventh round.

### Incremental payments, stopping, and accepted loss exposure

From the client's perspective, there are two separately funded circuit halves. The client pays its own selected relays to the rendezvous point, and separately pays the service's SCQ through Lightning. The service spends that SCQ funding on its own selected relays. These payments are incremental purchases of circuit rounds, not a single upfront purchase of the entire ten-minute connection. Initial service-side funding covers the first round and explicitly agreed setup fees.

The client may stop buying further rounds at any time, including because performance is inadequate or the connection is no longer needed. Accepting an SCQ fixes the price of any rounds purchased within its lifetime; it does not obligate the client to buy all ten. If the client purchases rounds one through three and does not fund round four, required-payment enforcement closes the affected circuit at the applicable deadline/grace. If round four was already prepaid, choosing to stop does not undo that settled payment or automatically entitle the client to its refund.

A relay or service may fail, disconnect, or cease delivering traffic at any time, even after payment. The protocol cannot force an uncooperative peer to remain online or deliver bandwidth, and a receipt/accepted SCQ is not an uptime or delivery guarantee. Nonpayment closure follows the agreed deadline/grace; unrelated failure or disconnection can happen earlier. The client/service can stop further renewal and reconcile outstanding work when either circuit half fails. This availability risk does not waive the price, authorization, spending-limit, or agreed failure-accounting rules.

**Accepted tradeoff:** incremental payment bounds prepayment exposure but does not make it zero or guarantee a refund. The client may lose amounts already paid for service that is not subsequently delivered. With the candidate 60-second lead time, the current round can still have unused time while the next round is prepaid or in flight. Exposure can therefore include the unused portion of the current round plus the prepaid next round, agreed setup charges, and routing fees. It is not strictly limited to one minute's fees.

The client-side relay payments and SCQ payments each contribute to the client's total budget and exposure. Service-to-relay payments funded by the SCQ must not be counted again as a second client charge. Enforce the disclosed outstanding-funding cap across both current/next-round commitments and unreconciled attempts; retries, overlapping builds, or restarts MUST NOT multiply that cap. Do not release uncertain in-flight funds merely because the client closed the connection. The existing reconciliation/refund policy governs confirmed remaining funds and actual agreed costs; it does not promise recovery from an uncooperative recipient.

### Security and exposure limits

Protect quote, recipient, and invoice binding together: a valid service quote must not be usable with a substituted invoice, amount, destination, circuit, or round. Invoice/payment-method verification must authenticate the intended receiver without imposing a universally public wallet identity. Reject expired or incompatible quotes before payment, and reconcile payments that nevertheless settle late under the existing failure rules. Exact signing and binding formats need validation before implementation.

Rate-limit quote requests, candidate selection, invoice issuance, and outstanding quote state using bounded per-request and aggregate budgets. An unpaid requester must not trigger paid circuits or unbounded memory/CPU work. Many fresh client keys must not evade the service's global limits. Client-supplied rendezvous details still require normal Tor eligibility/path checks and authenticated binding to the accepted quote.

A client receiving a quote for a private path cannot independently verify its component relay prices without additional disclosure or a separate reviewed proof mechanism. Treat the total as a service-authenticated price offer which the client may reject. Do not claim that the quote proves honest pricing, relay performance, successful delivery, or automatic fair exchange.

Keep uncertain in-flight payments reserved until reconciled, including after circuit closure or a request to re-quote. Refund/unused-funding claims require client authorization bound to the original quote, confirmed remaining funds, and bounded refund fees; a retry or refund request must not redirect someone else's funds. Detailed refund mechanics remain open, and the SCQ does not promise an unconditional refund of already incurred agreed costs.

### Shared construction and validation

Carry SCQ negotiation and compact references in the authenticated client/service exchange. Preserve spec one's unchanged per-hop construction, unique per-hop round identifiers, dummy zero-fee handshake fields, encryption, and equal-size padding. Do not add an onion-service purpose marker to intermediate relays' payment payloads. A full SCQ need not fit in one introduction cell; define bounded delivery and appropriately sized authenticated references before choosing an encoding.

Validate unequal relay prices, fee caps/rounding, price changes, stale descriptors, quote rejection/expiry, substituted invoices, destination/client binding, duplicate acceptances, replay, bounded quote floods, and loss of connectivity during the exchange. Check that existing circuits retain accepted rates, failures cannot overspend settled circuit funds, all simultaneous commitments stay within the cap, and clients can approve a complete budget without learning the service's path. Include stopping after round three, stopping after the next round was already prepaid, peer disconnection immediately after payment, and reconciling both circuit halves without double-counting the SCQ's onward relay payments.

SCQ encoding/authentication, bootstrap delivery, relay rate commitment, exact build/start events, and unused-fee/refund mechanics remain implementation-level design work. This section records the agreed architecture and its open security checks, not a completed implementation or executed test suite.

## Payment timing and circuit rounds

### Draft schedule

Keep one-minute payment rounds and an initial ten-round paid-circuit allocation. Successful negotiated renewal can extend the same circuit; otherwise the existing expiration remains in force. The initial timing candidate is **60 seconds of advance payment lead time**, separate from late-payment grace. While the current minute runs, the client starts funding the next minute. The service waits for incoming settlement, reserves that round's funds, and then pays its own relays. Client-side relay payments can proceed independently; payments to distinct service-side relays need not be serialized after the service has received sufficient funding.

| Parameter | Draft starting point | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Round duration | 60 seconds | Paid service interval; unchanged by paying early. |
| Payment lead time | 60 seconds | Start the client-to-service funding step before the next round is needed, leaving time for onward relay payments and wallet observation. |
| Late-payment grace | Separate bounded value; 15 seconds is the current relay implementation's baseline | Determines the final nonpayment closure deadline. It is not another minute of advance notice and must be explicit in agreed terms. |
| Rounds per allocation | 10 | No automatic eleventh round: a continuation needs accepted renewal terms, all-hop admission, and round funding. No automatic transfer to a replacement circuit. |

These are draft timing candidates, not measured guarantees. Small payment amounts do not guarantee fast routing or settlement. The additional lead time addresses the two successive payment stages, client-to-service and service-to-relays, rather than assuming that Tor's data-path hop count determines Lightning payment duration. The 60-second budget must cover both stages, wallet observation, and any permitted retries; it is not a separate 60-second promise for each stage.

### First paid-minute start and independent circuit schedules

**Approved policy; implementation deferred.** Let `t0` denote activation of the first service-side relay's paid round for the authorized setup attempt, after the service has verified and reserved the client's initial-round/setup funding. Start the service-side SCQ's first 60-second interval at that activation. Do not start it at client wallet dispatch, incoming invoice settlement, reservation, status receipt, or the eventual client-connected acknowledgment. Handshake/setup costs incurred before this activation must already be covered by the separately accepted initial funding/setup policy; this anchor cannot authorize unreserved spending. Paid-round activation is a protocol state event, including an explicitly zero-priced paid round under the mandatory accounting rules; do not invent a Lightning settlement for a zero charge or confuse a free-only introduction circuit with a paid rendezvous activation.

Persist the first activation/relay-schedule reference, service-local monotonic mapping, quote/core/client/circuit/attempt/round binding and associated deadline snapshot in the durable ledger. A queued wallet payment, controller dispatch or delayed callback does not by itself establish the relay's actual activation time. Define the relay activation acknowledgment/source event and bounded clock/observation uncertainty before implementation; inability to establish an agreed safe schedule must cause bounded refusal/failure, not an invented favorable timestamp. Authenticate the resulting start and upcoming funding/setup deadlines to the client under the existing status/delivery rules. Keep private relay identities/payment identifiers out of that report; it authenticates the service's schedule claim, not independently verified private-path expenditure.

Construction consumes the first minute. Neither joining the rendezvous, local client handshake completion, a client acknowledgment, polling nor a retry resets `t0` or buys another interval. A client cannot postpone service-side billing by withholding a connected acknowledgment. If the circuit becomes usable late in that minute, the remaining time is only what remains under the existing schedule; if construction fails, reconcile incurred/remaining/uncertain funds under the accepted policy without promising a delivered full minute or automatic refund. Observing a start late does not justify moving the clock forward or paying for an already closed/expired attempt.

For SCQ accounting, consecutive 60-second service intervals remain anchored to `t0`; the first ends at `t0 + 60 seconds`, with subsequent intervals following the agreed round numbering. Actual relay grace, funding acceptance and permitted padding remain separately bounded terms, not automatic extensions of the billable interval. Service relays can activate at different times during construction, and the client's own circuit can start earlier or later. Record their own negotiated schedules and map the service's outgoing funding obligations against the earliest relevant relay deadline. Do not force either circuit half or every hop onto a shared wall clock, reset a later hop to hide elapsed cost, reveal the private path to the client, or infer that paying one half proves funding of the other. Quote admission/start commitments must establish that the mapped schedules, all resulting costs and outstanding commitments fit the accepted budget and deadline policy.

The [setup deadline and startup funding cap](#setup-deadline-and-startup-funding-cap) below apply independently of the billing anchor. Advance payment can become due while construction is still in progress; inability to fit the required margin within those bounds causes bounded failure rather than operator wallet spending or implicit consent to additional rounds.

**Privacy/timing assessment:** activation/deadline/attempt records and status remain encrypted with fresh delivery bindings; no cross-hop identifier or role-specific payment header is added. Reporting progress/start can expose timing to the authorized client, and observers may still correlate construction, status and wallet traffic. Retain the bounded shared control-message timing/padding design without delaying an authenticated deadline until it becomes unsafe to act on. Measure callback/activation skew, delayed/reordered start reports, first-hop versus later-hop clocks, client-half offsets, clock uncertainty, startup-next-round exposure and free/paid resource pressure. Test immediate/late/no handshake, withheld connected acknowledgment, zero-priced paid activation, failure before/after activation, crash/restart at activation, duplicate status, budget exhaustion and no timer reset/unauthorized prepayment/rebuild. Initial quote sizes and round durations are unchanged. Only documentation checks accompany this decision; exact source-event/controller/status encodings, deadline parameters, wallet/relay integration and correlated runtime/privacy validation remain deferred under [ET-30 and related tasks](../TODO.md).

### Setup deadline and startup funding cap

**Approved draft policy; implementation deferred.** Use a candidate 60-second setup deadline starting at the durable transition to `FUNDED`: the service has verified initial settlement through its receiving node and reserved the required initial round/setup funds for the accepted quote and authorized attempt. Persist that trigger and the absolute deadline atomically with the reservation. This is a setup timer, distinct from `t0`, relay funding deadlines and late-payment grace. Activation, partial funding, status updates, retries, fragments, cached replies, key changes and restart MUST NOT move it forward. The 60-second value is an initial design candidate, not a measured Lightning or Tor completion guarantee; validate it before deployment and disclose the exact accepted policy before any payment.

While setup remains pending, authorize no more than round one and one prepaid next round (round two), plus the separately disclosed setup/handshake fees and routing costs already bounded by the quote. This is a ceiling, not a requirement to buy two rounds immediately. Require the client's explicit accepted startup/outstanding and cumulative spending limits; continue using separate round-bound invoices and reservations. Count settled funds, reservations and unresolved outgoing commitments without double counting, and do not reuse a cap merely because a callback is missing or a payment is being reconciled. The client's own rendezvous half has its separate approved budget. The SCQ's onward relay spending is not an additional client charge on top of the same SCQ funds.

Before each paid operation, atomically check the attempt state, remaining setup time, mapped relay deadlines, verified available reservation and all spending limits. A full-minute advance lead may require round-two funding during construction, but MUST NOT authorize round three while setup is pending. If the required safe funding margin cannot fit, decline or fail the attempt within its existing deadline. No pending invoice, request for status or timeout permits drawing down the operator's wallet, buying all ten rounds, borrowing another circuit's funds or silently increasing the cap.

At deadline expiration or earlier terminal failure, stop new paid operations and further automatic funding for the attempt. A funding trigger simultaneous with expiration MUST lose to the terminal deadline check. Reconcile wallet/controller work already in flight; cancellation is not proof that settlement or construction did not occur. Keep durable accounting and apply the accepted failure/refund policy. Late settlement, a late join or a delayed status MUST NOT reopen a terminal attempt or initiate a replacement. A replacement needs explicit acceptance of a new authorization, with prior uncertain commitments still accounted for. Construction time already consumed remains subject to the accepted billing policy; neither timeout nor the two-round cap guarantees delivery or refunds.

**Completion boundary still to specify:** the client alone establishes local connectivity through its verified Tor handshake; `JOIN_SENT` is not that proof. Finalize the authenticated setup-completion event, its deadline race handling and how the service distinguishes completed setup from an unacknowledged join before implementing this policy. A missing client ACK cannot reset billing, extend setup or enable additional startup rounds. Do not introduce an unreviewed ACK requirement that kills an otherwise established stream merely because a status message was lost. Until this boundary is defined and tested, the startup cap cannot be lifted solely on a positive service status.

**Privacy/timing assessment:** keep reservation timestamps, attempt references, budgets and deadline reports inside the authenticated encrypted control flow, with fresh delivery bindings and common bounded padding/timing policies. No new public role marker or cross-hop identifier is required. Fixed deadlines, funding bursts and terminal failures can still expose timing correlations; progress scheduling must not conceal a deadline until action is unsafe. Measure reservation-to-build and build-to-handshake latency, wallet/relay observation delays, hop schedule skew, aggregate startup load and adversarial delay/drop behavior before selecting deployment values. Validate expiration races, partial/late funding, lost status/ACK, restart without timer reset, uncertain commitments, zero-priced rounds, refusal of a third startup round and no duplicate spend/rebuild. Documentation checks are not runtime or anonymity evidence; implementation and correlated tests remain deferred under [ET-31](../TODO.md).

### Initial round and setup

There is no preceding minute in which to prepay round one. Before the service incurs any paid setup or relay charges, the client MUST settle funding for the initial round and all explicitly agreed setup/handshake costs. The service MUST reserve that funding before spending it. A pending payment or the client's claim that it has sent funds is insufficient.

The quote and circuit establishment exchange must distinguish the bounded build/setup phase from the paid round schedule. They must define the start event and build timeout, what setup charges may be incurred before rendezvous succeeds, and what happens if either circuit half fails to become usable. First-round funding does not promise a successful connection. Exact start-event signaling remains wire-protocol work; it MUST NOT depend on an unpaid request being allowed to spend the operator's funds.

### Round identifiers, acceptance windows, and deadlines

Every payment and its authorization MUST identify the intended circuit, recipient, round, and agreed amount. Define a valid early-payment acceptance window, the round start/end, and final grace deadline. The service's incoming funding deadline must leave time before its outgoing relay-payment deadlines. An advertised schedule must be achievable across all selected hops; paying the service alone does not prove that any relay has been paid.

The protocol MUST accept legitimate, correctly bound prepayment for an upcoming round instead of rejecting it merely because the preceding round is still active. Early settlement does not start the next round early, extend circuit lifetime, authorize another round, or permit replay. Duplicate observations must refer to the same payment record and reservation.

Use authenticated circuit-relative schedules and bounded clock/observation tolerances rather than requiring perfectly synchronized clocks across all relays. The final encoding must specify how each relay's local schedule maps to its payment identifiers and how the client/service learns the relevant deadlines. A relay need not learn the identities of the service's other relays. Recipient-node settlement records are authoritative for payment status and settlement time where available; callback arrival time alone is not the settlement time. Wallet adapters must expose the information needed to enforce the agreed acceptance policy.

### Round payments and failure behavior

During round N, begin funding round N+1 with the advertised lead time. The service MUST wait for settled incoming funding and reserve enough for the corresponding outgoing payments, including agreed fees and padding costs, before sending them. The schedule does not permit the service to borrow from unrelated circuit rounds or subsidize a delayed client payment. The relay's explicitly agreed grace bounds its own exposure; it does not authorize unlimited spending by the service.

Keep incoming funding, outgoing payment state, and circuit state distinct. Incoming settlement, outgoing payment initiation, outgoing settlement, and a usable circuit are different events. A required payment missing its final deadline closes the affected paid circuit; any remaining work and the other circuit half must be stopped or reconciled without silently extending fees, lifetime, or access. Do not grant a new full grace period merely because a poll, retry, or process restart occurred.

A wallet timeout or RPC cancellation does not prove that a payment failed: an in-flight payment may still settle. Persist its identity and reconcile its terminal state before issuing a replacement payment, releasing its reserved funds, or considering a refund. A late-settling payment MUST NOT reopen a closed circuit or automatically authorize a different circuit or round. Record actual agreed costs and resolve unused settled funds under the disclosed failure/refund policy. Authenticated refund details, fees, and eligibility remain open design work; no unconditional refund or delivery guarantee is implied.

### Allocation boundary and validation

Without successful renewal admission, prepayment stops at round ten: round nine may fund round ten, but round ten MUST NOT fund an unauthorized round eleven. With accepted renewal terms and the required acknowledgments below, advance funding for round eleven follows the existing schedule and spending caps. Circuit replacement begins a separate authorization/payment sequence; circuit-specific funding MUST NOT silently migrate to it. Reconcile pending payments and unused reservations on closure, including when the client disconnects before a prepaid round is used.

Before finalizing the lead time or grace value, measure the complete client-to-service-to-relays sequence, including invoice acquisition, settlement, wallet polling/event latency, concurrent onward payments, and retries. Test initial construction, valid early payment, boundary timings, late wallet observations, late settlement after closure, duplicate callbacks, uncertain outgoing payments, restarts, and the absence of unauthorized round-eleven charges. Add the renewal boundary tests below. Local mocked-wallet timing tests do not establish live Lightning routing latency. This section records the agreed draft direction; implementation and timing measurements remain future work.

## Renewing both rendezvous circuit halves

This proposal inherits [spec one's circuit renewal draft](01_paid_circuits.md#circuit-renewal-draft) and the proposed [`PAYMENT_RENEW`/`PAYMENT_RENEWED` wire protocol](01a_circuit_renewal_wire.md). Renewal is a shared transport feature, not an onion-service-specific way to move streams. Each circuit origin separately sends the same per-hop messages to its own relays, with independent private bindings and identifiers. Preserve the two existing circuits joined at the same rendezvous point, their Tor stream/cryptographic state, and the client/service authorization binding. Neither the client nor the service can keep a connection alive if the other half disappears. Renewal does not require a new introduction or descriptor publication solely because another payment period begins.

### SCQ continuation and funding

Before the existing authorization runs out, the service offers an authenticated extension referencing the accepted SCQ, circuit authorization identifier, fresh extension identifier, next period and round range, unchanged per-minute price, and the agreed activation boundary. The extension must retain the client-key/recipient binding and disclose cumulative cost, outstanding-funding limits, deadlines, and failure accounting. It authorizes further individual round purchases on the same circuit, not a reusable session or cross-circuit balance. No ten-round lump payment or repeated build/testing handshake fee is introduced. Whether to permit explicitly accepted price changes in future extensions remains open; this draft preserves the continuing circuit's fixed price.

The client may decline or approve continuation within an explicit renewal/spending policy. Approval of the original SCQ alone does not authorize unlimited extensions. Preparing an extension and registering fresh relay identifiers must fit bounded control-plane resources and cannot incur unfunded paid work for the service. Each hop may refuse future admission; existing paid service still follows its accepted schedule.

### Coordinated preparation and activation

1. Use spec one's draft trigger at the start of round seven in each ten-round allocation (global rounds 7, 17, 27, and so on). When continuation is wanted and authorized, the client prepares renewal with every hop on its existing path. In parallel, the service prepares renewal with every hop on its private existing path, under the proposed SCQ extension. Use independent per-hop identifiers and acknowledgments. Each half follows its agreed relative schedule; specify how preparation is coordinated when their boundaries differ.
2. The client authenticates the service's extension/readiness response, bound to the existing connection and upcoming round range. The service need not reveal its private relay identities or acknowledgments. Its readiness statement is a service commitment, not independently verifiable proof of its path, payment, or future delivery.
3. Before initiating funding for the new period, require accepted extension terms, admission on the client's own path, and the service's authenticated readiness statement. Preparation beginning at round seven must finish before the existing 60-second payment lead is needed. It does not move round-eleven payment into round seven: keep funding incremental on the agreed schedule and within the same outstanding-payment cap. Exact completion deadlines must be specified and measured; negotiation is not assumed to be instantaneous or covered by another free grace period.
4. Continue the existing two-stage funding schedule: the client pays its own relays and settles the service's next-round funding; the service reserves that settlement before paying its own relays. An admission/readiness acknowledgment does not authorize borrowing from the operator's wallet or another client's funds. Pay only the next authorized round within existing caps.
5. On success, both halves continue through their agreed schedules without deliberately closing active streams at round ten. If either side cannot renew or fund its next period, stop further renewal payments, report failure through the authenticated control exchange where possible, and expire under the existing deadlines. Preserve uncertain payment records and reconcile unused funding. Do not cut short already purchased service merely because a future extension was refused.

This is coordinated preparation, not atomic settlement across the client, service, and relays. A half can fail after readiness or after some payments settle. Accepted loss exposure still includes unused current-round service, the prepaid next round, and disclosed fees across both halves; the SCQ's onward relay payments are not another client charge. No automatic refund or live-stream migration is promised. A separately authorized replacement can serve new connections; resuming an interrupted download still depends on the application and remote server.

### Remaining decisions and tests

The per-hop wire draft specifies exact batch acknowledgment, replay/conflict handling, conservative timing, and bounded pending-state rules. The authenticated client/service continuation/readiness/status encodings, relay-to-end-to-end round mappings, and cumulative age/renewal defaults remain separate design tasks. SCQ extensions cannot override relay limits or send a common SCQ identifier to transport relays as a renewal token. Free introduction-circuit renewal remains undecided; retain its existing bounded-free replacement policy, and do not indefinitely renew an idle descriptor-upload circuit under this paid extension. All design/implementation/validation work is tracked in the [El Tor TODO](../TODO.md), including separate deferred replay protection.

Test continuous transfers across multiple allocation boundaries without changing either rendezvous circuit or stream. Test each side declining, one hop refusing, stale/duplicate extension acceptance, unequal boundary times, partial settlement, missing service readiness, payment delay, budget exhaustion, and a relay actually dropping. Verify no unfunded service spending, no unauthorized eleventh round, no duplicate setup fee or quota reset, bounded pending state, and correct closure/reconciliation. This section defines draft acceptance criteria; no renewal implementation or executed end-to-end test is claimed.

## Payment methods and receiver privacy

### Receiver choice and negotiation

Receiver-hiding payment paths are RECOMMENDED, not mandatory. BOLT 12 offers with receiver-hiding blinded paths are the preferred option when both wallets support them. BOLT 11 invoices, including blinded-path invoices where supported by the relevant implementations, are also permitted. An operator may explicitly choose a method that exposes its Lightning node identity or other receiver information.

The receiver advertises its accepted methods and their relevant capabilities in authenticated service metadata. The client selects a mutually supported method according to its wallet capabilities and configured privacy preferences before paying. A payment method MUST NOT be inferred to provide receiver privacy merely from its BOLT number; the actual fields, path configuration, and wallet behavior determine what is exposed. A public relay and a location-hidden onion-service operator may make different choices.

This is an extensible method policy, not a claim that every wallet supports every method. Additional methods require an explicit integration, an authenticated payment-request mechanism, and reliable recipient-side settlement verification. Unsupported methods MUST be rejected before funds are sent. Any fallback must respect the receiver's advertised choices and the client's configured policy; the protocol does not impose a universal requirement to hide receiver identity.

### Shared verification requirements

Regardless of method or privacy choice, the receiver MUST verify the intended recipient, agreed amount, settled status, circuit/round binding, and single redemption. A matching preimage/hash pair alone is insufficient without the receiver's own settled-payment record. Privacy preferences do not relax payment authorization, spending limits, or replay protection.

Each circuit round needs a distinct payment identifier and authorization; duplicate delivery or retry must follow the existing idempotency rules rather than reuse an already consumed payment. A BOLT 12 offer may be reused to request fresh invoices, but an already paid invoice MUST NOT fund another round. Methods based on BOLT 11 or an external invoice-acquisition service must define how fresh invoices are obtained and authenticated before the paid rendezvous exists. That bootstrap exchange and its bounded resource use remain protocol design work.

### Privacy properties and implementation scope

When the receiver chooses BOLT 12 blinded paths, account for both invoice-request delivery and payment delivery, and inspect identity fields in the offer and invoice for unintended disclosure. Blinding does not hide the receiver from all of its direct Lightning counterparties or remove timing/amount correlation. Network-address privacy also depends on wallet transport and deployment choices. The operator decides which of these properties it requires.

LND documents blinded invoice creation through its `is_blinded` option and an ephemeral signing key. The local LNI LND adapter exposes this option; that source support is not an end-to-end interoperability test. Wallet-specific support must be checked and tested before advertising a method as usable. No new wallet implementation is claimed by this specification update.

## Descriptor publication, retrieval, and free discovery

### Ordinary onion-service publication flow

Descriptor publication is separate from maintaining an introduction point. For a normal anonymous onion service:

1. The service's Tor software establishes introduction points, creates the descriptor describing how to contact them, and signs and encrypts it using the v3 onion-service descriptor construction.
2. The service derives the responsible HSDir relays from the current consensus, time-period/blinded-key rules, and shared randomness. These are selected directory relays, not the directory authorities receiving an ordinary relay descriptor.
3. It builds anonymized, normally three-hop upload circuits ending at those HSDirs and uploads descriptor copies. It MUST NOT replace this with a direct connection that exposes the service's origin address.
4. In ordinary Tor, an upload circuit need not remain open merely to keep the descriptor stored. The proposed El Tor lifecycle below normally retains it until its ten-minute expiration. The service continues maintaining separate introduction circuits for forwarding client introductions.
5. A client knowing the onion address derives the appropriate lookup information, fetches a descriptor through Tor, and verifies and decrypts it. Optional client authorization can restrict who can decrypt the connection information.

Descriptors are replicated and refreshed, including when introduction information changes and across descriptor time-period transitions. They are not a single permanent upload or a cleartext network-wide directory listing. HSDirs need not learn the onion address or decrypt introduction details merely to store and serve a valid descriptor. Ordinary clients knowing a non-restricted onion address can retrieve its descriptor.

### El Tor upload-circuit lifetime

After completing publication, normally retain the descriptor-upload circuit until its ten-minute expiration rather than closing it immediately because the upload finished. This follows the shared lifetime policy in spec one, subject to user choice, failures, and free-resource admission/enforcement limits. Remaining idle after a small transfer and closing early when a circuit performs poorly are also normal client behaviors; circuit lifetime or idleness alone is not an onion-service identifier.

Retaining an upload circuit is a lifecycle choice, not a requirement for HSDir storage. Its expiration MUST NOT itself cause an unnecessary new upload or immediate circuit replacement. Publish again when a descriptor update or refresh is needed. Preserve the shared padding/maintenance policy within the free resource budget and count retained idle circuits against the same aggregate limits. Introduction circuits, which must remain available to receive invitations, have their separate replacement requirements below.

### Proposed pricing: bounded free discovery

Basic descriptor publication, storage, refresh, and retrieval SHOULD be available without Lightning payments within explicitly advertised resource limits. This allows a service to become discoverable before its first paying client and allows clients to learn contact and payment information before purchasing rendezvous traffic. It is a proposed policy, not an already implemented free service or an unlimited availability guarantee.

The transport part uses the same purpose-independent bounded free allowance at every hop, including the guard, middle, and HSDir endpoint. Intermediate relays enforce resource limits without needing to inspect descriptor contents or recognize a directory operation. The HSDir separately enforces limits on its storage and request-processing resources. Making endpoint storage free alone would not remove transport charges on the earlier hops.

The allowance must fit legitimate complete descriptor uploads/downloads, acknowledgments, copies, refreshes, and permitted replacement overlap. A descriptor is larger than a single introduction message; a tiny sustained rate may require a bounded burst budget to finish publication in a useful time. Measure this workload before selecting byte rates or burst sizes. Include transport/padding overhead and any compression/decompression limits in the resource accounting. Descriptor size, lifetime, and periodic refresh are distinct from circuit payment periods and the baseline ten-minute expiration of non-renewed circuits.

Free capacity does not silently waive the normal price of an already paid circuit, turn paid application traffic into free traffic, or authorize an automatic wallet charge if a limit is reached. Eligible discovery setup must fit the bounded free setup policy. Additional paid capacity requires explicit operator/client consent and a spending limit; overload should produce bounded retries or temporary unavailability rather than an unexpected charge.

HSDir endpoints cannot be chosen arbitrarily merely because another relay offers free storage: clients must derive the same responsible locations as publishers. Capability signaling and HSDir eligibility/selection rules must be consistent across the network. If a responsible endpoint lacks this feature or is overloaded, use the permitted replicas and bounded retry behavior. Any alternative eligibility rule requires an explicitly specified interoperable protocol change; do not silently change the hash ring on one side.

### Spam and resource-exhaustion controls

Low bandwidth per honest service does not make unrestricted publication safe. An origin can create many service keys, issue repeated descriptor updates, submit malformed documents, or trigger repeated lookups. These consume storage, signature-verification CPU, circuit setup, queues, and response bandwidth. A valid signature proves control of a key, not scarcity of identities or absence of spam.

Supporting HSDirs and path relays MUST implement the following five resource bounds. The policy is recorded here; new numeric defaults remain provisional until legitimate workloads and overload behavior are measured.

| Bound | Initial policy | Scope and rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Descriptor size | Retain Tor's active consensus size limit, currently `HSV3MaxDescriptorSize` with a default of 50,000 bytes. Do not invent a smaller El Tor format. | Bound complete documents and request framing; reject oversized/malformed inputs without unbounded allocation. Preserve descriptor encryption and padding rules. |
| Upload/update rate | Allow a bounded burst, then a configurable sustained update rate per descriptor key, with explicit retry/backoff behavior. | Accommodate introduction replacement, legitimate revisions, replicas, overlapping periods, and recovery. Repeated uploads or new keys must not bypass global work limits. |
| Total storage | Use a configurable HSDir cache/memory budget, expire entries, and retain the appropriate latest revision rather than unlimited revision history. | Validate descriptor certificates/signatures and revision rules. Do not overwrite a newer accepted revision with an older one. Establish bounded overload/eviction behavior. |
| Free traffic | Bound sustained and burst traffic per circuit and across the relay, in both directions, including responses, padding, and accounting overhead. | A burst must fit a complete valid descriptor transfer. Cap repeated lookups/misses and combined traffic across circuits; the free allowance is not multiplied without limit by opening more circuits. |
| Setup and processing | Bound circuit creation, concurrent uploads/downloads, verification work, queued requests, pending builds, and buffering. | Tiny transfers still consume CPU and memory. Enforce relay-wide ceilings across connections and newly generated identities. |

Relay-local resource budgets should be configurable, with interoperable advertised free-capacity limits and predictable rejection/retry behavior. Retaining a Tor consensus size limit does not mean that every other quota becomes a consensus parameter. Clients and services must have enough information to select a usable path without exposing their onion identity to intermediate relays. Test defaults against actual publication and retrieval workloads, especially our proposed ten-minute introduction-circuit replacements, before selecting update rates, free byte rates, burst sizes, or concurrency/cache budgets.

Keep the outer descriptor verifiable without requiring HSDirs to decrypt its connection information, learn the long-term onion identity, or link successive blinded identities solely to enforce quotas. Per-key and per-connection throttles supplement global limits; they cannot prevent one origin from creating many identities. Fair admission under a flood remains a design and test topic. Limits bound resource exposure but do not promise that spam cannot make a service temporarily unreachable.

### Validation and open decisions

Measure publication size, replica count, update rate, and retrieval traffic for legitimate services, especially with ten-minute introduction-circuit replacement and time-period overlap. Test that an onion service can publish, refresh, and be found through an entirely eligible free path without paying continuous relay fees or exposing its origin. Confirm that clients and publishers agree on responsible HSDirs and reject stale/invalid descriptors.

Then test many-key upload floods, frequent revisions, malformed/oversized inputs, repeated lookups/misses, and combined traffic across circuits and connections. Verify bounded storage, CPU, queues, setup work, and bandwidth, as well as recovery for legitimate users after overload. Numeric quotas, fair admission, capability encoding, and any extra anti-spam mechanism remain undecided until these tests inform them. This section records a bounded-free proposal, not completed spam-resistance validation.

## Bounded free allowance for introduction availability

### Purpose and scope

The service needs open circuits to its introduction points so those relays can forward client introductions. These circuits are shared availability infrastructure across many visitors, not a new service-side circuit built for every client. Charging ordinary paid-circuit rates continuously could make staying reachable prohibitively expensive.

This proposal uses spec one's shared, purpose-independent free traffic allowance at **every hop** of an eligible ordinary El Tor circuit, including its guard, middle, and introduction endpoint. Waiving only the introduction endpoint's fee would leave the other hops charging the operator. A service MUST choose a path whose relays support the allowance and currently admit free circuits. Availability remains subject to advertised resource limits; free capacity is not guaranteed under overload.

The allowance is based on enforceable resource use, not a claim that a circuit is for an onion service. Earlier relays MUST NOT need to learn the onion-service identity, full path, or introduction role to apply it. Any origin may use this limited capacity, including for other low-volume communication. Such use is an intentional subsidy; the design does not claim that a tiny allowance is useless to a freeloader.

### Relay policy and enforcement

A relay's normal advertised paid rate remains under the operator's control. Supporting relays MUST also advertise the free allowance and admission limits explicitly; clients MUST NOT infer support from a zero handshake fee or from the presence of an introduction endpoint. The allowance is a proposed protocol feature, not a behavior already guaranteed by the implementation.

Free capacity MUST have enforceable limits for:

- Sustained traffic per circuit, with a bounded burst allowance sufficient for legitimate introduction messages and maintenance.
- Traffic in both directions, including padding, with clearly defined cell/byte accounting and wire overhead treatment.
- Circuit creation rate, handshake computation, pending circuit builds, and concurrent established circuits.
- Total free bandwidth, circuit state/memory, and setup work across the relay. Per-circuit limits alone are insufficient because an origin can open many circuits and combine them.
- Queued data and queued work. Excess traffic must be bounded, throttled, or rejected without allowing unlimited buffering or silently increasing fees.

Enforcement must account for rebuilds, overlapping replacements, and attempts spread across multiple connections or claimed identities. Anonymous origins can create new identities, so identity-based limits alone are insufficient; the relay-wide resource ceiling remains mandatory. Exact limits, overload/fairness behavior, capability signaling, and accounting formats remain open until measured under local tests.

At the introduction endpoint, enforce the introduction protocol's permitted behavior and rate-limit forwarded requests. Do not permit an established introduction circuit to become an unrestricted application-data path. These endpoint checks complement resource limits at earlier hops; they do not prove the circuit's purpose to the guard or middle relay. Payload semantics at earlier hops remain encrypted.

### Circuit lifetime and payment behavior

Free introduction circuits follow the proposed ten-minute maximum circuit lifetime. The service establishes replacements before retiring old circuits and updates its descriptor when introduction points or their published keys change. Replacement overlap, setup, acknowledgments, and normal maintenance must fit the bounded free allowance. The descriptor upload circuit is separate and need not stay open just to keep the descriptor published. Descriptor distribution uses the proposed bounded free discovery policy above; exact capacity limits remain design work.

Eligible free setup and maintenance MUST NOT require the service to pay a normal paid-circuit handshake fee merely to remain reachable. The existing paid-circuit handshake-fee logic remains: relay operators set that fee, and new relays may advertise zero sats. This proposal does not introduce a new paid-activation fee or use the free allowance as a promise of full-bandwidth testing.

Rendezvous/application-data circuits continue to use one-minute payment rounds with bounded settlement padding and ten-round allocations. Continuing past an allocation requires negotiated paid renewal of the existing circuit; free-only circuit renewal is still undecided. Failure to make a required payment closes the paid circuit. A closed or unpaid paid circuit MUST NOT continue by switching into free mode. A separately admitted free circuit is still subject to the same small allowance and all aggregate limits.

The service MUST NOT automatically spend wallet funds when free capacity is exhausted. It may retry within bounded setup limits, select another supporting path, or temporarily lose introduction availability. Any choice to purchase additional capacity requires an explicit operator budget; unsolicited traffic cannot authorize that purchase.

### Required design validation

Before choosing numeric limits or claiming this is affordable, measure introduction message sizes, bursts, maintenance/padding, circuit setup, and descriptor-update work under the ten-minute rotation policy. Validate that a legitimate service remains reachable through repeated rotations without continuous relay payments. Separately test combined free circuits, rebuild storms, queue exhaustion, and attempts to carry bulk traffic. Verify that each hop enforces its local limits, aggregate resource use stays bounded, and exhausted free capacity cannot drain either wallet. This is future validation work, not a report of completed tests.

## Planning resume point

The receiver-choice privacy policy, payment-timing draft, bounded-free descriptor discovery proposal, and Service Circuit Quote (SCQ) architecture are recorded. An SCQ totals selected relay descriptor rates plus disclosed fees, is accepted before service-side paid construction, stays fixed for that circuit, and is paid in one-minute rounds. Quote/bootstrap encoding, relay rate commitments, exposure accounting, and failure/refund details remain open as listed above. Spec one now has a shared renewal architecture and concrete per-hop wire draft, with two-half coordination above. Exporter/security review, initial commitments, local controller and SCQ continuation encodings, cumulative age defaults, free-only renewal and repricing decisions remain tracked in the [implementation TODO](../TODO.md). Strict El Tor service version agreement is now recorded above: payment handling is mandatory, the initial service version is 1, and the authenticated SCQ confirms an exact prepayment match without automatic downgrade or ordinary-Tor fallback. The introduction-point request/reply transport for prepayment quotes is now recorded above. Fixed-size envelopes, shared response timing, bounded retries, request/reply field inventories, quote signatures inside HPKE replies, and separate request-encryption keys with bounded rotation are now recorded. The version-one cryptographic suite and strict binary encoding rules are now fixed below. Initial field-by-field layouts, exact signature/HPKE domains, and the sizing inventory are now drafted in the initial wire section. The authenticated payment-policy catalog and exact-reference resolution requirements are now recorded above, including immutable accepted terms and the later invoice-verification gate. Client-signed acceptance and round-specific invoice acquisition are now recorded, including durable idempotency and node-settled reservation before paid construction. Authenticated funded/building/join status, client-confirmed handshake readiness and failure-after-funding rules are now recorded. The service-side first minute now starts at first paid relay-round activation, includes construction, and does not synchronize the client half. The candidate 60-second funded-to-setup deadline and two-round startup funding ceiling are now recorded; the authenticated setup-completion boundary and deadline race handling remain open; exact source-event/status wire, metadata schemas, independent security/privacy review and runtime validation remain required.

A fingerprinting review is also open for combinations of observable traffic behavior, introduction-circuit replacement, descriptor update timing/size, and identifier reuse. The shared spec-one construction already requires equal-size per-hop encrypted payment fields and dummy zero-fee handshake values; their mere presence is not an added onion-service marker. Normal client comparisons must include idle ten-minute circuits and early closure for poor performance. No traffic-correlation finding has been demonstrated by this planning discussion. Standard Tor introduction-point lifetimes are randomized over a default 18–24-hour range; the draft's ten-minute circuit replacement is a design difference, not a demonstrated deanonymization finding. Preserve the distinction between introduction-point lifetime and the lifetime of a circuit connecting to it when reviewing alternatives. The paid renewal proposal changes the former hard cutoff into renewable payment periods when negotiated; its effect on traffic patterns and long-lived-path exposure requires review. The free-only replacement and receiver-choice privacy policies remain unchanged.

Remaining design work includes security/privacy review of the quote return path, its authenticated bootstrap/SCQ and retry-envelope wire encoding, the complete version-1 required-feature profile, initial relay price/start commitment, SCQ continuation messages, and open renewal/lifetime decisions above. The separate paid-circuit replay-protection investigation remains deferred; these specification edits do not implement its fixes.
