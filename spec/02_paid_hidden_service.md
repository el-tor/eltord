# Paid Hidden Service
### How to pay for a Rendezvous Circuit (Tor Hidden Service + Lightning Network)

**Overview:** The diagram below shows a UML-style sequence of interactions between a Tor **Client**, a **Hidden Service** (HS), an **Introduction Point** (Intro), a **Rendezvous Point** (RP), and the **Lightning Network**, implementing a *paid rendezvous circuit*. In this scenario, the hidden service requires the client to pay via Lightning before granting access. The sequence covers retrieving the HS descriptor (with a BOLT 12 Lightning offer), completing a Lightning payment, and then the usual Tor hidden service rendezvous handshake (INTRODUCE1/2 and RENDEZVOUS1/2 cells) with the payment proof integrated. All Tor protocol cell types (e.g. `INTRODUCE2`, `RENDEZVOUS1`, etc.) and payment-related messages are annotated in the diagram.  

![Paid Tor Hidden Service Flow](../assets/paid_hs_flow.png)  
*Figure: UML Sequence Diagram of a paid Tor hidden service rendezvous, involving Lightning Network payment. The client pays the hidden service (via a BOLT12 offer/Lightning invoice) before the hidden service will complete the Tor rendezvous handshake. Tor cells like `INTRODUCE1/2` and `RENDEZVOUS1/2` are shown in bold text, and the payment flow is included on the left side.* 

**Design status:** This is a proposed El Tor extension, not a description of standard Tor or a completed implementation. The four revision requirements below refine the payment and authorization flow. The existing diagram is conceptual; it does not yet show client-bound authorization, circuit/round funding, retry accounting, or the bounded free introduction allowance. Paid circuits use one-minute rounds and an initial ten-round payment period. The renewal draft below allows that same paid circuit to continue only through negotiated, funded extensions; it does not claim implemented renewal support. The Service Circuit Quote (SCQ) below defines the fixed pricing agreement for the service-to-rendezvous circuit. Wire formats, padding durations, and free-capacity limits remain open design work.

## Circuit construction inherited from spec one

All circuits in this proposal MUST be built the same way as [spec one](01_paid_circuits.md#shared-circuit-construction-and-bounded-free-allowance): descriptor upload/fetch circuits, both introduction paths, and both rendezvous paths inherit the ordinary El Tor per-hop payment construction. Retain the handshake hash/preimage fields, ten unique round identifiers per hop, per-hop encryption, and equal-size padding. Use fresh dummy handshake values where no fee applies; do not omit fields or introduce shared constants for free circuits.

The small free allowance is part of spec one's shared circuit/resource policy, not an onion-service-only circuit header or a shorter payment payload. No guard/middle payment field needs to label its origin as an onion service, and construction size or payment-field presence MUST NOT vary merely because a circuit is using the free allowance. Normal Tor introduction, directory, and rendezvous commands still operate at their intended endpoints. The client-to-service access authorization in this document is carried separately inside the service-encrypted introduction message; it does not replace or change the shared relay-payment construction.

One-minute paid rounds, ten-round payment allocations, early closure, and idle periods are ordinary El Tor lifecycle behavior. Paid renewal inherits spec one; without compatible renewal, expiration still requires replacement. This section preserves the same construction and resource policy across roles; it is not a claim that every traffic pattern is indistinguishable or that these proposed extensions are implemented.

**Service setup:** Before a client arrives, the service establishes circuits to its introduction points and publishes a descriptor listing them to hidden-service directories. The service keeps introduction circuits available using the bounded free allowance described below. Descriptor upload is a separate operation; publishing a descriptor does not require retaining its upload circuit. Introduction circuits carry introduction messages, while the later rendezvous circuits carry application traffic.

The sequence below uses BOLT 12 as the recommended example. It does not require BOLT 12 or receiver-hiding paths; other mutually supported, verifiable payment methods are permitted under the payment-method policy below.

Below is a step-by-step breakdown of this sequence:

1. **Client Fetches Hidden Service Descriptor:** The client uses the onion address to query the Tor network’s distributed hash table (HS directory) for the hidden service’s descriptor ([Tor Project | How do Onion Services work?](https://community.torproject.org/onion-services/overview/#:~:text=it%2C%20so%20they%20connect%20to,descriptor%20of%20SecureDrop%27s%20Onion%20Service)). The descriptor is signed by the HS and contains a list of its introduction point addresses/keys ([Tor Project | How do Onion Services work?](https://community.torproject.org/onion-services/overview/#:~:text=And%20inside%20the%20descriptor%2C%20there,themselves%20to%20SecureDrop%27s%20Onion%20Service)). The HS descriptor also advertises its accepted payment methods and the information needed to request a Service Circuit Quote (SCQ) and obtain a fresh payment request. In this BOLT 12 example, it includes a Lightning **BOLT 12 offer** that the client can use to request a fresh invoice. The method list and invoice-acquisition mechanism must be authenticated with the service descriptor; exact encoding remains open.

2. **Service Circuit Quote and Invoice Request:** Before funding the service's path, the client selects its proposed rendezvous relay and a fresh client authorization key and requests an SCQ through the advertised authenticated bootstrap mechanism. Selecting the rendezvous relay does not require the service to construct its circuit. The service reads candidate relays' descriptors, selects a compatible private path, and quotes the combined per-minute relay cost plus disclosed setup and routing-fee budgets. The client may reject the quote without authorizing paid work by the service. If it accepts, it obtains an initial-round invoice bound to that SCQ, circuit authorization identifier, and client key. In the BOLT 12 example, the advertised offer is used to request a fresh invoice through Lightning; other methods follow their negotiated invoice-acquisition mechanism. Quote delivery, acceptance, and invoice binding remain wire-protocol work. An invoice request or dummy handshake value alone does not authorize service-side spending.

3. **Client Pays Invoice (Lightning Payment):** The client’s Lightning wallet routes the payment through the Lightning Network to the hidden service’s node. Upon successful payment, the hidden service’s Lightning node receives the funds and, as per the Lightning protocol, releases the secret preimage associated with the invoice’s `payment_hash` ([How to prove that a lightning invoice was paid? | Blink FAQ](https://faq.blink.sv/blink-and-other-wallets/how-to-prove-that-a-lightning-invoice-was-paid#:~:text=,was%20included%20in%20the%20invoice)). The preimage travels back to the client as proof of payment. At this point, the payment is complete – the client knows the payment succeeded (having received the preimage), and the hidden service knows it has been paid. The client retains the `payment_hash` and the preimage returned on settlement; the preimage cannot be derived from the hash. These identify and demonstrate payment, but do not by themselves authenticate the connecting client. Before payment, the service must associate the invoice with a fresh client authorization key and agreed terms, for the intended circuit and initial round, as described in revision 1 below.

4. **Client Establishes a Rendezvous Point:** In parallel with the payment (or afterward), the client prepares for the Tor connection. It uses the **rendezvous point (RP)** selected for the accepted SCQ. The client builds a 3-hop Tor circuit to that relay and sends a `RELAY_COMMAND_ESTABLISH_RENDEZVOUS` message to designate it as the RP ([Tor Project | How do Onion Services work?](https://community.torproject.org/onion-services/overview/#:~:text=Before%20the%20introduction%20takes%20place%2C,part%20of%20the%20rendezvous%20procedure)). This message contains a randomly generated 20-byte **rendezvous cookie** that will serve as a shared secret for linking circuits ([The rendezvous protocol - Tor Specifications](https://spec.torproject.org/rend-spec/rendezvous-protocol.html#:~:text=The%20client%20sends%20the%20rendezvous,byte%20value)). The rendezvous point stores this cookie and replies with a `RENDEZVOUS_ESTABLISHED` acknowledgment (confirming it’s ready) ([torspec/rend-spec-v3.txt at main · torproject/torspec · GitHub](https://github.com/torproject/torspec/blob/main/rend-spec-v3.txt#:~:text=39%20)). At this stage, the RP is “parked” waiting for the hidden service to connect with the same cookie; it does not yet know which HS will connect.

5. **Client Builds Introduction Circuit:** Next, the client opens a Tor circuit to one of the hidden service’s **introduction points** listed in the descriptor. This is also a 3-hop anonymized circuit terminating at the chosen intro point. (The HS is already connected to this intro point via its own long-lived circuit.) Now the client has two circuits in place – one to the RP and one to the Intro point – and has paid the required Lightning invoice. The client is ready to introduce itself to the HS.

6. **Client Sends `INTRODUCE1` (with Rendezvous Info & Payment Authorization):** Over the intro circuit, the client sends an **`INTRODUCE1`** cell to the introduction relay, requesting introduction to the HS ([torspec/rend-spec-v3.txt at main · torproject/torspec · GitHub](https://github.com/torproject/torspec/blob/main/rend-spec-v3.txt#:~:text=34%20)). This cell (encrypted to the HS’s service key) contains: the rendezvous cookie generated in step 4, the identity of the chosen rendezvous point, the client’s ephemeral key and other handshake data, **and a compact payment authorization** referencing the invoice and proving possession of its bound client authorization key. The authorization covers this connection attempt, including its rendezvous details, as described in revision 1; a payment hash alone is not an access credential. The introduction point then forwards this to the hidden service over the HS’s intro circuit as an **`INTRODUCE2`** cell ([torspec/rend-spec-v3.txt at main · torproject/torspec · GitHub](https://github.com/torproject/torspec/blob/main/rend-spec-v3.txt#:~:text=35%20)). In essence, the intro point “introduces” the client to the HS by delivering the client’s rendezvous details and payment proof. The hidden service receives the INTRODUCE2 message (containing the rendezvous cookie, RP address and payment authorization) ([Tor Project | How do Onion Services work?](https://community.torproject.org/onion-services/overview/#:~:text=The%20introduction%20point%20passes%20your,whether%20you%27re%20trustworthy%20or%20not)).

7. **Hidden Service Verifies Payment and Reserves Round Funding:** Upon receiving `INTRODUCE2`, the HS verifies the client-bound authorization and checks its own Lightning node's invoice records through a synchronized local ledger. The invoice must have been issued by this service for the accepted SCQ, agreed payment method, offer where applicable, circuit/round amount, and terms, and must be settled. When the authorization supplies a preimage, the service checks that its SHA-256 hash equals the invoice's `payment_hash`; a matching pair without the service's settled invoice record is insufficient. The service then atomically reserves the settled funding assigned to this circuit's initial round, including any agreed setup costs, before constructing a paid circuit. Invalid, expired, unpaid, or replayed authorizations do not trigger paid circuit construction. Wallet synchronization happens asynchronously rather than through a blocking wallet RPC in the Tor handshake. `INTRODUCE_ACK` is sent by the introduction point to report introduction delivery; it is not confirmation that the service accepted payment. Service-level rejection/status signaling still needs an explicitly defined protocol.

8. **Hidden Service Builds Circuit to Rendezvous Point:** The hidden service now initiates the rendezvous from its side. Using the round funding reserved in step 7, the HS selects and pays its own relays and creates a new 3-hop Tor circuit out to the chosen rendezvous point (using the rendezvous address provided by the client in the INTRODUCE2 message). Once this circuit to the RP is established, the hidden service sends a **`RENDEZVOUS1`** cell to the rendezvous point over that circuit ([torspec/rend-spec-v3.txt at main · torproject/torspec · GitHub](https://github.com/torproject/torspec/blob/main/rend-spec-v3.txt#:~:text=36%20)). This message includes the same 20-byte rendezvous cookie (received via the intro point) and the HS’s part of the handshake (encrypted with the client’s public key from the introduction handshake). By sending RENDEZVOUS1, the HS is effectively saying “I am the hidden service that the client is trying to reach, here is the cookie to match and my handshake data.” The rendezvous point, upon seeing the cookie, recognizes it from step 4 and knows this HS corresponds to the waiting client connection.

9. **Exchanging `RENDEZVOUS1/2` and Completing Handshake:** When the HS’s RENDEZVOUS1 cell arrives at the rendezvous point, the RP links together the two circuits that share the same cookie (the client’s circuit from step 4 and the HS’s circuit from step 8). It then forwards the HS’s handshake data to the client by sending a **`RENDEZVOUS2`** cell down the client’s rendezvous circuit ([torspec/rend-spec-v3.txt at main · torproject/torspec · GitHub](https://github.com/torproject/torspec/blob/main/rend-spec-v3.txt#:~:text=37%20)). The RENDEZVOUS2 carries the hidden service’s encrypted handshake information to the client. The client’s Tor instance receives RENDEZVOUS2 and completes the cryptographic handshake (e.g. finishing the ntor key agreement using the HS’s public key in the handshake) ([Tor Project | How do Onion Services work?](https://community.torproject.org/onion-services/overview/#:~:text=The%20Onion%20Service%20connects%20to,been%20relayed%20through%20the%20service)). At this point, the rendezvous is established: the client and HS now have a shared session key, and the two Tor circuits are joined at the RP. (The rendezvous point has verified the cookie from both sides matched, and it will simply pass traffic between the client and HS from now on.)

10. **Rendezvous Circuit Established – Data Flows:** The client and hidden service now have an end-to-end encrypted channel through the rendezvous point. The Tor rendezvous handshake is complete, and a secure Tor circuit spans from the client to the HS (consisting of 3 hops from the client to RP, plus 3 hops from the HS to RP) ([Tor Project | How do Onion Services work?](https://community.torproject.org/onion-services/overview/#:~:text=Act%209%3A%20Where%20the%20Onion,Service%20rendezvous%20with%20the%20client)). The rendezvous point does not learn anything about the data or identities; it only knows the cookie used to connect the circuits. At this stage, the client can begin communicating with the hidden service (for example, sending an HTTP request if the HS is a web server). All data (the “secure data stream”) is tunneled through the rendezvous circuit – encrypted end-to-end between the client and HS and relayed by the RP. The hidden service has successfully received payment prior to communication, and the client and HS now communicate over the paid rendezvous circuit as they normally would over any Tor hidden service connection.

## Revision requirements

These four requirements retain the descriptor, invoice, introduction, and rendezvous architecture while defining its authorization and economic boundaries. They are proposed requirements; exact encodings and interoperability rules must be specified before implementation.

### 1. Bind payment authorization to the client

The service MUST verify payment against its own Lightning node's records. It must confirm the expected invoice or transaction, service association, offer where applicable, amount, terms, and settled status. If a preimage is presented, its SHA-256 hash MUST match that invoice's payment hash. A pending payment, an invoice issued by another recipient, or a matching hash/preimage pair without a settled local invoice MUST NOT authorize access.

The Lightning node is authoritative for settlement; the service's durable ledger is authoritative for access redemption within the agreed window. An invoice can remain settled after its access allowance has been used, so checking settled status alone does not prevent replay.

A hash identifies an invoice, and a preimage demonstrates payment knowledge; neither identifies the connecting client. Before payment, the service MUST bind the invoice to a fresh client authorization public key through an authenticated invoice/quote exchange. The client proves possession of the corresponding private key when requesting access. This is a separate authorization key unless a reviewed construction explicitly defines safe reuse of a Tor or Lightning key. The protocol must specify how the binding is carried and authenticated; an arbitrary payer note is insufficient.

The signed authorization MUST cover the protocol version/domain, service identity, invoice identifier, opaque circuit authorization identifier, round number, agreed terms, expiry, unique attempt identifier, rendezvous relay and cookie, and client handshake material. This prevents moving an authorization to a different service, circuit, or round. The service MUST reject expired authorizations and atomically prevent duplicate redemption. A pre-build authorization identifier must be bound to the resulting circuit once it is created; its exact encoding remains to be specified. Retry and refund rules below do not grant reusable access across circuits.

Use a fresh authorization identity for each new paid circuit rather than a reusable wallet/node identity. Keep payment authorization inside the service-encrypted introduction payload. The final compact encoding MUST fit Tor's introduction payload constraints; do not insert an entire invoice or an unbounded proof. Advertise support and version requirements so unsupported clients do not pay for an extension they cannot use.

### 2. Define who funds each circuit

Paying the service for access and paying transport relays are distinct charges. The client funds the variable costs of its connection one circuit round at a time. The service maintains its introduction circuits within the bounded free allowance below; exceeding that allowance MUST NOT silently charge the operator. Descriptor publication and retrieval use the proposed bounded free discovery allowance below. Optional paid capacity beyond that baseline requires explicit payer approval and a spending cap.

| Activity | Payer and budget |
| --- | --- |
| Service-to-introduction-point circuits | Free within advertised limits at every hop, including bounded setup and replacement work. |
| Descriptor publication, storage, and client retrieval | Proposed bounded free discovery service, covering path traffic and HSDir resource limits; any optional paid extras require explicit approval and caps. |
| Client introduction circuit | May use the same purpose-independent free allowance where supported and sufficient. |
| Client-to-rendezvous circuit | Client pays its chosen relays in one-minute rounds. |
| Service-to-rendezvous circuit | Service pays its privately selected relays using this client's funding for the corresponding circuit and round. |
| Application access, setup fees, and wallet/routing fees | Explicitly disclosed in the quote, separately from transport allowance. |

The descriptor lists introduction points, not a permanent rendezvous point. The client selects the rendezvous relay for an attempt. The service retains private selection and payment of its own path; the client MUST NOT need a list of service-side relays to fund access. Onion-service traffic has no exit to the public Internet. Relay charging details remain governed by [the paid circuit proposal](01_paid_circuits.md), with endpoint-specific rules still to be defined.

Paid circuits retain spec one's one-minute payment rounds and ten-round allocations, with explicitly bounded timing padding for Lightning routing and settlement. Extending a paid circuit past the initial allocation requires the renewal procedure below. The client pays its own side and funds the service's side one round ahead. Each funding payment MUST be bound to one circuit and round and MUST NOT authorize another circuit or round. Before payment, the authenticated Service Circuit Quote (SCQ) MUST specify the fixed round price, setup or handshake charges, acceptance expiry, padding/deadline rules, fee limits, and failure/refund policy. The service MUST reserve settled round funding before relay payments, including any costs of the agreed padding. A pending or held Lightning payment is not settled funding. This is circuit-specific payment accounting, not a reusable payment session or shared balance across circuits. Without successful renewal, expiration requires a replacement circuit with a new authorization and payment sequence. An admitted continuation stays bound to the existing circuit and does not transfer its funding elsewhere.

### 3. Specify failures, retries, and round accounting

Payment settlement and Tor rendezvous completion are separate events. A successful Lightning payment MUST NOT be represented as a successful connection. Maintain a durable circuit/round ledger recording invoice settlement, bound client key, circuit authorization identifier, round number, terms, expiry, reservations, actual charges, and consumed payment identifiers. Each attempt has a unique identifier, bounded reservation, deadline, and lifecycle: reserved, building, active, then closed or failed.

Concurrent introductions, duplicate wallet notifications, retries, and restarts MUST NOT create duplicate funding, reservations, circuits, or payments. Persist reservations and outgoing-payment identifiers before starting paid work. Duplicate requests must refer to the existing attempt rather than launch another one. After a timeout or restart, reconcile uncertain outgoing payments and circuit state before releasing a reservation or retrying a payment.

If rendezvous fails, record setup or relay costs already incurred under the agreed terms and reconcile the unused part of that attempt's reserved funding. An exact retransmission is idempotent and MUST NOT create another paid circuit or charge. A fresh attempt for an unestablished circuit requires an authenticated retry decision, fresh attempt details, and atomic rebinding of only demonstrably unspent funding; the old authorization must become unusable. Replacement of an established or expired circuit starts a new payment sequence. Do not automatically carry unused rounds to another circuit or grant unlimited free rebuilds. A failed attempt may have consumed real relay fees.

The protocol still needs an authenticated status/retry mechanism, a definition of successful connection establishment, and an explicit unused-funding/refund policy. Refunding unused funding is a separate Lightning operation requiring authenticated destination handling and its own fee policy; it does not establish a reusable payment session. Do not interpret introduction delivery acknowledgment as payment acceptance, completed rendezvous, or delivered application data.

### 4. Cap spending and keep wallet work off the handshake path

An unpaid, malformed, expired, or replayed request MUST NOT cause the service to initiate a paid rendezvous circuit, relay payment, or unlimited automatic top-up. Before any outgoing payment, enforce both the circuit round's remaining reserved budget and the operator's wallet-wide limits, including routing fees, outstanding payment commitments, and concurrent attempts. Renewal or rebuild costs must also fit these limits. Missing round funding or exhausted limits stop further paid work. When a required payment misses its bounded deadline/grace period, close the paid circuit; it MUST NOT silently fall back to the free allowance.

Standing infrastructure needs separate protection: the bounded free introduction allowance below MUST NOT trigger operator wallet spending when it is exhausted. Any separately paid descriptor publication or hosting needs explicit spending caps; unsolicited introductions or descriptor retrievals MUST NOT create unbounded charges. Apply introduction rate limits, bounded invoice issuance and queues, and bounded retention of unsettled requests. Standard Tor introduction DoS controls can help limit forwarding; they do not themselves enforce a financial budget. Hitting a cap may reduce availability but MUST NOT silently increase spending.

A background wallet component synchronizes settled invoices and reconciles outgoing payments into the durable ledger. The Tor handshake reads local authorization state and reserves circuit/round funding without waiting on wallet network RPCs. If settlement is not yet synchronized, defer or reject the attempt without spending; allow a later authenticated retry. If wallet state is stale or unavailable, do not authorize new spending until it can be reconciled. Payment, replay, and reservation state required to prevent overspending must survive process restarts.

These measures bound financial exposure; they do not eliminate CPU, bandwidth, liquidity, or availability attacks. Tor encryption and Lightning blinded paths also do not eliminate payment timing/amount correlation. The final protocol needs a privacy review as well as spending-limit validation.

## Service Circuit Quote (SCQ)

### Definition and calculation

A **Service Circuit Quote (SCQ)** is the onion service's authenticated quote to the client for funding its **service-to-rendezvous circuit**. The client separately selects and pays the relays on its own circuit to the same rendezvous point. An initial SCQ covers one service-side circuit for at most ten one-minute rounds, paid incrementally rather than all ten rounds upfront. Continuing that same circuit requires an authenticated, explicitly authorized SCQ extension under the renewal rules below. It is not a reusable payment session or a balance across circuits.

Relay operators set their own prices in their relay descriptors, as in spec one. The service reads those descriptors, selects a compatible candidate path, and totals its cost. It does not override relay rates or assume they are equal. Payment intervals and capabilities must be compatible with the quoted schedule. Candidate selection and quoting do not authorize paid construction or relay payments.

The fixed per-minute amount includes the combined selected relay rates and an explicitly disclosed service-side Lightning routing-fee budget. One-time relay handshake/setup charges are itemized separately and MUST NOT recur every round. Specify fee limits, integer units such as millisatoshis, any rounding, and how unused reserved fee amounts are treated. A separate application/access charge, if any, must be identified and accepted separately from the transport total rather than hidden in relay costs.

The client can display its own selected-path costs alongside the SCQ to show the complete connection budget. Its own wallet routing fees remain subject to its own fee limits; do not silently count them again as service-side charges. The service need not reveal its relay identities or full path. An SCQ authenticates the service's price commitment, not an independent proof of its private relay costs, path selection, or delivered bandwidth.

### Descriptor advertisement and required terms

The onion-service descriptor advertises accepted payment methods, SCQ capability, and how to obtain an authenticated quote. Generate an SCQ for the intended connection from current candidate relay prices. It need not be uploaded to HSDirs or trigger descriptor republication for every connection or round. Discovery metadata and cached descriptors are not substitutes for a current accepted quote.

The exact wire format remains open. Authenticated terms MUST include:

| Field or term | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Protocol version and unique quote identifier | Identify this agreement and avoid confusing quotes or versions. |
| Service identity and client authorization binding | Authenticate the intended service and bind acceptance to a fresh client authorization key without requiring its public wallet identity. |
| Opaque circuit authorization identifier and rendezvous relay | Bind the quote to the intended service-side circuit/destination without disclosing its private path. |
| Fixed per-round amount, unit, and breakdown | State the selected relay-rate total and disclosed service-side routing-fee budget for each 60-second round. |
| One-time charges and wallet fee limits | Itemize setup/handshake costs and bound all authorized extra spending. |
| Authorized period, maximum rounds, total cost, and outstanding commitments | Initially at most ten rounds/ten minutes; any continuation needs an accepted extension. Include setup/padding costs and bound cumulative spending and how much may be reserved, prepaid, or in flight at once. |
| Acceptance expiry and round/build schedule | Distinguish the deadline to accept from the lifetime of the accepted fixed price; specify lead time, grace, build timeout, and start conditions. |
| Payment method and invoice association | Bind each payable invoice/payment identifier to this accepted SCQ, circuit, and specific round. |
| Failure, retry, and unused-funding rules | Disclose permitted incurred costs and reconciliation/refund handling without automatic cross-circuit credit or unlimited rebuilds. |

Authenticate the SCQ as belonging to the onion service through its descriptor-authenticated identity or delegated signing key. The exact signing/delegation scheme and domain-separated serialization remain to be defined. Requests, replies, and acceptance need a bounded authenticated bootstrap exchange available before the paid service-side circuit exists. A quote receipt, a client acceptance, an issued invoice, and settlement are separate states.

### Fixed price and acceptance sequence

Once accepted within its validity window, the per-minute price is fixed for that circuit's agreed lifetime. Paying incrementally does not permit repricing each minute. Changed relay descriptors or service metadata cannot alter an accepted SCQ. The renewal draft preserves the accepted per-minute price for the continuing circuit; changing prices at renewal boundaries remains an open decision. The client may reject the quote, or stop renewing an accepted circuit under the existing performance/payment rules; acceptance does not commit the client to purchasing all ten rounds.

The proposed sequence is: request SCQ, select candidate relays and calculate cost, authenticate and deliver the quote, client accepts or declines, obtain and settle initial funding, then construct/pay the service-side circuit. The client may prepare its own rendezvous circuit independently. The service MUST NOT incur paid work until the agreed initial funding is settled and reserved. Before requesting payment, decline if no workable candidate path fits the quoted price.

Each underlying relay MUST honor the rate accepted when its circuit is established for that circuit's lifetime. This commitment requires relay negotiation and enforcement; publishing a descriptor alone does not reserve a price or capacity. If a cached rate is stale or a relay refuses the terms, use an equivalent path within the accepted budget/terms or follow the disclosed failure/requote policy. Never silently raise an accepted price or spend beyond the circuit's settled funding. A quote is not a guarantee that capacity has been reserved or that a build will succeed.

Record the accepted SCQ in the circuit/round ledger and bind every distinct subsequent invoice/payment to it. Duplicate quote requests or acceptance messages MUST NOT create extra circuits, duplicate reservations, or duplicate charges. Quote changes require fresh identifiers and explicit acceptance; old and replacement authorizations must not claim the same funds concurrently. Circuit replacement starts a new SCQ/payment sequence. Continuing the existing circuit past round ten requires an accepted SCQ extension and relay renewal; neither quote acceptance nor elapsed time automatically buys an eleventh round.

### Incremental payments, stopping, and accepted loss exposure

From the client's perspective, there are two separately funded circuit halves. The client pays its own selected relays to the rendezvous point, and separately pays the service's SCQ through Lightning. The service spends that SCQ funding on its own selected relays. These payments are incremental purchases of circuit rounds, not a single upfront purchase of the entire ten-minute connection. Initial service-side funding covers the first round and explicitly agreed setup fees.

The client may stop buying further rounds at any time, including because performance is inadequate or the connection is no longer needed. Accepting an SCQ fixes the price of any rounds purchased within its lifetime; it does not obligate the client to buy all ten. If the client purchases rounds one through three and does not fund round four, required-payment enforcement closes the affected circuit at the applicable deadline/grace. If round four was already prepaid, choosing to stop does not undo that settled payment or automatically entitle the client to its refund.

A relay or service may fail, disconnect, or cease delivering traffic at any time, even after payment. The protocol cannot force an uncooperative peer to remain online or deliver bandwidth, and a receipt/accepted SCQ is not an uptime or delivery guarantee. Nonpayment closure follows the agreed deadline/grace; unrelated failure or disconnection can happen earlier. The client/service can stop further renewal and reconcile outstanding work when either circuit half fails. This availability risk does not waive the price, authorization, spending-limit, or agreed failure-accounting rules.

**Accepted tradeoff:** incremental payment bounds prepayment exposure but does not make it zero or guarantee a refund. The client may lose amounts already paid for service that is not subsequently delivered. With the candidate 60-second lead time, the current round can still have unused time while the next round is prepaid or in flight. Exposure can therefore include the unused portion of the current round plus the prepaid next round, agreed setup charges, and routing fees. It is not strictly limited to one minute's fees.

The client-side relay payments and SCQ payments each contribute to the client's total budget and exposure. Service-to-relay payments funded by the SCQ must not be counted again as a second client charge. Enforce the disclosed outstanding-funding cap across both current/next-round commitments and unreconciled attempts; retries, overlapping builds, or restarts MUST NOT multiply that cap. Do not release uncertain in-flight funds merely because the client closed the connection. The existing reconciliation/refund policy governs confirmed remaining funds and actual agreed costs; it does not promise recovery from an uncooperative recipient.

### Security and exposure limits

Protect quote, recipient, and invoice binding together: a valid service quote must not be usable with a substituted invoice, amount, destination, circuit, or round. Invoice/payment-method verification must authenticate the intended receiver without imposing a universally public wallet identity. Reject expired or incompatible quotes before payment, and reconcile payments that nevertheless settle late under the existing failure rules. Exact signing and binding formats need validation before implementation.

Rate-limit quote requests, candidate selection, invoice issuance, and outstanding quote state using bounded per-request and aggregate budgets. An unpaid requester must not trigger paid circuits or unbounded memory/CPU work. Many fresh client keys must not evade the service's global limits. Client-supplied rendezvous details still require normal Tor eligibility/path checks and authenticated binding to the accepted quote.

A client receiving a quote for a private path cannot independently verify its component relay prices without additional disclosure or a separate reviewed proof mechanism. Treat the total as a service-authenticated price offer which the client may reject. Do not claim that the quote proves honest pricing, relay performance, successful delivery, or automatic fair exchange.

Keep uncertain in-flight payments reserved until reconciled, including after circuit closure or a request to re-quote. Refund/unused-funding claims require client authorization bound to the original quote, confirmed remaining funds, and bounded refund fees; a retry or refund request must not redirect someone else's funds. Detailed refund mechanics remain open, and the SCQ does not promise an unconditional refund of already incurred agreed costs.

### Shared construction and validation

Carry SCQ negotiation and compact references in the authenticated client/service exchange. Preserve spec one's unchanged per-hop construction, unique per-hop round identifiers, dummy zero-fee handshake fields, encryption, and equal-size padding. Do not add an onion-service purpose marker to intermediate relays' payment payloads. A full SCQ need not fit in one introduction cell; define bounded delivery and appropriately sized authenticated references before choosing an encoding.

Validate unequal relay prices, fee caps/rounding, price changes, stale descriptors, quote rejection/expiry, substituted invoices, destination/client binding, duplicate acceptances, replay, bounded quote floods, and loss of connectivity during the exchange. Check that existing circuits retain accepted rates, failures cannot overspend settled circuit funds, all simultaneous commitments stay within the cap, and clients can approve a complete budget without learning the service's path. Include stopping after round three, stopping after the next round was already prepaid, peer disconnection immediately after payment, and reconciling both circuit halves without double-counting the SCQ's onward relay payments.

SCQ encoding/authentication, bootstrap delivery, relay rate commitment, exact build/start events, and unused-fee/refund mechanics remain implementation-level design work. This section records the agreed architecture and its open security checks, not a completed implementation or executed test suite.

## Payment timing and circuit rounds

### Draft schedule

Keep one-minute payment rounds and an initial ten-round paid-circuit allocation. Successful negotiated renewal can extend the same circuit; otherwise the existing expiration remains in force. The initial timing candidate is **60 seconds of advance payment lead time**, separate from late-payment grace. While the current minute runs, the client starts funding the next minute. The service waits for incoming settlement, reserves that round's funds, and then pays its own relays. Client-side relay payments can proceed independently; payments to distinct service-side relays need not be serialized after the service has received sufficient funding.

| Parameter | Draft starting point | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Round duration | 60 seconds | Paid service interval; unchanged by paying early. |
| Payment lead time | 60 seconds | Start the client-to-service funding step before the next round is needed, leaving time for onward relay payments and wallet observation. |
| Late-payment grace | Separate bounded value; 15 seconds is the current relay implementation's baseline | Determines the final nonpayment closure deadline. It is not another minute of advance notice and must be explicit in agreed terms. |
| Rounds per allocation | 10 | No automatic eleventh round: a continuation needs accepted renewal terms, all-hop admission, and round funding. No automatic transfer to a replacement circuit. |

These are draft timing candidates, not measured guarantees. Small payment amounts do not guarantee fast routing or settlement. The additional lead time addresses the two successive payment stages, client-to-service and service-to-relays, rather than assuming that Tor's data-path hop count determines Lightning payment duration. The 60-second budget must cover both stages, wallet observation, and any permitted retries; it is not a separate 60-second promise for each stage.

### Initial round and setup

There is no preceding minute in which to prepay round one. Before the service incurs any paid setup or relay charges, the client MUST settle funding for the initial round and all explicitly agreed setup/handshake costs. The service MUST reserve that funding before spending it. A pending payment or the client's claim that it has sent funds is insufficient.

The quote and circuit establishment exchange must distinguish the bounded build/setup phase from the paid round schedule. They must define the start event and build timeout, what setup charges may be incurred before rendezvous succeeds, and what happens if either circuit half fails to become usable. First-round funding does not promise a successful connection. Exact start-event signaling remains wire-protocol work; it MUST NOT depend on an unpaid request being allowed to spend the operator's funds.

### Round identifiers, acceptance windows, and deadlines

Every payment and its authorization MUST identify the intended circuit, recipient, round, and agreed amount. Define a valid early-payment acceptance window, the round start/end, and final grace deadline. The service's incoming funding deadline must leave time before its outgoing relay-payment deadlines. An advertised schedule must be achievable across all selected hops; paying the service alone does not prove that any relay has been paid.

The protocol MUST accept legitimate, correctly bound prepayment for an upcoming round instead of rejecting it merely because the preceding round is still active. Early settlement does not start the next round early, extend circuit lifetime, authorize another round, or permit replay. Duplicate observations must refer to the same payment record and reservation.

Use authenticated circuit-relative schedules and bounded clock/observation tolerances rather than requiring perfectly synchronized clocks across all relays. The final encoding must specify how each relay's local schedule maps to its payment identifiers and how the client/service learns the relevant deadlines. A relay need not learn the identities of the service's other relays. Recipient-node settlement records are authoritative for payment status and settlement time where available; callback arrival time alone is not the settlement time. Wallet adapters must expose the information needed to enforce the agreed acceptance policy.

### Round payments and failure behavior

During round N, begin funding round N+1 with the advertised lead time. The service MUST wait for settled incoming funding and reserve enough for the corresponding outgoing payments, including agreed fees and padding costs, before sending them. The schedule does not permit the service to borrow from unrelated circuit rounds or subsidize a delayed client payment. The relay's explicitly agreed grace bounds its own exposure; it does not authorize unlimited spending by the service.

Keep incoming funding, outgoing payment state, and circuit state distinct. Incoming settlement, outgoing payment initiation, outgoing settlement, and a usable circuit are different events. A required payment missing its final deadline closes the affected paid circuit; any remaining work and the other circuit half must be stopped or reconciled without silently extending fees, lifetime, or access. Do not grant a new full grace period merely because a poll, retry, or process restart occurred.

A wallet timeout or RPC cancellation does not prove that a payment failed: an in-flight payment may still settle. Persist its identity and reconcile its terminal state before issuing a replacement payment, releasing its reserved funds, or considering a refund. A late-settling payment MUST NOT reopen a closed circuit or automatically authorize a different circuit or round. Record actual agreed costs and resolve unused settled funds under the disclosed failure/refund policy. Authenticated refund details, fees, and eligibility remain open design work; no unconditional refund or delivery guarantee is implied.

### Allocation boundary and validation

Without successful renewal admission, prepayment stops at round ten: round nine may fund round ten, but round ten MUST NOT fund an unauthorized round eleven. With accepted renewal terms and the required acknowledgments below, advance funding for round eleven follows the existing schedule and spending caps. Circuit replacement begins a separate authorization/payment sequence; circuit-specific funding MUST NOT silently migrate to it. Reconcile pending payments and unused reservations on closure, including when the client disconnects before a prepaid round is used.

Before finalizing the lead time or grace value, measure the complete client-to-service-to-relays sequence, including invoice acquisition, settlement, wallet polling/event latency, concurrent onward payments, and retries. Test initial construction, valid early payment, boundary timings, late wallet observations, late settlement after closure, duplicate callbacks, uncertain outgoing payments, restarts, and the absence of unauthorized round-eleven charges. Add the renewal boundary tests below. Local mocked-wallet timing tests do not establish live Lightning routing latency. This section records the agreed draft direction; implementation and timing measurements remain future work.

## Renewing both rendezvous circuit halves

This proposal inherits [spec one's circuit renewal draft](01_paid_circuits.md#circuit-renewal-draft). Renewal is a shared transport feature, not an onion-service-specific way to move streams. Preserve the two existing circuits joined at the same rendezvous point, their Tor stream/cryptographic state, and the client/service authorization binding. Neither the client nor the service can keep a connection alive if the other half disappears. Renewal does not require a new introduction or descriptor publication solely because another payment period begins.

### SCQ continuation and funding

Before the existing authorization runs out, the service offers an authenticated extension referencing the accepted SCQ, circuit authorization identifier, fresh extension identifier, next period and round range, unchanged per-minute price, and the agreed activation boundary. The extension must retain the client-key/recipient binding and disclose cumulative cost, outstanding-funding limits, deadlines, and failure accounting. It authorizes further individual round purchases on the same circuit, not a reusable session or cross-circuit balance. No ten-round lump payment or repeated build/testing handshake fee is introduced. Whether to permit explicitly accepted price changes in future extensions remains open; this draft preserves the continuing circuit's fixed price.

The client may decline or approve continuation within an explicit renewal/spending policy. Approval of the original SCQ alone does not authorize unlimited extensions. Preparing an extension and registering fresh relay identifiers must fit bounded control-plane resources and cannot incur unfunded paid work for the service. Each hop may refuse future admission; existing paid service still follows its accepted schedule.

### Coordinated preparation and activation

1. Use spec one's draft trigger at the start of round seven in each ten-round allocation (global rounds 7, 17, 27, and so on). When continuation is wanted and authorized, the client prepares renewal with every hop on its existing path. In parallel, the service prepares renewal with every hop on its private existing path, under the proposed SCQ extension. Use independent per-hop identifiers and acknowledgments. Each half follows its agreed relative schedule; specify how preparation is coordinated when their boundaries differ.
2. The client authenticates the service's extension/readiness response, bound to the existing connection and upcoming round range. The service need not reveal its private relay identities or acknowledgments. Its readiness statement is a service commitment, not independently verifiable proof of its path, payment, or future delivery.
3. Before initiating funding for the new period, require accepted extension terms, admission on the client's own path, and the service's authenticated readiness statement. Preparation beginning at round seven must finish before the existing 60-second payment lead is needed. It does not move round-eleven payment into round seven: keep funding incremental on the agreed schedule and within the same outstanding-payment cap. Exact completion deadlines must be specified and measured; negotiation is not assumed to be instantaneous or covered by another free grace period.
4. Continue the existing two-stage funding schedule: the client pays its own relays and settles the service's next-round funding; the service reserves that settlement before paying its own relays. An admission/readiness acknowledgment does not authorize borrowing from the operator's wallet or another client's funds. Pay only the next authorized round within existing caps.
5. On success, both halves continue through their agreed schedules without deliberately closing active streams at round ten. If either side cannot renew or fund its next period, stop further renewal payments, report failure through the authenticated control exchange where possible, and expire under the existing deadlines. Preserve uncertain payment records and reconcile unused funding. Do not cut short already purchased service merely because a future extension was refused.

This is coordinated preparation, not atomic settlement across the client, service, and relays. A half can fail after readiness or after some payments settle. Accepted loss exposure still includes unused current-round service, the prepaid next round, and disclosed fees across both halves; the SCQ's onward relay payments are not another client charge. No automatic refund or live-stream migration is promised. A separately authorized replacement can serve new connections; resuming an interrupted download still depends on the application and remote server.

### Remaining decisions and tests

Specify the authenticated continuation/readiness/status messages, the relay and end-to-end round mappings, recovery from partial acknowledgments or process restarts, and a bounded cumulative circuit age/renewal policy. SCQ extensions cannot override a relay's age or admission limits. Free introduction-circuit renewal remains undecided; retain its existing bounded-free replacement policy, and do not indefinitely renew an idle descriptor-upload circuit under this paid extension.

Test continuous transfers across multiple allocation boundaries without changing either rendezvous circuit or stream. Test each side declining, one hop refusing, stale/duplicate extension acceptance, unequal boundary times, partial settlement, missing service readiness, payment delay, budget exhaustion, and a relay actually dropping. Verify no unfunded service spending, no unauthorized eleventh round, no duplicate setup fee or quota reset, bounded pending state, and correct closure/reconciliation. This section defines draft acceptance criteria; no renewal implementation or executed end-to-end test is claimed.

## Payment methods and receiver privacy

### Receiver choice and negotiation

Receiver-hiding payment paths are RECOMMENDED, not mandatory. BOLT 12 offers with receiver-hiding blinded paths are the preferred option when both wallets support them. BOLT 11 invoices, including blinded-path invoices where supported by the relevant implementations, are also permitted. An operator may explicitly choose a method that exposes its Lightning node identity or other receiver information.

The receiver advertises its accepted methods and their relevant capabilities in authenticated service metadata. The client selects a mutually supported method according to its wallet capabilities and configured privacy preferences before paying. A payment method MUST NOT be inferred to provide receiver privacy merely from its BOLT number; the actual fields, path configuration, and wallet behavior determine what is exposed. A public relay and a location-hidden onion-service operator may make different choices.

This is an extensible method policy, not a claim that every wallet supports every method. Additional methods require an explicit integration, an authenticated payment-request mechanism, and reliable recipient-side settlement verification. Unsupported methods MUST be rejected before funds are sent. Any fallback must respect the receiver's advertised choices and the client's configured policy; the protocol does not impose a universal requirement to hide receiver identity.

### Shared verification requirements

Regardless of method or privacy choice, the receiver MUST verify the intended recipient, agreed amount, settled status, circuit/round binding, and single redemption. A matching preimage/hash pair alone is insufficient without the receiver's own settled-payment record. Privacy preferences do not relax payment authorization, spending limits, or replay protection.

Each circuit round needs a distinct payment identifier and authorization; duplicate delivery or retry must follow the existing idempotency rules rather than reuse an already consumed payment. A BOLT 12 offer may be reused to request fresh invoices, but an already paid invoice MUST NOT fund another round. Methods based on BOLT 11 or an external invoice-acquisition service must define how fresh invoices are obtained and authenticated before the paid rendezvous exists. That bootstrap exchange and its bounded resource use remain protocol design work.

### Privacy properties and implementation scope

When the receiver chooses BOLT 12 blinded paths, account for both invoice-request delivery and payment delivery, and inspect identity fields in the offer and invoice for unintended disclosure. Blinding does not hide the receiver from all of its direct Lightning counterparties or remove timing/amount correlation. Network-address privacy also depends on wallet transport and deployment choices. The operator decides which of these properties it requires.

LND documents blinded invoice creation through its `is_blinded` option and an ephemeral signing key. The local LNI LND adapter exposes this option; that source support is not an end-to-end interoperability test. Wallet-specific support must be checked and tested before advertising a method as usable. No new wallet implementation is claimed by this specification update.

## Descriptor publication, retrieval, and free discovery

### Ordinary onion-service publication flow

Descriptor publication is separate from maintaining an introduction point. For a normal anonymous onion service:

1. The service's Tor software establishes introduction points, creates the descriptor describing how to contact them, and signs and encrypts it using the v3 onion-service descriptor construction.
2. The service derives the responsible HSDir relays from the current consensus, time-period/blinded-key rules, and shared randomness. These are selected directory relays, not the directory authorities receiving an ordinary relay descriptor.
3. It builds anonymized, normally three-hop upload circuits ending at those HSDirs and uploads descriptor copies. It MUST NOT replace this with a direct connection that exposes the service's origin address.
4. In ordinary Tor, an upload circuit need not remain open merely to keep the descriptor stored. The proposed El Tor lifecycle below normally retains it until its ten-minute expiration. The service continues maintaining separate introduction circuits for forwarding client introductions.
5. A client knowing the onion address derives the appropriate lookup information, fetches a descriptor through Tor, and verifies and decrypts it. Optional client authorization can restrict who can decrypt the connection information.

Descriptors are replicated and refreshed, including when introduction information changes and across descriptor time-period transitions. They are not a single permanent upload or a cleartext network-wide directory listing. HSDirs need not learn the onion address or decrypt introduction details merely to store and serve a valid descriptor. Ordinary clients knowing a non-restricted onion address can retrieve its descriptor.

### El Tor upload-circuit lifetime

After completing publication, normally retain the descriptor-upload circuit until its ten-minute expiration rather than closing it immediately because the upload finished. This follows the shared lifetime policy in spec one, subject to user choice, failures, and free-resource admission/enforcement limits. Remaining idle after a small transfer and closing early when a circuit performs poorly are also normal client behaviors; circuit lifetime or idleness alone is not an onion-service identifier.

Retaining an upload circuit is a lifecycle choice, not a requirement for HSDir storage. Its expiration MUST NOT itself cause an unnecessary new upload or immediate circuit replacement. Publish again when a descriptor update or refresh is needed. Preserve the shared padding/maintenance policy within the free resource budget and count retained idle circuits against the same aggregate limits. Introduction circuits, which must remain available to receive invitations, have their separate replacement requirements below.

### Proposed pricing: bounded free discovery

Basic descriptor publication, storage, refresh, and retrieval SHOULD be available without Lightning payments within explicitly advertised resource limits. This allows a service to become discoverable before its first paying client and allows clients to learn contact and payment information before purchasing rendezvous traffic. It is a proposed policy, not an already implemented free service or an unlimited availability guarantee.

The transport part uses the same purpose-independent bounded free allowance at every hop, including the guard, middle, and HSDir endpoint. Intermediate relays enforce resource limits without needing to inspect descriptor contents or recognize a directory operation. The HSDir separately enforces limits on its storage and request-processing resources. Making endpoint storage free alone would not remove transport charges on the earlier hops.

The allowance must fit legitimate complete descriptor uploads/downloads, acknowledgments, copies, refreshes, and permitted replacement overlap. A descriptor is larger than a single introduction message; a tiny sustained rate may require a bounded burst budget to finish publication in a useful time. Measure this workload before selecting byte rates or burst sizes. Include transport/padding overhead and any compression/decompression limits in the resource accounting. Descriptor size, lifetime, and periodic refresh are distinct from circuit payment periods and the baseline ten-minute expiration of non-renewed circuits.

Free capacity does not silently waive the normal price of an already paid circuit, turn paid application traffic into free traffic, or authorize an automatic wallet charge if a limit is reached. Eligible discovery setup must fit the bounded free setup policy. Additional paid capacity requires explicit operator/client consent and a spending limit; overload should produce bounded retries or temporary unavailability rather than an unexpected charge.

HSDir endpoints cannot be chosen arbitrarily merely because another relay offers free storage: clients must derive the same responsible locations as publishers. Capability signaling and HSDir eligibility/selection rules must be consistent across the network. If a responsible endpoint lacks this feature or is overloaded, use the permitted replicas and bounded retry behavior. Any alternative eligibility rule requires an explicitly specified interoperable protocol change; do not silently change the hash ring on one side.

### Spam and resource-exhaustion controls

Low bandwidth per honest service does not make unrestricted publication safe. An origin can create many service keys, issue repeated descriptor updates, submit malformed documents, or trigger repeated lookups. These consume storage, signature-verification CPU, circuit setup, queues, and response bandwidth. A valid signature proves control of a key, not scarcity of identities or absence of spam.

Supporting HSDirs and path relays MUST implement the following five resource bounds. The policy is recorded here; new numeric defaults remain provisional until legitimate workloads and overload behavior are measured.

| Bound | Initial policy | Scope and rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Descriptor size | Retain Tor's active consensus size limit, currently `HSV3MaxDescriptorSize` with a default of 50,000 bytes. Do not invent a smaller El Tor format. | Bound complete documents and request framing; reject oversized/malformed inputs without unbounded allocation. Preserve descriptor encryption and padding rules. |
| Upload/update rate | Allow a bounded burst, then a configurable sustained update rate per descriptor key, with explicit retry/backoff behavior. | Accommodate introduction replacement, legitimate revisions, replicas, overlapping periods, and recovery. Repeated uploads or new keys must not bypass global work limits. |
| Total storage | Use a configurable HSDir cache/memory budget, expire entries, and retain the appropriate latest revision rather than unlimited revision history. | Validate descriptor certificates/signatures and revision rules. Do not overwrite a newer accepted revision with an older one. Establish bounded overload/eviction behavior. |
| Free traffic | Bound sustained and burst traffic per circuit and across the relay, in both directions, including responses, padding, and accounting overhead. | A burst must fit a complete valid descriptor transfer. Cap repeated lookups/misses and combined traffic across circuits; the free allowance is not multiplied without limit by opening more circuits. |
| Setup and processing | Bound circuit creation, concurrent uploads/downloads, verification work, queued requests, pending builds, and buffering. | Tiny transfers still consume CPU and memory. Enforce relay-wide ceilings across connections and newly generated identities. |

Relay-local resource budgets should be configurable, with interoperable advertised free-capacity limits and predictable rejection/retry behavior. Retaining a Tor consensus size limit does not mean that every other quota becomes a consensus parameter. Clients and services must have enough information to select a usable path without exposing their onion identity to intermediate relays. Test defaults against actual publication and retrieval workloads, especially our proposed ten-minute introduction-circuit replacements, before selecting update rates, free byte rates, burst sizes, or concurrency/cache budgets.

Keep the outer descriptor verifiable without requiring HSDirs to decrypt its connection information, learn the long-term onion identity, or link successive blinded identities solely to enforce quotas. Per-key and per-connection throttles supplement global limits; they cannot prevent one origin from creating many identities. Fair admission under a flood remains a design and test topic. Limits bound resource exposure but do not promise that spam cannot make a service temporarily unreachable.

### Validation and open decisions

Measure publication size, replica count, update rate, and retrieval traffic for legitimate services, especially with ten-minute introduction-circuit replacement and time-period overlap. Test that an onion service can publish, refresh, and be found through an entirely eligible free path without paying continuous relay fees or exposing its origin. Confirm that clients and publishers agree on responsible HSDirs and reject stale/invalid descriptors.

Then test many-key upload floods, frequent revisions, malformed/oversized inputs, repeated lookups/misses, and combined traffic across circuits and connections. Verify bounded storage, CPU, queues, setup work, and bandwidth, as well as recovery for legitimate users after overload. Numeric quotas, fair admission, capability encoding, and any extra anti-spam mechanism remain undecided until these tests inform them. This section records a bounded-free proposal, not completed spam-resistance validation.

## Bounded free allowance for introduction availability

### Purpose and scope

The service needs open circuits to its introduction points so those relays can forward client introductions. These circuits are shared availability infrastructure across many visitors, not a new service-side circuit built for every client. Charging ordinary paid-circuit rates continuously could make staying reachable prohibitively expensive.

This proposal uses spec one's shared, purpose-independent free traffic allowance at **every hop** of an eligible ordinary El Tor circuit, including its guard, middle, and introduction endpoint. Waiving only the introduction endpoint's fee would leave the other hops charging the operator. A service MUST choose a path whose relays support the allowance and currently admit free circuits. Availability remains subject to advertised resource limits; free capacity is not guaranteed under overload.

The allowance is based on enforceable resource use, not a claim that a circuit is for an onion service. Earlier relays MUST NOT need to learn the onion-service identity, full path, or introduction role to apply it. Any origin may use this limited capacity, including for other low-volume communication. Such use is an intentional subsidy; the design does not claim that a tiny allowance is useless to a freeloader.

### Relay policy and enforcement

A relay's normal advertised paid rate remains under the operator's control. Supporting relays MUST also advertise the free allowance and admission limits explicitly; clients MUST NOT infer support from a zero handshake fee or from the presence of an introduction endpoint. The allowance is a proposed protocol feature, not a behavior already guaranteed by the implementation.

Free capacity MUST have enforceable limits for:

- Sustained traffic per circuit, with a bounded burst allowance sufficient for legitimate introduction messages and maintenance.
- Traffic in both directions, including padding, with clearly defined cell/byte accounting and wire overhead treatment.
- Circuit creation rate, handshake computation, pending circuit builds, and concurrent established circuits.
- Total free bandwidth, circuit state/memory, and setup work across the relay. Per-circuit limits alone are insufficient because an origin can open many circuits and combine them.
- Queued data and queued work. Excess traffic must be bounded, throttled, or rejected without allowing unlimited buffering or silently increasing fees.

Enforcement must account for rebuilds, overlapping replacements, and attempts spread across multiple connections or claimed identities. Anonymous origins can create new identities, so identity-based limits alone are insufficient; the relay-wide resource ceiling remains mandatory. Exact limits, overload/fairness behavior, capability signaling, and accounting formats remain open until measured under local tests.

At the introduction endpoint, enforce the introduction protocol's permitted behavior and rate-limit forwarded requests. Do not permit an established introduction circuit to become an unrestricted application-data path. These endpoint checks complement resource limits at earlier hops; they do not prove the circuit's purpose to the guard or middle relay. Payload semantics at earlier hops remain encrypted.

### Circuit lifetime and payment behavior

Free introduction circuits follow the proposed ten-minute maximum circuit lifetime. The service establishes replacements before retiring old circuits and updates its descriptor when introduction points or their published keys change. Replacement overlap, setup, acknowledgments, and normal maintenance must fit the bounded free allowance. The descriptor upload circuit is separate and need not stay open just to keep the descriptor published. Descriptor distribution uses the proposed bounded free discovery policy above; exact capacity limits remain design work.

Eligible free setup and maintenance MUST NOT require the service to pay a normal paid-circuit handshake fee merely to remain reachable. The existing paid-circuit handshake-fee logic remains: relay operators set that fee, and new relays may advertise zero sats. This proposal does not introduce a new paid-activation fee or use the free allowance as a promise of full-bandwidth testing.

Rendezvous/application-data circuits continue to use one-minute payment rounds with bounded settlement padding and ten-round allocations. Continuing past an allocation requires negotiated paid renewal of the existing circuit; free-only circuit renewal is still undecided. Failure to make a required payment closes the paid circuit. A closed or unpaid paid circuit MUST NOT continue by switching into free mode. A separately admitted free circuit is still subject to the same small allowance and all aggregate limits.

The service MUST NOT automatically spend wallet funds when free capacity is exhausted. It may retry within bounded setup limits, select another supporting path, or temporarily lose introduction availability. Any choice to purchase additional capacity requires an explicit operator budget; unsolicited traffic cannot authorize that purchase.

### Required design validation

Before choosing numeric limits or claiming this is affordable, measure introduction message sizes, bursts, maintenance/padding, circuit setup, and descriptor-update work under the ten-minute rotation policy. Validate that a legitimate service remains reachable through repeated rotations without continuous relay payments. Separately test combined free circuits, rebuild storms, queue exhaustion, and attempts to carry bulk traffic. Verify that each hop enforces its local limits, aggregate resource use stays bounded, and exhausted free capacity cannot drain either wallet. This is future validation work, not a report of completed tests.

## Planning resume point

The receiver-choice privacy policy, payment-timing draft, bounded-free descriptor discovery proposal, and Service Circuit Quote (SCQ) architecture are recorded. An SCQ totals selected relay descriptor rates plus disclosed fees, is accepted before service-side paid construction, stays fixed for that circuit, and is paid in one-minute rounds. Quote/bootstrap encoding, relay rate commitments, exposure accounting, and failure/refund details remain open as listed above. A shared paid-circuit renewal draft is now recorded in spec one, with two-half coordination in the renewal section above. Its wire format, cumulative age limit, free-only renewal policy, and price-change policy remain open. Client compatibility/version negotiation follows this renewal discussion.

A fingerprinting review is also open for combinations of observable traffic behavior, introduction-circuit replacement, descriptor update timing/size, and identifier reuse. The shared spec-one construction already requires equal-size per-hop encrypted payment fields and dummy zero-fee handshake values; their mere presence is not an added onion-service marker. Normal client comparisons must include idle ten-minute circuits and early closure for poor performance. No traffic-correlation finding has been demonstrated by this planning discussion. Standard Tor introduction-point lifetimes are randomized over a default 18–24-hour range; the draft's ten-minute circuit replacement is a design difference, not a demonstrated deanonymization finding. Preserve the distinction between introduction-point lifetime and the lifetime of a circuit connecting to it when reviewing alternatives. The paid renewal proposal changes the former hard cutoff into renewable payment periods when negotiated; its effect on traffic patterns and long-lived-path exposure requires review. The free-only replacement and receiver-choice privacy policies remain unchanged.

Remaining discussion topics are the open renewal decisions above and client compatibility/version negotiation. The separate paid-circuit replay-protection investigation remains deferred; these specification edits do not implement its fixes.
